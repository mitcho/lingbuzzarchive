On U-syncretism and Passives: A Response to Key 2024
Chigchi Bai
March 2026
 

While Key (2024) is right in claiming that u-syncretism is a surface phenomenon that can “mask” the structural difference between canonical and noncanonical passive derivations, he incorrectly analyzes u-syncretic passive morphology as a manifestation of a nonactive Voice head. This paper proposes an alternative approach based on the following specific points. First, agents are suppressed in noncanonical (low) passives but not in canonical passives; an internal argument is promoted to the low Voice layer in noncanonical passives and a higher Voice layer in canonical passives. Second, u-syncretism arises from internal argument promotion to the low Voice layer, rather than being a manifestation of a nonactive Voice head. Third, as a syntactic head, Voice is not specified for an active-nonactive alternation; instead, it consistently introduces a syntactic argument through either external or internal merge; instead of an alternating Voice head (active/nonactive), Free Merge provides the basis for both passive variation and u-syncretism.

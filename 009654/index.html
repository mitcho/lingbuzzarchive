On U-syncretism and Passives: A Response to Key 2024
Chigchi Bai
January 2026
 

Key (2024) claims that u-syncretism is a manifestation of morphosyntactic ambiguity, which is attributed to a nonactive Voice head. So-called nonactive Voice does not have a specifier and therefore does not project an external argument. Key (2024) also claims that canonical passives, which are formed from a transitive-marked stem, have a Voice-stacking structure, where the upper Voice is nonactive, thereby deriving the passive, and the lower Voice is active and endowed with a D feature, which is spelled out with transitive marking. This paper shows that Key’s (2024) treatment of u-syncretism as a manifestation of morphosyntactic ambiguity in Turkish is correct, his attribution of it and passive derivation to the purported nonactive Voice is incorrect. It will be argued that it is the promotion or the referential linking of an internal argument to Spec of a Voice head rather than the active-nonactive Voice variation that gives rise to voice-syncretism subsuming u-syncretism. It is demonstrated that external arguments are suppressed in noncanonical (low) passives but not in canonical passives and that there is no active-nonactive variation of Voice as a syntactic head. Syncretic morphology, a passive suffix or a causative one, spells out Voice at varying heights, where Voice always introduces an argument, either external or internal, and there does not exist nonactive Voice in the syntax. Consequently, building voice constructions is reducible to introducing arguments through Voice heads; it is Free Merge of DPs at various heights rather than a feature on Voice that leads to voice variation. Voice syntax is thus simplified, as demanded by Minimalism.

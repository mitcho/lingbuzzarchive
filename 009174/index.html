Context Shift and Viewpoint Shift: Super Monsters Revisited
Philippe Schlenker, Jason Lamberton, Jonathan Lamberton
January 2026
 

While Kaplan's classic theory of indexicals took them to be rigidly anchored to the context of the actual speech act, more recent research has found multiple cases in which an indexical depends on the context of a reported speech act, suggesting that the latter can sometimes shift the context (in Kaplan's terms, attitude reports can be 'monsters'). It was further proposed that context shift is sometimes overtly realized in sign language by Role Shift, an operation in which the signer visibly adopts someone else's perspective. However, in ASL and LSF, Role Shift involves peculiarities that have motivated the development of non-standard context-shifting operators ('Super Monsters'), and also of alternative treatments (in terms of demonstrations). First, Role Shift arises not just in attitude reports but also in action reports, where no monsters have been found in spoken language. Second, Role Shift forces elements within its scope to be interpreted in a maximally iconic fashion ('Maximize Iconicity'), something that has not been found in spoken language monsters. We argue that a key observation, familiar from informal descriptions, has been missed by formal analyses: Role Shift usually shifts the viewpoint ('camera position') relative to which iconic elements are evaluated. Building on the formal framework of Iconological Semantics, which has an explicit pictorial component, we develop a minimal analysis in which Role Shift only involves overt viewpoint shift. We find that it arguably leaves some facts unaccounted for, and consider an alternative in which Role Shift involves both context shift and viewpoint shift, hence a new take on Super Monsters. While our main new data are from ASL, we include gestural examples that suggest that the analysis could be extended to speech with gestures.

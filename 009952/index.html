The Bare Hand: Pre-PIE *de- as a free morpheme, the compositional decomposition of *-tero-, and the bimorphemic structure of *deh₃-
J. M. Chavis, A.I. Claude
May 2026
 

The first two papers of this project argued that pre-PIE *de- "hand, open palm" underlies the lexical cluster Greek δῶρον / Hesychian δάρις / Celtic *durno- / Latvian dūre (Paper 1) and the contrastive suffix *-tero- together with the numeral *du̯o-/du̯i- "two" as derivational siblings on the same root (Paper 2). The present paper completes the framework's verbal and particle branches by going one step deeper: *der- itself decomposes into two independently attested morphemes — a bare hand-element *de- and a locative/spatial extension *-r- — and the same bare *de- underlies the give-verb *deh₃- via a verbal extension *-h₃-. The bare *de- is independently attested as the directional particle *de/do "to, toward" across the IE family: Greek allative -δε productive in Mycenaean Greek by 1400–1200 BCE, Old Church Slavonic do, English to, and Celtiberian DV in the Bronze of Novallas (Beltrán Lloris et al. 2021; Prósper 2022). Three independent published treatments converge on the locatival status of *-r-: Lipp (2009) on athematic heteroclite locatives, Bauhaus (2019) on *-r as a true PIE locative case ending, and Kahl (2024) on an archaic locatival morpheme *-er underlying Indo-Iranian and Greek degree morphology. The paper presents direct discriminating evidence for the bimorphemic decomposition of *deh₃- in the Greek root-aorist 2pl imperative paradigm. Roots the framework analyzes as bimorphemic (Class A: *deh₃- → δότε, *dheh₁- → θέτε, *yeh₁- → ἕτε) preserve a bare-CV form in 2pl; roots the framework does not analyze as bimorphemic (Class B: *steh₂- → στῆτε, *ǵneh₃- → γνῶτε, *gu̯eh₂- → βῆτε) preserve the laryngeal-conditioned long vowel. The asymmetry is descriptively well-established in the standard Greek-grammar tradition; the standard view describes it as paradigmatic accident; the framework predicts it from the bimorphemic decomposition. The conditional probability is assessed at 35–45%.

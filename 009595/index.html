The Internal Structure of R-Loci: Index–Reference Alignment in Sign Languages
Tariq Nazar
December 2025
 

This paper proposes a structural reanalysis of R-loci in sign languages as syntactic objects with internal structure that underlie verbal argument positions. I argue that every R-locus consists of two distinct components—an index slot and a reference slot—and that argument licensing follows from the systematic specification of these components rather than from agreement, pronominal elements, or post-syntactic discourse inference. On this basis, I introduce 'Locus-Checking' as a uniform syntactic operation that aligns partially specified R-loci in order to license verbal arguments. The proposal accounts for apparent agreement effects, null arguments, generic interpretations, and first-person readings with body-anchored verbs. The analysis is primarily based on data from German Sign Language (DGS), but the structural assumptions are intended to apply more broadly to the grammatical organization of reference in sign languages.

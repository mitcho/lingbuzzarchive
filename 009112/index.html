A New Analysis of the Morphological Structure of Japanese Verbal Adjectives
Shinya Makino
July 2025
 

The present paper reanalyses the morphological structure of Japanese verbal adjectives (VAs) — simply adjectives, or i-adjectives. Traditionally, inflected forms of verbal adjectives, such as takaku — so-called ren'yōkei — from the VA takai "to be high/tall", have been analysed as consisting of the so-called stem taka- and the so-called ending -ku. However, this analysis appears to have limitations. Considering the morphological, syntactic, morpho-syntactic and semantic parallelism with verb stems — such as tabe from the verb taberu "to eat" — it can be inferred that takaku as a whole corresponds to the stem of the verbal adjective. This paper thus analyses taka- as one of the two immediate constituents of the VA stem takaku, which denotes, as a VA radical, the core lexical meaning of the verbal adjective and -ku as the other constituent, functioning as the VA thematic suffix — namely, VA stem-forming suffix — that licences the VA radical as a syntactic constituent within the VA category at the sentential level. The paper then examines how verbal adjectives encode the tense value [−PAST], proposing a new interpretation based on historical and morphological perspectives. Rather than the traditional interpretation that -i, as in takai, expresses non-past, it is argued that the phonologically zero inflexional suffix -0, as in [[[taka]i] ]0, denotes [−PAST] through opposition to -ta, specified as [+PAST], as in [[[taka]k]at]ta. Based on this, the paper further explores the allomorphy of the VA thematic suffix -i ≈ -(k)i ≈ -ku ≈ -ke and investigates how the distribution of these allomorphs is determined by their syntactic environments. Finally, the paper explores the similarities between the morphological structures of verbal adjectives and true verbs — particularly vowel-stem verbs.

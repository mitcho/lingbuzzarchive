A New Analysis of the Morphological Structure of Japanese Verbal Adjectives
Shinya Makino
October 2025
 

The present paper reanalyses the morphological structure of Japanese verbal adjectives (VAs) — simply adjectives, canonical adjectives, or i-adjectives. Traditionally, inflected forms of this class, such as takaku from takai "to be high/tall", have been analysed as consisting of the so-called stem taka- and the so-called ending -ku. However, given the MULTIPLE morphological, syntactic and semantic parallelisms with verbal stems, it can be inferred that takaku as a whole functions as the stem of the verbal adjective. This paper thus analyses taka- as one of the two immediate constituents of the VA stem takaku, which denotes, as a VA radical, the core lexical meaning of the verbal adjective and -ku as the other constituent, functioning as the VA thematic suffix — namely, VA stem-forming suffix — that licences the VA radical as a syntactic constituent within the VA category at the sentential level (from the perspective of Distributed Morphology, this constituent may be regarded as an overt instantiation of the categorial head a). The paper then examines how VAs encode the tense value [−PAST], proposing a new interpretation based on historical and morphological perspectives. Rather than the traditional view that -i, as in takai, expresses the non-past, it is argued that the phonologically zero inflexional suffix -0, as in [[[[taka]i] ]0], acquires the [−PAST] value through opposition to -ta, which is specified as [+PAST], as in [[[[taka]k]at]ta]. By contrast, the atemporal form takaku lacks any temporal suffix as in [[[[taka]ku] ] ]. Based on this, the paper further explores the allomorphy of the VA thematic suffix -i ≈ -(k)i ≈ -ku ≈ -ke and investigates how the distribution of these allomorphs is determined by their syntactic environments.

"Non-local A-movement" is predicted to exist, and it does
Giovanni Roversi
September 2025
 

In this paper I put together two ingredients: (i) featurally relativized probes (Béjar 2003 et seq.); (ii) a featural approach to the A/Ā-distinction (van Urk 2015). These two, combined, predict that we should find movement phenomena that are non-local, and yet show no other Ā-type properties (i.e. "non-local A-movement"). I show that this does in fact exist: in Äiwoo, a less local argument can move to spec,TP over a closer one if it has the right features. The existence of this type of movement supports van Urk (2015)'s argument that the locality properties of canonical A/Ā-movement are completely epiphenomenal, and only determined by the features searched for by the movement-triggering probe. Even without appealing to Ā-features of any kind (i.e., without resorting to "mixed A/Ā-movement"), we can still derive this kind of non-strictly-local A-movement as long as there is a featural motivation for it.

[Note: this paper is a proceedings-sized, simplified version of what I cover in chapters 3/6 of my dissertation (Roversi 2025, "Locality: a case study from Äiwoo"), also posted on lingbuzz.]

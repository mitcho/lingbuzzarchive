Reanalyzing perfects: The definite article and the rise of Romance HAVE-auxiliaries
Lieven Danckaert, Gerhard Schaden
August 2026
 

We propose a formal syntactic analysis of the Latin construction at the origin of Romance perfect tenses with a have-auxiliary, arguing that the Latin construction ‘habere + NP.acc + PaPa.acc’ is a grammatically passive structure in which habere ‘have’ is the main verb, selecting a small clause with the accusative NP as subject and the participle as predicate. Drawing on Myler’s (2016) analysis of have and insights from the literature on stative passives and the internal structure of participles, we show that the participles in these constructions may denote event and state tokens, and that different semantic readings of the construction correspond to a single syntactic structure. On the basis of this analysis, we develop a new account of the rise of analytic perfect tenses in Romance. We argue that the trigger for reanalysis was an independent change in the nominal domain — the emergence of a D-layer and in particular the grammaticalization of definite articles — which forced reanalysis specifically in contexts involving inalienably possessed direct objects. Once established in this critical context, the construction spread to new environments, including, for the first time, intransitive verbs.

Reconstruction of Middle Chinese Lai 来母 and Xia 匣母 Initials & their Old Chinese origin
Yanguang炎光 Guo郭
August 2026
 

Traditional Chinese phonology reconstructs the Middle Chinese Lai initial(来母) as the alveolar lateral approximant [l]. However, this reconstruction fails to account for its classification as a "tongue sound" (sheyin 舌音) in the Yunjing and for its distributional property of lacking chongniu doublets. This paper examines the phonological classification of Middle Chinese tongue sounds and proposes that the Lai initial should instead be reconstructed as the palatal lateral approximant [ʎ]. An articulatory analysis of [ʎ] indicates that it developed from the voiced palatal affricate [dʝ]. Drawing on previous scholarship regarding the palatalization mechanism of the Jian initial(见母), this study further demonstrates that the Middle Chinese Qun initial [ɡ] could likewise derive from [dʝ]. On this basis, the paper proposes a mechanism to account for the alternation between the Jian and Lai initials: certain characters classified under the Lai or Jian initials in the fanqie system of the Qieyun in fact shared the initial [dʝ] in Old Chinese, thereby obviating the need for the traditional *kl- cluster hypothesis. The affricate [dʝ] corresponds to the Middle Chinese Cheng initial. Its complementary counterpart, the Ding initial, is reconstructed as the labialized voiced palatal affricate [dʝʷ]. The sound [dʝʷ] could follow an analogous evolutionary path, diverging into the Qun initial of Division IV [ɡy] and the velar lateral approximant [ʟ], the latter being the proposed reconstruction for the Middle Chinese Xia initial(匣母). This provides an explanation for the classical dictum that "the third-division Yun initial belongs to the Xia initial" (yu san gui xia 喻三归匣): characters classified under the Yun or Xia initials in the Qieyun fanqie system shared the initial [dʝʷ] in Old Chinese.

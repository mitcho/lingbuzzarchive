Topicalization defined by syntax
Wei-Tien Dylan Tsai
February 2025
 

In this chapter, we present a general picture of how topicalization and topicality can be defined in morpho-syntactic terms. We start with an overview of the syntax and semantics of topics from the vantage point of generative syntax and formal logic. We then show that the notion of topic prominence can be defined by typological correlations through a systematic survey across languages. By applying objective tests to sort out the “gray areas” or the spectrum effects of topicality, this line of research may well contribute to our pursuit of an optimal solution to the mystery of linguistic variations: Both the macro-parameter setting of analyticity and the micro-parameter setting of null topic are to be justified on empirical grounds, which, if so justified, will provide an explicit account of the inner working of topicalization, as well as its interaction with other types of syntactic mechanisms such as pro-drop and focalization. Furthermore, the recent advancement of grammatical theories also offers a way to relate the core syntax to language use in the form of syntactic cartography. It becomes clear that the information structure can be encoded by the functional projections in the left periphery, and that various pragmatic/semantic considerations (e.g., aboutness, illocutionary forces, contrastivity, modality, etc.) may enter into the computation of the core syntax by fulfilling the interpretive needs on the interfaces. Through a painstaking investigation of the relative word order of various types of Chinese topics, a general topography has emerged, very much conforming to those observed in Italian and Hungarian. At the end, we test the above approach with a couple of non-canonical cases such as postverbal topics, topic copying and reflexives of nature, producing some quite interesting results.

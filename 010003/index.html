Exclusive focus in Akan and type flexibility
Ka-Fai Yip, Comfort Ahenkorah
May 2026
 

Exclusive focus particles in many languages have flexible positions: as adverbial particles (as in English, John onlyAdv saw Mary) or as adfocal particles (John saw onlyAdfoc Mary), with the same truth conditions. This raises the question of whether exclusives are ambiguous between an A-quantifier entry and a D-quantifier entry, dubbed the type-ambiguity view here (Horn 1969, Rooth 1985, 1992, Coppock and Beaver 2014, Yip and Adedeji 2024, Yip 2026; cf. Renans 2017); or uniformly as an A-quantifier, and the adfocal use is merely a syntactic “concord” phenomenon with a null exclusive operator OPEXCL, dubbed the uniform operator view here (Lee 2005, Quek and Hirsch 2017, Sun 2021, Bassi et al. 2022, Branan and Erlewine 2023, Aremu 2026; cf. Bayer 1996, i.a.).

This paper argues for the type-ambiguity view over the uniform operator view of exclusives for Akan. In Akan, only adfocal particles are attested: nkoaa ‘only’ and pɛ ‘only’, which may be doubled together. We show that nkoaa allows for cross-categorical association with focus, whereas pɛ alone does not and must depend on nkoaa for association with VP and CP. Based on their scopal behavior, we further demonstrate that the locus of exclusive semantics is on the adfocal particles but not a null OPEXCL. We propose a type-flexible analysis of exclusives with higher-order quantification and abstraction, which also explains exclusive doubling and the asymmetries in focus association.

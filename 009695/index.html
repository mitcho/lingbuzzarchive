On the identity of *-rare* in Passives and Potentials in Japanese
Tomoko Ishizuka, Hilda Koopman
January 2026
 

This paper argues that Japanese passive and potential -rare instantiate a single morpheme, as required by Anti-Homophony (Kayne,2017,2025). It shows that the passive is a (sub)component of one type of potential (psv-potential) and examines promotion and demotion in passives and psv-potentials for different ‘pseudopassives,’ i.e oblique passives. While goal, directional, and source obliques are possible in passives, locative and instrumental obliques are excluded. These are possible however in psv-potentials with an alethic possibility reading. We motivate a fine-grained clausal structure in which passive Voice and potential constructions, consisting of passive -rare and silent modal flavor *possibility* and *ability*, are merged at distinct heights (cf. Cinque 1999, 2004), as supported by aspectual ordering and the distribution of various obliques. This structure allows a unified antisymmetric analysis in which a single -rare is merged at different heights, derives its suffixal status from leftward movement of a phrase containing a verb, also deriving head-final orders. Anti-Locality blocks movement of the immediate complement of - rare. Attract Closest targets the highest movable complement, stranding the embedded subject (PRO). A minimal change in the merge order of modal and passive heads derives active potentials, unifying passives and all potential types. Comparative evidence supports key aspects of the analysis, which in turn provides strong support for approaches such as Collins (2016, 2025), where the external argument of passivized predicates is syntactically projected and passive Voice does not alter argument structure.

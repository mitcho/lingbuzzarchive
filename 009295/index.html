Feature spreading, redundancy, and blocking behavior
Scott Nelson, Eric Baković
July 2026
 

We analyze a collection of long-distance assimilation patterns using Boolean Monadic Recursive Schemes (BMRS; Bhaskar et al. 2020, Chandlee & Jardine 2021). BMRS describe input-output maps as logical transductions using conditional IF…THEN…ELSE… syntax. These patterns emerge from varying the presence, type, and position of redundancy conditions within BMRS feature spreading functions. When potential targets fail to receive or propagate harmonic features, redundancy conditions override harmony by blocking feature reception, halting propagation, or preventing both, depending on their type and scope. This offers a unified computational account of variation in feature spreading and blocking without traditional representational assumptions.

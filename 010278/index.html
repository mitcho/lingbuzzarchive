Mood and Modality
Tanya Bondarenko
June 2026
 

This chapter is dedicated to the grammatical category of mood and its relation to modality. It overviews some key empirical phenomena discussed in the literature, such as existence of subjunctive clauses with distinct profiles (selected vs. optional vs. polarity subjunctives), possibility of mood marking in embedded vs. root clauses, as well as mood marking in other environments such as purpose and relative clauses. The chapter compares analyses of these phenomena under three main theoretical approaches, outlining their virtues as well as the challenges that they face: ordering source-based theories, which link the choice of mood to the presence of an ordering source in the semantics of a modal, commitment-based theories, which consider the choice of mood to depend on the truth of the embedded proposition in some dedicated set of possible worlds, and alternatives-based theories, for which mood reflects presence of obligatorily activated focus alternatives to the embedded proposition. The chapter also raises the question of what the contribution of mood morphology is, suggesting that the connection between the modal meanings and mood morphemes is indirect: mood morphemes are better viewed as exponents of agreement with higher modal-introducing elements rather than the source of modality.

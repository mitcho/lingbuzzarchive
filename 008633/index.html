The remote and the impossible in Serbian
Magdalena Kaufmann, Neda Todorović
December 2024
 

Conditionals characterize epistemic possibilities (O-marked conditionals) or states of affairs that are somehow remote or impossible (X-marked conditionals). Serbian uses a combination of different verbal moods, conditional connectives, and tense marking to encode the relevant distinctions. We provide a compositional analysis that treats interpretation as sensitive to a salient belief state (by default, a representation of what is considered epistemically possible in the ongoing conversation). The salient belief state determines mood marking (indicative vs. conditional), and can be shifted by the connective da. Additionally, perfect in da-marked antecedents can introduce a temporal backshift to ”rerun history”. We argue that Serbian provides empirical evidence for a split between modal and temporal X-marking as proposed in the recent formal semantic literature, and discuss extensions of our assumptions to other occurrences of the relevant markers (complement clauses, purposive clauses, and standalone uses of dependent marked clauses).

'Lexical DP Blocking' in the Person-Case Constraint as a licensing failure: Comparative evidence from Mixtec
Michelle Yuan, Claudia Juárez Chávez
July 2025
 

****NEW TITLE: Remarks on 'Lexical DP Blocking' in the Person-Case Constraint: Comparative evidence from San Juan Piñas Mixtec**** Many languages display the Person-Case Constraint (PCC), or person-based restrictions on combinations of two pronominal clitics. Sichel and Toosarvandani (to appear) show that Southeastern Sierra Zapotec (henceforth, 'SSZ') displays a novel PCC-like pattern: object clitics of all persons are banned when the subject is a lexical DP. This is framed as a Lexical DP Blocking effect (LDB): the subject DP intervenes between a higher Agreeing probe and the object pronoun, preventing it from cliticizing. This paper brings forth novel data from the San Juan Piñas variety of Mixtec (SJPM), another Eastern Otomanguean language that displays the PCC and the LDB. However, the exact LDB pattern in SJPM contrasts with that in SSZ in analytically informative ways. Thus, this paper evaluates whether Sichel and Toosarvandani's (to appear) analysis of the LDB can be extended to SJPM. It also considers an alternative account, building on Deal's (2024) recent Interaction/Satisfaction theory of the PCC. While both accounts can broadly accommodate the core LDB patterns in SSZ and SJPM, we outline various outstanding questions and testable predictions arising from our discussion. We also show that ditransitives in both languages present an empirical challenge, in that they do not conform to the patterns seen in monotransitives. More broadly, this paper is intended to serve as an analytical blueprint for future work on this topic.

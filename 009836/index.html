Complex Segments and the Perceptual Compression Hypothesis
Carlos Wagner Oviedo
March 2026
 

This proposal introduces a new principle, the Perceptual Compression Hypothesis (PCH), which postulates that subsyllabic elements—onset, nucleus, and coda—represent segmental temporal units in phonetics capable of hosting multiple phonetic segments, thereby giving rise to complex segments. Following the tenets of Strict CV theory, which proposes that CV is the only syllable structure existing in phonology, this framework argues that the onset position admits only complex segments, while the arrangement of complex segments within rhyme elements depends on grammatical patterns exclusive to each language. The hypothesis distinguishes between two types of complex segments: primary complex segments, which obtain their linear order from a subsegmental algorithm following Wagner Oviedo (2026), and derived complex segments, which occur during derivation through the fusion of linear elements into a single segmental temporal position. To identify these structures, three diagnostics are proposed: predictability, separability, and distributional identification. The proposal further argues that, at a specific point in the derivation, primary and derived complex segments neutralize, exhibiting identical effects in phonological processes. Finally, this model is applied to three languages showing distinct arrangements: Spanish, characterized by more derived than primary complex segments; Sanskrit, with a similar distribution of both; and Huautla Mazatec, which features a predominance of primary complex segments.

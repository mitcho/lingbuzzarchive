Pivoting away from isomorphism: Content, Sound, and Constituent Structure
Noam Faust
September 2026
 

We make the double claim that the domain of Content (‘lexical meaning’) is not a constituent, ‎but a Span, and that the search for atomic Content is contingent on phonological representations. ‎We argue for such a dependency based on properties of (so-called) Hebrew complex prepositions, ‎such as ʕal jed-ej (múzika), ‘by (music)’ (lit ‘on hands of music’). The sequence P+N(+X) in ‎Modern Hebrew (MH) as well as in Biblical Hebrew and interim periods comprises hundreds of ‎attested tokens with rich idiosyncratic Content. However, the phonological, prosodic, Content, and ‎syntactic properties of P+N(+X) combinations do not align, with N forming a constituent with its ‎complement that excludes P (jedej muzika), but with Content and phonological properties ‎associated with the non-constituent P+N (ʕal jedej) arguing for the syntactic structure [ P [ N ‎DP/X]] in which Content can be associated with the non-constituent P-N, a Span. To affirm that ‎conclusion, we discuss and dismiss on empirical grounds several alternative analyses, among ‎which (i) the view that P+N are an instance of a possibly complex P; (ii) the possibility that N ‎incorporates into P; (iii) the possibility that Content is associated not with the sequence P-N, but ‎with N alone in the context of a designated P. ‎

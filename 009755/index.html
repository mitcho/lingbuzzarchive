Approaches to Experimental Methodologies to Study Stigmatized Languages: The Case of Ecuadorian Andean Spanish
Christian Puma, María Biezma
February 2026
 

The difficulty of obtaining quantitative evidence under controlled experimental conditions is exponentially increased when the language under study is highly stigmatized, and its speakers are aware of its stigma. In this paper, we describe the difficulties encountered in designing experimental tasks to study focus-marking strategies in Ecuadorian Andean Spanish (EAS). EAS is an emblem of the indigenous people of Ecuador and is frequently associated with an ideal rural, poor, uneducated, indigenous speaker. EAS differs from Ecuadorian Spanish (the standard spoken by the non-indigenous majority in Ecuador) in the level of influence of Kichwa, the Quechua language of Ecuador. We use EAS as a case study to examine the role of language ideologies in the participants’ performance in controlled experimental settings and to discuss possible solutions to overcome the difficulties derived from such complex sociolinguistic pictures. Specifically, we introduce what we dub the Community-Based Approach and the Saving-Face Approach to experimental methodologies. These approaches allow participants to, first, fall into everyday speech practices and, second, to detach themselves from the stigma carried by their language. Together, these approaches to experimental methodologies lead to a collection of data that accurately portrays the participants’ grammar.

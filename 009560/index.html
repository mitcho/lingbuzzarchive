Comitatives as pseudo-verbs in Ch'ol
Carol Rose Little
December 2025
 

This paper analyzes the two-place comitative in Ch’ol, a Mayan language of Chiapas, Mexico. This construction behaves like a transitive verb in some respects: it licenses two arguments, exhibits variable agreement, and there are two distinct semantic roles. In other respects, however, it does not take aspect or verbalizing morphology. These properties set the two-place comitative apart from canonical verbs, motivating its classification as a pseudo-verb. The analysis links the availability of the construction to the high-/low-absolutive distinction in Mayan and draws parallels with clause-union phenomena in Romance and Germanic, contributing to the typology of complex predicates and restructuring.

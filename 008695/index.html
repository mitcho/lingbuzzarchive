Differentiation of the hỏi and ngã tones in Binh Dinh
Thi Mai Tran
December 2024
 

Vietnamese is a tonal language with six tones, but it is commonly held that Northern Vietnam is the only part of the country with the complete set. Other parts of the country supposedly have only five tones or fewer as they make no distinction between hỏi and ngã. In this work we record the tones as pronounced in Bình Định province and show that this region also has the complete set of tones, with distinct pronunciations for hỏi and ngã. However, unlike in the North, Bình Định natives pronounce hỏi with a distinctly higher pitch than ngã. This is similar to the way these tones were produced during the 17th to 19th century, as recorded by western missionaries in musical notation. We can surmise that Bình Định has retained the original pronunciation brought by their migrant ancestors from the north, while the northern provinces around Hà Nội have for some reason inverted the pitches of hỏi and ngã, most likely during the Trịnh-Nguyễn secession or later (17th to 19th centuries). This result has implications for the history of chữ quốc ngữ, the latinized Vietnamese script. It is already known that Bình Định was one of the cradles of quốc ngữ, but some believe that the tonal system of the script, in particular the hỏi ngã distinction, must be based on the northern dialect. In view of the complete tonal set being available in Bình Định, this assumption is unnecessary.

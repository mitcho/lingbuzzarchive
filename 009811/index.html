Gebärdensprachen als bewegte Bilder? Eine Untersuchung zur Anwendbarkeit von Filmbeschreibungsbegriffen auf bildliche Diskursphänomene in Deutscher Gebärdensprache (DGS)
Anke Müller
April 2018
 

Titel English: Sign Languages as Moving Images? A Study on the Applicability of Descriptive Categories from Film Studies to Iconic Discourse Phenomena in German Sign Language (DGS)

Sign languages ​​exhibit a high proportion of iconic discourse structures, that are characterized by their scenic and illustrative nature and as such reminiscent of film sequences. Due to their productive imagery and combinability, these structures cannot be adequately described with traditional linguistic concepts. Starting from the topos of the film-like quality of sign languages, this dissertation systematically examines how and to what extent descriptive tools and concepts of film studies can be applied to structures specific for sign languages such as constructed action and so-called classifier constructions. The investigation is based on video recordings of various text types in German Sign Language (DGS). A basic analogy between cinematic and sign language images is established and argued for, thus allowing for the definition and operationalization of film concepts for use in language analysis. The comparative structural approach examines highly iconic structures in sign languages ​​from a new descriptive perspective and provides film-theoretical descriptive concepts for the analysis of signed discourse. The focus is on the key descriptive parameters of shot size, camera movement, and camera perspective. Concepts of these categories can be applied and, in some cases, fit very well with sign language. Limitations of their application arise where the composition of the sign language image does not correspond to that of film. As a result, this conceptually organized comparison elucidates the sign language specifics of its iconic structures.

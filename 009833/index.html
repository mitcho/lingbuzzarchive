How Music Refers: Music Semantics and Cross-Modal Correspondences
Léo Migotti Ramponi, Philippe Schlenker, Emmanuel Chemla
March 2026
 

Recent theoretical work in music semantics has proposed a formal framework to model how music can convey information about the extra-musical world. At the same time, a large body of empirical research on cross-modal correspondences has evidenced systematic mappings between perceptual dimensions across sensory domains. In this paper, we present several experimental audiovisual paradigms designed to show that the two efforts should be integrated, and argue that music semantics must be grounded in cross-modal correspondences while allowing for more expressive forms of meaning. We first demonstrate that pitch and loudness can be interpreted as the level of energy of a visual object, or as its distance from a viewpoint (experiment 1). Second, we provide evidence that pitch and dynamics can be used to pick specific objects as referents (experiment 2). Third, by evidencing preferences for persistent timbre/object associations, we show that timbre can be associated with objects on the fly in the absence of any salient cross-modal correspondence, a prediction that cross-modal correspondences alone cannot make without a richer music semantic model (experiment 3). Finally, we show that these associations persist and can be reused to infer the behavior of a specific object, even in the absence of visual cues (experiment 4). Taken together, a parsimonious interpretation of these results is that music can refer to objects of the extra-musical world and simultaneously convey some properties of these objects.

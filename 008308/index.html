The Person Case Constraint (PCC) and honorification
Soo-Hwan Lee
August 2025
 

The realization of honorific markers is driven by phi(φ)-feature agreement, specifically through first and second person features, rather than a dedicated honorific feature (see Wang 2023). In detail, I show that restrictions on hon- orification align with a version of the Person Case Constraint (PCC). This approach unifies two seemingly unrelated phenomena at first approximation, namely honorification and the PCC (see Adamson & Zompì 2024 for polite- ness pronouns and the PCC). The analysis accounts for why only certain argu- ments can bear honorific morphology, depending on their structural position. By recasting honorification as a case of person feature agreement, the proposal secures parsimony that links honorification to a broader theoretical constraint, while lending credence to the idea that syntax encodes pragmatic information through core grammatical features (see Pancheva & Zubizarreta 2018). To this end, two independent-looking phenomena receive a parallel treatment in syntax.

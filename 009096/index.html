Size-sensitive Sequence of Tense in Hungarian
János Egressy
November 2025
 

The English sentence John said that Mary was pregnant is ambiguous: Its simul- taneous reading is John said: ‘Mary is pregnant’; its backshifted reading is John said:‘Mary was pregnant’. Under Sequence of Tense Analyses of such past-under-past configurations (e.g. Ogihara 1995), the simultaneous reading arises if an embedded past head undergoes “deletion"-under-Agree with the matrix past head (the embedded past form is phonetically unaffected). Without deletion-under-Agree, the embedded past tense remains backshifted. The availability of deletion-under-Agree has been handled as a language-wide parameter, e.g. unlike English, Japanese lacks deletion-under-Agree (e.g. Ogihara and Sharvit 2012). In Hungarian, speech-reporting clauses are Japanese-like and obligatorily backshifted, whereas non-speech-reporting clauses are English-like, i.e. have a simultaneous reading. I propose that the impossibility of deletion-under-Agree in speech-reporting clauses comes from size-dependent opacity (e.g. Williams 2003).

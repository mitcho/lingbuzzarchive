A well-formedness condition on alternative generation
Zachary Satoshi Feldcamp, Ido Benbaji-Elhadad, Ido Benbaji
September 2025
 

Listeners compute inferences by negating alternatives to a given utterance. In this study of the syntax-pragmatics interface, it is argued that a syntactic well-formedness filter acts on structural alternatives, so that an inference that could only be generated by negating the interpretation of an ill-formed syntactic structure does not arise, a claim which is in tension with previous suggestions that grammar does not constrain alternatives. This paper takes as its empirical focus the distribution of temporariness inferences in English. The use of progressive aspect with a stative predicate gives rise to an inference formed by negating a non-progressive alternative, which may contradict world knowledge and yield infelicity. But novel data is presented which show that temporariness inferences do not arise in reduced relative clauses, existential sentences, and predicate inversion constructions, where syntax independently requires that an active verb be progressive. It is argued that the syntactic well-formedness filter prevents the pragmatic component from accessing the ill-formed non-progressive alternative that would need to be negated to form a temporariness inference.

Recursion in NP: pseudopartitive measures require complementation, not specification
Malhaar Shah
September 2025
 

This paper provides evidence favoring one of two hypotheses regarding the structure of measure phrases in pseudopartitives (three boxes of books). It compares the ‘Spec’ hypothesis (Schwarzschild, 2006), which places measure phrases (three books) in Specifier position of the substance, and the the ‘Comp’ hypothesis (Stickney, 2009), which places substance phrases (of books) in Complement position of the measure. Only the latter requires nominal complements to nominals (recursion inside NP, which has been more controversial than clausal recursion – Stowell, 1989; Frank and Kroch, 1994; Frank, 2002; Chomsky, 2008; Kayne, 2008; Citko, 2014). The Spec hypothesis is motivated by semantic considerations relating to ‘Monotonicity’, but Monotonicity appearing in verbal comparatives (Wellwood 2019) undercuts this as an argument for this syntax for pseudopartitives. Syntactic diagnostics (agreement, constituency, movement and ellipsis, and locality) show that the Spec hypothesis cannot be correct.

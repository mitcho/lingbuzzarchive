What Can Mongolian Tell Us about Binding?
Chigchi Bai
June 2026
 

Drawing on Mongolian data, this paper proposes that syntactic binding arises as Agree between the phi-features on a predicate-internal subject and those in the specifier of an anaphor as a possessive DP in a lower position on the one hand, and is reassessed when the subject moves up to a position intertwining with verbal inflections on the other hand, leading to the distinction between predicate-internal (phase-based) and predicate-external (inflection-based) binding. Predicate-external binding occurs when this Agree is followed by Move (of the subject from inside the predicate into the inflectional domain), while predicate-internal binding occurs without Move. The paper aligns with the pre-Minimalist Standard Binding Theory by defining TP as the binding domain for external binding, and with the current Minimalist binding theories by using the vP/VoiceP phase boundary for internal binding. It argues that the locality of syntactic binding holds for predicate-external binding, arising from verbal inflections, and that the Anaphor Agreement Effect holds for predicate-internal binding, stemming from the “immaturity” of the internal subject as the binder before inflectional properties are assigned to the predicate, rather than from a capsule-like structure of anaphors.

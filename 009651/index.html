What Can Mongolian Tell Us about Binding?
Chigchi Bai
January 2026
 

In Mongolian, division of labor is clear for the anaphor öör and the reflexive-possessive suffix, -aa, the latter being the hallmark of reflexive binding: öör marks binding not intertwined with verbal inflections and -aa marks it when it is intertwined with verbal inflections. The morpho-syntactic properties of Mongolian binding strategies centered on this property can tell us the following. First, binding is dynamic and derivational process; it is an Agree relation between phi-features; Agree may be followed by Move of the binder from the predicate-internal position (Spec of VoiceP) to a position outside the predicate (Spec of TP); when not followed by Move, Agree makes up predicate-internal binding; when Agree plus Move takes place, predicate-external binding is implemented. Second, TP as the binding domain as identified in the pre-minimalist binding theory (the Standard Binding Theory (Chomsky 1981)) holds true for predicate-external binding, while the minimal phase boundary as a binding domain as in the late minimalist era holds for predicate-internal binding. Third, The Anaphor Agreement Effect follows from the “internal-hood” of the binder, which is “immature” as a subject, with the inflectional properties of the predicate is yet to be assigned, but not from a capsule-like structure of anaphors. Fourth, anaphors are possessive DPs structurally, which are formed in a portmanteau fashion, where phi-features that sit in the specifier of the possessive D head are valued by those on antecedent nominals occupying Spec of a predicate phrase (VoiceP). Fifth, reflexivity, which is conditioned by binding as feature-valuation (Agree), is thus a Spec-Spec (subject-subject or subject-possessor) relation, rather than a Spec-Comp (subject-object) relation.

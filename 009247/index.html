Role of Diacritics in Markedness and Nasality in Tiv Phonology
Tagesa Akpagher
August 2025
 

This paper, Role of Diacritics in Markedness and Nasality in Tiv Phonology examines the role of diacritics in Tiv phonology, focusing on their function in marking nasality and distinguishing phonemic contrasts. Using Distinctive Feature Theory as a framework, the study analyses how diacritics systematically represent marked features, particularly nasalised vowels and consonants. Data were drawn from native-speaker intuition, interviews, and orthographic texts. The findings demonstrate that diacritics consistently indicate contrastive features in Tiv, clarifying the phonemic inventory and revealing an internally coherent system that aligns with broader Niger-Congo patterns, enabling cross-linguistic comparison. The study also highlights the importance of precise diacritic use in supporting effective orthographic representation, literacy materials, and language teaching. It is recommended that future research continue documenting tone and nasalisation patterns and develop standardised orthographic conventions through collaboration with educators and native speakers. Such efforts will improve teaching resources, dictionaries, and written communication, helping to preserve the Tiv linguistic heritage and promote effective language use among both native speakers and learners.

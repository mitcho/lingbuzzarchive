The expression of number and (in)definiteness in two varieties of Central Kurdish
Samuel Jambrović, Arsalan Kahnemuyipour
June 2025
 

In Central Kurdish, plural marking appears in different positions in indefinite, definite, and demonstrative noun phrases, and there are certain morpheme orders that cannot be derived by standard approaches to movement. To address this issue, we propose a reanalysis of number and definiteness morphology in this language, one that is based on a comparative investigation of the Silemānī and Ardałānī varieties. Building on insights by Wiltschko (2008, 2009), we represent number as a PLURALIZER (PL) whose adjunction site depends on whether DP projects. In indefinite noun phrases, PL modifies n, the head of a functional projection that dominates NP, and in definite and demonstrative noun phrases, PL modifies D. We then draw a connection to research on strong versus weak definiteness and decompose the definite suffix -aká into -ak, a component that expresses anaphoricity, and -á, a component that expresses uniqueness. Finally, we argue that PL and D undergo fusion in anaphoric constructions, in which case these nodes are realized by the same form, -ān, in both Silemānī and Ardałānī Kurdish.

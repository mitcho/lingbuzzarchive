The "-Ly Manner Adverb Opposability Theorem"
Samuel Clymer
April 2025
 

This paper introduces the "-Ly Manner Adverb Opposability Theorem," which states that any "-ly" adverb describing the manner in which an action is performed must possess a definable scalar opposite to carry semantic meaning. Applying this theorem reveals that "consciously" and "nonconsciously" fail the opposability test, rendering them linguistically and philosophically incoherent. Although the incoherence of terms like "subconscious" and "unconscious" was previously demonstrated by Franz Brentano, this paper focuses specifically on the linguistic structure of manner adverbs and the vacuity of "consciously" and "nonconsciously".

Some Notes towards a Dual Model of Language Development— The Computational System behind a Non-INFL stage of Child Language Acquisition Absent Minded: A Reply to Borer and Rohrbacher
Joseph Galasso
May 2006
 

The study of syntactic development in children, for all intents and purposes, is reducible to a single-minded inquiry into how the very young child (implicitly) knows to distinguish between lexical stems and functional affixes. Hence, the overriding question burning in the minds of most developmental linguists is morpho-phonological in nature. It would seem that the child must at least know (a priori) the stem before she can then engage in a dual-track process by which ambient sound separation of the morpho-phonological distinction attributive to past tense is carried out, say, between the paradigmatic representation of the English word play vs. play-ed (a dual processing which provokes separation of the {play}-stem and the {ed}-affix). Similarly, we can ask what it is the child exactly knows’ when she correctly decomposes (from the very earliest MLU) only one of two seemingly similar phonological chunks taken from the ambient speech flow, say, speaks /spi:ks/ and dance /dæns/—where the former entails a decomposed [stem+affix] dual processing while the latter does not.

The Meaning of Choreographies: Making a Case for a Multimodal Semantic Analysis
Eléonore Scholler
June 2024
 

Empirical observations have shown that there are clear intuitions of dance-music coherence in dance performances. This paper builds on previous applications of formal linguistic methodology to other cognitive systems to assess the advantages of considering a semantic analysis over a purely syntactic approach, to account for dance and music perceptual integration as a coherent whole. Syntactic analyses of music and of dance have been proposed, and were recently combined into a case-study analysis identifying structural matches/mismatches between dance and music. Formal semantic analyses of narrative dance, of music, and of combined music-animations suggest that semantics may also play a role in dance-music inferences. Despite support from both formal and nonformal research, the joint meaning of combined dance and music has not been formally addressed. Based on choreographical examples, I first examine how dance-music multimodal interpretations can be analyzed purely syntactically by mapping congruent (corresponding) structural properties. However, comparison with intuitive interpretations of dance-music sequences demonstrates that a strictly syntactic analysis is too narrow to account for the complex range of inferences evoked by choreographies. I then assess how previous applications of formal semantic methodology to non-standard objects – namely truth-conditional semantics and discourse referents – can extend to dance-music combinations, and further propose a novel analysis of multimodal sequences using semantic conjunction of each modality’s set of denotations. I argue that such a semantic analysis is not only possible, but also necessary to better account for intuitive inferences by distinguishing cases of dance-music in-/coherence within both syntactic incongruence and congruence. Finally, I conclude that a combined semantic analysis of dance-music sequences is necessary and complementary to the syntactic congruence analysis in order to productively account for intuitive inferences of coherence. As a more general goal, this work attempts to offer a novel, formal approach to current endeavors exploring the underlying cognitive architecture of integrative perceptual processing by investigating multimodal interactions.

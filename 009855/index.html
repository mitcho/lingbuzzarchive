Peripheries in Continental West-Germanic
Mark de Vries, Jan-Wouter Zwart
March 2026
 

The Continental West Germanic languages provide a fertile ground for studying the structure of the clausal peripheries and the syntactic relations (movement or otherwise) between elements in the clausal peripheries and positions in the remainder of the clause. The left periphery is marked by the verb-second effect, positioning the finite verb to the immediate right of the first constituent. There is some evidence for distinguishing separate positions for topics and wh-elements in the left periphery, but very little evidence for the full range of left-peripheral positions proposed in the literature. In addition, there appears to be a position for even more peripheral elements that do not trigger the verb-second effect, i.e., left-dislocation. The right periphery likewise shows a distinction between more and less canonical peripheral regions, involved in extraposition and right-dislocation (backgrounding or afterthought), respectively. Neither the structure of the right periphery nor the processes by which elements come to occupy these positions are fully understood, suggesting that the traditional tool kit for describing them (including rightward movement) may need to be replaced or enriched by others, involving conjunction and ellipsis.

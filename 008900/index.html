What Backward Negative Concord tells us about control
Anastasiia Voznesenskaia
November 2024
 

In this article, I propose an analysis of a phenomenon I call Backward Negative Concord (BNC). This is when a negative concord item in a matrix clause is licensed by negation in an embedded clause, contra expectations of standard neg- ative concord requiring clausemate negation or at the very least the c-command of negation. It is shown that BNC appears in control structures in Russian if the NCI is the controller. Based on the generalization that a CP layer in the control complement is required I propose that the complementizer introduces a [uNEG] feature that gets transmitted to the controller. It is also demonstrated that an embedded CP layer is a necessary but insufficient condition for BNC. Finally, cases of BNC occurring with what look like raising verbs are discussed together with their differences from BNC in control structures.

Outlook Management: 'Subjective' Meanings of Discourse-Sensitive Adverbs and Particles
Yusuke Kubota
December 2025
 

This chapter examines Japanese discourse-sensitive adverbs and focus particles (e.g., dōse, mushiro, yoppodo; nanka, kurai, koso) that express “subjective” evaluation meanings. I argue that these items are outlook markers in the sense of Kubota & Ido (2025; 'Outlook markers (as abstract discourse markers)' SALT35): they are abstract discourse markers that signal how an attitude holder (typically, the speaker) positions the prejacent relative to a salient counterstance in the discourse. After reviewing major categories of secondary (or, ‘non-at-issue’) meaning in the literature, the chapter shows that outlook markers pattern partly like presuppositions and partly like conventional implicatures, motivating a more fine-grained view of discourse management. Empirically, outlook markers exhibit a ‘two-layered’ meaning: (i) a presuppositional component imposing a constraint on the prior discourse on the existence of a counterstance, and (ii) an expressive-like component that conveys the attitude holder’s stance by signalling the relationship between the prejacent and the counterstance. Unlike typical expressives, however, their evaluative import readily shifts under attitude predicates and interacts systematically with modal expressions. The chapter concludes that outlook markers are meta-level devices that make subjective stances explicit in proposing discourse updates.

Foundations of Biolinguistics
Kleanthes K. Grohmann
August 2026
 

Biolinguistics, to define it in a single phrase, is the study of language that takes its biological underpinnings seriously. This short book out-lines the basic tenets of a biolinguistic approach framed in the form of five ‘foundational questions’. While often associated with generative investigations of language, biolinguistics is not—and should not be—just another term for generative grammar; biolinguistic approaches to the scientific study of human language will thus be put in a richer context by inspecting and dissecting the foundational questions, with par-ticular reference to cognitive approaches from generative and non-generative perspectives alike. The organizing distinction developed here is between ‘cognitive linguistics in the narrow sense’ (nativist, hence largely generative) and ‘cognitive linguistics in the broad sense’ (usage-based or emergentist, i.e. definitely non-generative). As such, the book can be seen as a primer for cognitive at-large approaches to the study of human language on biolinguistic foundations. This first chapter presents the basic notion and the dynamic field of biolinguistics somewhat historically, provides relevant theoretical and conceptual backgrounds, introduces the five foundational questions and their eponymous problems, and sets the stage for what is to come.

Successor-Saturated Ordinals and Indefinite Tail Activation A RED/DED Operator Extension of the Successor-Closed Sequence Lexicon
MAKS PANNER
January 2026
 

This paper strengthens a lexical hypothesis about ordinal meaning: encoding a position as FIRST necessarily encodes a successor position, and more strongly, encodes all successor positions in the relevant sequence. Formally, sequence is defined as an index structure whose domain is successor-closed (or at minimum two-element) and whose semantic interface guarantees a successor for the initial index. Under the Successor-Closed Sequence Lexicon (SCSL), FIRST ⇒ SECOND is not pragmatic enrichment but a lexical entailment/presupposition triggered by the ordinal itself. Building on SCSL, I introduce Indefinite Tail Activation (ITA): when FIRST anchors the beginning of a sequence, all remaining successor slots are introduced into the discourse as an underspecified remainder that is canonically lexicalized as non-specific indefiniteness (“some / a few / others”). ITA is not derived from conversational reasoning; it is a lexical interface consequence of successor saturation. The proposal is integrated into a RED/DED operator architecture: RED implements refinement along the successor chain (FIRST → SECOND → THIRD → …), while DED implements controlled collapse from refined ordinal microstructure back to a coarse remainder expressed by non-specific indefinites. The resulting system intentionally changes the diagnostic landscape: standard implicature tests (cancellability, weakening under embedding) are not preserved, because successor availability and tailindefiniteness are lexical definedness/interface conditions rather than conversational inferences. The paper concludes with a replication-ready diagnostic set, a compact elicitation grid, and computational/neuroscientific implications for successor-chain activation and ordinal-driven discourse updates.

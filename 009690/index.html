When AI’s helpfulness becomes harmful II: A current AI system recommends cherry-picking and HARKing
Shigeto Kawahara
January 2026
 

This study examines whether Gemini, an AI research assistant system that is widely used among college students, recommends ethically questionable statistical practices when analyzing experimental data. I presented Gemini with mock data designed to test for “cherry-picking”—--selectively reporting results from a subset of the data that supports a hypothesis. When the full dataset showed no significant difference but a subset did, Gemini recommended reporting only the subset analysis and suggested post-hoc hypothesis changes (i.e. HARKing). The results underscore the need for stronger ethical guardrails in AI systems and explicit instruction in research ethics for students using AI tools as their study partner.

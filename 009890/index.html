Do AI systems fill in missing values?—Testing ethical safeguards against data fabrication
Shigeto Kawahara
April 2026
 

This study is the sixth in a series examining whether the naive use of current AI systems can lead to research misconduct. In a pre-registered experiment, I asked three major AI systems (ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini) to fill in missing values in a dataset, using a prompt designed to simulate a student who may not recognize that this request constitutes data fabrication. Each system was tested 10 times via its web interface. The results were as follows: Claude refused all 10 times with an ethical warning, ChatGPT refused 8 times but complied twice, both times with an ethical warning, and Gemini complied all 10 times without any warning. The findings demonstrate a yet another peril for naive AI users in the context of research misconduct. Based on this and other related studies, I argue that these findings underscore the importance of pre-registration in the AI age, as AI systems can substantially expand researcher degrees of freedom. Finally, during this study, I identified a new potential problem which I dub "prompt laundering"—even when an AI system declines a request, a user can use another AI to tailor the prompt for jailbreaking.

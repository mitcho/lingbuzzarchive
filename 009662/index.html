Scrambling in Japanese: Hidden Similarities to Topicalization
Hideharu Tanaka, Koji Shimamura
January 2026
 

The purpose of this paper is to establish two novel claims on the nature of scrambling in Japanese. First, as opposed to the general consensus that has been reached since Saito (1992) and Tada (1993), we show that not only clause-internal (CI) scrambling, but also long-distance (LD) scrambling can create new binding/scope relations, so the latter need not undergo radical reconstruction, either. Second, we argue that, though CI- and LD-scrambling can both have semantic effects, they are still not uniform in nature and should be derived by distinct grammatical processes, contra Tada (1990), Kitahara (2002), and Saito (2003). In particular, we show that, for LD-scrambling to apply, it must obey the same contrast condition as topicalization in English does, but CI-scrambling need not. To derive this disparity, we adopt our recent idea in Shimamura and Tanaka (to appear), under which LD-scrambling is always a kind of topicalization, but CI-scrambling can be derived, not only by topicalization, but also by base-generation. We lend further support to this dual identity of CI-scrambling by examining the behavior of VP-internal scrambling in the double object constrcution, based on some of Nemoto’s (1993) findings.

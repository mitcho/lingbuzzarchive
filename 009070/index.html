On the Underlying Long Vowels in Contemporary Standard European Portuguese
Shinya Makino
September 2025
 

Traditionally, Portuguese linguistics has acknowledged surface vowel length alternation conditioned by stress in Contemporary Standard European Portuguese (EP), yet has rarely addressed the possibility that underlying vowel quantity oppositions — established through phonological developments during the 14th-15th centuries — may persist in the contemporary language. In contrast, Makino (2006¹, 2006², 2007²) argued for the actual persistence of such underlying oppositions even in Contemporary EP — underlying short /ɛ a ɔ/ → [ɛ́ː áː ɔ́ː] ≈ [ɨ ɐ u] vs. underlying long /ɛː aː ɔː/ → [ɛ́ː áː ɔ́ː] ≈ [ɛ a ɔ] — seeking to account for a range of phonological phenomena. The present study revisits these issues and offers a novel account of two phenomena. The analysis is mainly developed within a framework of moraic theory and Optimality Theory with phonological stratification, and employs a revised view of feature geometry in which the traditional [±ATR] is reinterpreted as a tongue-height feature. Rathar than introducing new phonetic data, this study attempts a theoretical reinterpretation — from an entirely different perspective — of well-known facts, whose interrelations may not previously have been considered.

On Externalization (talk handout)
Richard Kayne
February 2026
 

To say that linear order is part of core syntax is to say that temporal order is part of core syntax. Conversely, to say that linear order is not at all part of core syntax is to say that temporal order is not at all part of core syntax. Which is in turn to say that the language faculty has not integrated into its core any notion of time. Such a(n arguably counter-biological) position, or close to it, was in effect taken by Chomsky (1995, 221), who said “If humans could communicate by telepathy, there would be no need for a phonological component, at least for the purposes of communication.” The question for future work (not limited to linguistics) is whether or not the human brain has or has not entirely disregarded time at the very core of one of its most important components.

Feed me your manuscript, confidential or not: Current AI systems may not protect the confidentiality of peer review
Shigeto Kawahara
September 2026
 

Manuscripts under peer review are confidential materials, and as such, major publishers prohibit reviewers from uploading them to commercial generative AI systems. The current experiments tested whether the current AI systems consistently comply with this policy, and showed that this is not necessarily the case. In Experiment 1, Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini rarely explained confidentiality when a first-time reviewer requested general help (2/30 trials). When the user explicitly asked whether a manuscript could be shared, ChatGPT accepted in 10/10 trials and Claude in 6/10, whereas Gemini consistently refused with a confidentiality warning. However, in Experiment 2, given a very similar prompt but with an attached manuscript, Gemini analyzed the attached file without warning in all 10 trials, suggesting the instability of its safeguard system. Further, Experiment 3a found that Gemini sometimes accepted sharing when prompted in Japanese. The result of this exploratory experiment motivated a confirmatory experiment (Experiment 3b), which directly compared English and Japanese. As predicted, Gemini accepted sharing more often in Japanese (27/30) than in English (1/30). Taken together, the current findings show that an apparently reliable confidentiality safeguard may fail when the request format or language changes, underscoring the need to test whether the AI's ``ethical'' responses generalize across conditions.

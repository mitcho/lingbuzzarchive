Syntax and Semantics of Old Iranian Relative Clauses: Theoretical and Formal Implications
Setayesh Dashti
August 2025
 

Old Iranian languages display patterns of relativization that do not fit neatly into existing accounts. This thesis examines free relatives and correlatives, primarily in Old Avestan, with comparative evidence from Old Persian and Young Avestan. Its first chapter addresses a basic methodological problem: how can we reliably classify constructions in ancient and low-resource languages when interpretations are uncertain and native-speaker judgments unavailable? It develops a replicable algorithm that moves from formal schema induction to typological comparison and the testing of structural and interpretive predictions. Using this method, the Old Avestan data are situated within the cross-linguistic typology of headless and head-internal free relatives, correlatives, and related constructions. The comparison establishes these patterns as grammatical constructions in their own right, rather than variants of postnominal relative clauses, and motivates expanding the cross-linguistics typology of free relatives to include non-interrogative-based forms. The thesis also proposes a novel analysis of free relatives at the syntax-semantics interface. Its central advantage is that it derives the maximalizing semantics of free relatives without recourse to empty categories or silent operators. It further examines Old Avestan generalizing relative clauses and relates their generalizing expressions to Negative Polarity and Free Choice Items. The surveys of Old Persian and Young Avestan show that these constructions are less productive and less varied outside Old Avestan.

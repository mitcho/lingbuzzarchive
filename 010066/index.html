Wh-infinitives as topic-setting questions
Colin Brown, Starr Sandoval
June 2026
 

This paper investigates the semantics and pragmatics of root wh-infinitive questions (INFQs); that is, unembedded wh-questions featuring an infinitival verb. Building on prior work on non-finite wh-constructions (Kjellmer 1982, Duffley and Enns 1996, Bhatt 1999), we propose that the INFQs in (1) and (2) should be characterized as topic-setting questions, a term we borrow from Farkas (2025). Informally, a topic-setting question is a non-canonical speech act in which the Speaker assumes that no salient answer is currently available to resolve the issue raised by the question. The conversational function of uttering a topic-setting question is to prompt deliberation in order to arrive at a resolution. We argue that this is a conventionalized effect that arises from the marked status of INFQs relative to canonical finite wh-questions.

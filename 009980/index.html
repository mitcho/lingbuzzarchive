The optional infinitive stage in Japanese
Shigeru Miyagawa, Nozomi Moritake, Ken Wexler
May 2026
 

In adult Japanese, subjects can be marked with -ga (nominative case) or -no (genitive case). This case alternation is possible only in embedded contexts headed by a nominal expression, such as a nominal complement clause or a relative clause; genitive subjects never appear in the matrix clause. In child Japanese, however, genitive subjects have been observed to show up in the matrix clause. In this study, we explore the reason why the genitive subject can occur in the matrix clause in child Japanese. We argue that the matrix genitive subject in child Japanese is an instance of the Optional Infinitive Stage of language development. We propose a modification to the feature combination in Wexler’s OIS work in order to adopt it for a language without phi-feature agreement.

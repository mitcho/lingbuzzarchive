Accommodation, acquisition, attrition? The (un)changing grammars of Standard Southern British English speakers in Belfast
E Jamieson, Glyn Hicks, Laura Domínguez, Tanja Schmid
June 2024
 

There has been traditionally little communication and consensus between the field of language attrition, and variationist sociolinguists investigating dialect change, despite shared interest in the outcomes of language and dialect contact. In this paper, we aim to bridge this divide, outlining ways in which the fields already converge, and where they can inform each other to build a greater understanding of the mechanisms underlying changes to an adult’s otherwise established grammatical state. We do so by investigating two properties of Belfast English in the grammars of L1a Standard Southern British English speakers who have been living in Belfast for, on average, 25 years, having migrated from England after childhood. We find no use or acceptance of the Northern Subject Rule, but individual-level use and acceptance of embedded inverted questions. The study is framed by Hicks & Domínguez’s (2020) Attrition via Acquisition model. We outline how this model of grammatical attrition can be used to predict where and how morphosyntactic change as a result of dialect (or language) contact may occur, and show how greater specification of this model with relevant factors from sociolinguistics can account for the results of the study at hand, concluding there is more to be gained from these fields working together than remaining disparate.

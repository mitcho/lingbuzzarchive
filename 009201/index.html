Language Evolution Across Generations: A Comparative Study of Gen Z and Gen Alpha Communication Patterns
Rabia Shehzad
July 2025
 

This research article explores the evolving communication patterns of Generation Z (Gen Z) and Generation Alpha (Gen Alpha) in the context of their digital media consumption. Both generations are characterized as digital natives, but the technological landscapes they have grown up in differ substantially, influencing the ways they use language. Gen Z, born roughly between 1997 and 2012, grew up during the rise of social media platforms such as Facebook, YouTube, and Instagram, where text-based interactions and image sharing were primary modes of communication. Gen Alpha, born from 2013 onwards, have come of age in an era dominated by short-form video platforms like TikTok and YouTube Shorts, as well as voice-activated assistants and interactive apps, which shape their language use in distinct ways. By analyzing content from multiple social media platforms—including interviews and informal conversations on YouTube, comment sections on Instagram posts, and viral TikTok videos—this study identifies unique linguistic features, slang, and discourse styles that characterize each generation. The data collected reflect natural language use in both casual and semi-structured settings, offering insight into vocabulary choices, syntax, pragmatic use of language, and the increasing role of visual and multimodal elements such as emojis, GIFs, and video filters in communication. The study applies a qualitative research methodology, using thematic and content analysis to examine transcripts of interviews and selected social media interactions. Special attention is given to the influence of digital culture on language acquisition and development, showing how each generation adopts, modifies, or creates language suited to their respective digital ecosystems. For example, Gen Z is shown to use a wide range of internet slang that often reflects irony, sarcasm, and intertextuality, such as phrases like “sus” (suspicious), “vibe check,” and “no cap,” many of which have roots in gaming communities and meme culture. Meanwhile, Gen Alpha exhibits a more direct and repetitive linguistic style, heavily borrowing from TikTok trends and influencer catchphrases, such as “let’s gooo,” “periodt,” and “hit that like button,” which often accompany video content and are reinforced by visual aids. Findings reveal that Gen Alpha’s communication is increasingly multimodal, integrating verbal language with emojis, stickers, and video effects in ways that go beyond traditional textual communication. This reflects a broader trend toward communication that relies not only on words but also on visuals and auditory cues, a feature less dominant in Gen Z’s interactions. The study also highlights educational implications, noting that teachers and linguists observe Gen Alpha struggling more than Gen Z when shifting between informal digital language and formal academic registers. Ultimately, this research fills a critical gap in sociolinguistic literature by providing a detailed comparative analysis of two digitally native generations within the rapidly changing media landscape. It offers valuable insights for educators, content creators, and language researchers interested in understanding the dynamic interplay between technology and language evolution. The study emphasizes the need to continuously adapt educational and communication strategies to better engage with younger generations whose language is increasingly shaped by their digital environments.

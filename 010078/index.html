To seem or not to seem
Anton Zimmerling
June 2026
 

This paper examines the semantics and morphosyntax of the Russian verb kazat’sja ‘seem’. Kazat’sja and vygljadet’‘look’ are raising verbs that promote their Stimulus argument and raise an embedded constituent to the matrix clause. I argue that kazat’sja is a hybrid predicate combining properties of putative and perceptual verbs, and discuss Apresjan’s proposal to distinguish two lexemes: putative kazat’sja¹ versus perceptual kazat’sja². Kazat’sja² is an obligatory raising verb, whereas with kazat’sja¹ raising is optional. Both kazat’sja¹ and kazat’sja² project three thematic roles: sentential Stimulus, dative Experiencer; and Observer, which may remain covert. The analysis is based on the distribution of adjectives marked by the instrumental case versus non-agreeing predicatives in structures such as X-u kažetsja strannym / stranno + [Stimulus].

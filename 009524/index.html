Fact, Say, or Fiction: Factivity Alternation, Expletive Negation in Korean/Japanese vs. Non-canonical whether in English, and Imaginative Worlds
Chungmin Lee
May 2025
 

We examine how different complementation types help determine the semantic class of the attitude predicates that select them. We show cross-linguistic variation of factivity alternation, where canonically factive predicates such as ‘know’ and ‘recognize’ develop non-factive alternants—a phenomenon distinct from the inherent non-factivity of doxastic predicates such as ‘think’ and ‘believe.’ These non-factive alternants instead take SAY-related (reportative) complementation, rather than the FACT-selecting complements of their factive uses, and arise only when nonverdical logical or discourse factors—such as temporal distance, misbelief, or counter-doxastic contexts—override the predicate’s default FACT profile. Fictive predicates such as ‘imagine’ also employ this non-factive, SAY-type complementation. We propose broad cross-linguistic constraints governing attitude expression. We show how surface-ambiguous complements are resolved in different languages by focus or complement morphology. We argue that local perspective-shift accounts do not adequately account for the non-factive alternants of cognitive-discovery predicates and emotive factives. These patterns suggest a structural distinction between factive complements headed by a covert FACT and non-factive complements headed by a covert SAY. We also analyze ‘non-canonical’ whether-complements with neg-raisable predicates (‘think’, ‘believe’), and with ‘hope’, ‘fear’, and ‘imagine’, comparing them to expletive negation in Korean and Japanese—both functioning as logical expletivization yielding positive bias. Finally, we consider how ‘imagine’ (whether- or that-complements) contributes to the creation of aesthetically unusual worlds.

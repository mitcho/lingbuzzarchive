Morphophysics: A Field-Theoretic Synthesis of Morphological Evolution and Typological Universals
MAKS PANNER
February 2026
 

Morphophysics models morphological structure as a dynamical field in which grammatical categories, affix positions, and co-occurrence constraints emerge as attractors shaped by a shared geometry (curvature, boundaries, resonance minima) and universal forces (upward drift, downward anchoring, lateral expansion, and restorative coherence). In this article, we synthesize the theory presented across the source documents into a unified framework that connects (i) diachronic cyclicity (drift → turbulence → collapse → renewal → integration), (ii) typological universals as emergent properties of shared field geometry, and (iii) cross-system convergence and typological clustering as global outcomes of trajectory dynamics. We make the synthesis explicit by (a) specifying a minimal formal vocabulary (state space, potential landscape, occupancy dynamics, interference constraints), (b) mapping each theoretical component to empirical diagnostics suitable for corpus and historical study, and (c) providing a quantitative ‘sanity check’ using term-frequency and co-occurrence structure extracted from the full text of the manuscript and article. The resulting article provides a consolidated theoretical statement, a testable empirical program, and a novelty assessment that separates genuine architectural contributions from metaphor risks. The primary contribution is not the use of physics metaphors per se, but the attempt to turn them into a predictive, modular architecture in which universals, typological clusters, and cyclic change are derived from a single set of shared primitives.

Chinese Dialectal Parameterisation and Microvariation: From Hong Kong to the World
Keith Tse
September 2026
 

As a child growing up in a triglossic society such as Hong Kong, my native intuitions in Chinese are shared between standard literary Mandarin and the Cantonese vernacular, which has proven to be useful to my research in comparative parametric syntax and teaching Chinese as a foreign language. Applying formal parameters based on universal cartography, the main dialectal differences between Cantonese and Mandarin can be classified in three types: 1) functional categories with different selectional properties 2) different word order of the same lexical items 3) syntactic operations that occur in certain dialects but not in others. This paper provides an outline of how my triglossic upbringing laid the foundation for my current work in linguistic research and language teaching.

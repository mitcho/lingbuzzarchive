Question Bias as the Pragmatics of Context Update
Deniz Rudin
September 2025
 

Typically, utterances of declarative sentences make assertions, whereas utterances of interrogative sentences ask questions. One popular idea about how to explain this is that the two clause types have denotations of different types (e.g. propositions vs. sets of propositions), which interact with a univocal context-update mechanism in a way that derives asserting and questioning from that denotational difference. This is a satisfying explanatory approach, because it proposes that interpretation is driven compositionally by the form of the sentence, and it refrains from construction-specific stipulations about context update. But there are many systematic exceptions to this typical association: for instance, rising declaratives and tag questions comprise askings despite their declarative form, and do so while signaling distinct varieties of bias. These observations have typically forced sacrifices of the properties that make the above style of explanation explanatory: either the connections between form and interpretation are weakened, allowing declarative sentences to sometimes share denotations with interrogative ones, or a univocal update procedure is jettisoned, bringing in construction-specific context updates to account for these ‘marked’ forms. In this paper I argue that neither sacrifice is necessary. If we treat some aspects of linguistic form as contributing meanings on the level of context update, and take seriously the idea that people make pragmatic inferences about the speaker’s choice to update the context in the way that they did, we can bring these constructions into the fold of well-behaved context updates. Question bias emerges as a consequence of pragmatic reasoning about the speaker’s context-update choices, without needing to be stipulated construction-specifically.

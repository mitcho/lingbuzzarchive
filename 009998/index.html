Hallucination, Formal Reasoning, and the α-Gradient: A Linguistic Framework for AI Architecture
Tariq Mozahid
May 2026
 

This paper applies the gradient conventionalization framework developed in Mozahid (2025a, 2025b, 2026) to the architecture of artificial intelligence systems, arguing that the persistent divide between statistical and categorical AI is fundamentally a semantic phenomenon rather than an engineering one. The framework operationalizes meaning components through levels of intersubjective agreement (α), providing a principled account of why different AI architectures can process only certain ranges of the α-gradient. Categorical systems operate exclusively at α→1.0; large language models (LLMs) operate across α=0.30–0.95, most reliably in the α=0.70–0.90 zone of default entailment. α-level assignments are derived from corpus-distributional measures established independently in Mozahid (2025a), treating AI behavioral data as a convergent test rather than the source of those assignments — a methodological structure that resolves a potential circularity and transforms divergences between predicted and observed AI behavior into theoretically informative evidence. The architectural mapping yields four contributions: it reframes LLM hallucination as α-level misassignment, the structural mismatch between assertoric output form and probabilistic generative mechanism; it reframes neuro-symbolic hybrid systems as implicitly α-stratified architectures whose routing criterion should be α-level rather than domain or task type; it argues that formal systems are natural language driven to α→1.0 through definitional stipulation, connecting the framework to debates in the philosophy of mathematics; and it identifies α-elevation — conventionalization's upward pressure on intersubjective agreement — as a theoretical concept currently absent from AI architecture research but necessary for any principled account of how formal reasoning emerges from natural language processing.

Compounding, Allosemy, and Locality: The case for root-root structures
Catarina Soares
May 2022
 

In this paper I explore the issue of the locality conditions for conditioning idiosyncratic meaning in the domain of attributive compounds in English—these are compounds like 'snow globe' and 'tennis shoes', in which the nonhead of the compound modifies the interpretation of the head in an unpredictable but contextually defined way. While since Arad’s influential work (Arad 2003) much investigation has been dedicated to deriving these locality constraints (Anagnostopoulou and Samioti 2013; Marantz 2013; Harðarson 2016; Wood 2020a,b), only more recently have compounds been the focus of this type of research (see e.g. Harðarson 2018; Harðarson 2018; Ingason and Sigurðsson 2020; Nóbrega and Panagiotidis 2020). This paper contributes to both the literature on compounds and on the locality of idiosyncratic meaning by working out in depth a type of idiosyncratic meaning of subset of attributive compounds which to my knowledge has not been explored to any great extent in the literature on compounds, and which has been only briefly approached in the literature on locality (Embick 2016). Specifically, I argue that there is a subset of attributive compounds in which the meaning of a root in the compound is conditioned by another root in the compound. I show that under the widespread assumption that roots in English compounds are categorized, most mainstream conceptions of locality do not predict post-syntactic interactions between roots in attributive compounds. In order to account for the attested interactions, I propose that there are (at least) two possible structures for attributive compounds: one which merges words (i.e. categorized roots), and one which merges uncategorized roots directly (i.e. smaller objects than words).

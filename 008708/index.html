The grammar of gender: insights from Bantu agreement asymmetries with conjoined subjects
Vicki Carstens
February 2026
 

An exploration of agreement with conjoined DPs in Xhosa and Shona motivates several proposals regarding the nature and workings of grammatical gender: (i) gender-matching plural agreement with conjoined gender-uniform singulars diagnoses formally interpretable gender content in a gender, construed as association to a salient class of countable entities; (ii) absent this there is "default"/semantic agreement rather than matching of the visible gender; (iii) arbitrary and conceptually motivated members of a gender pattern alike because even the arbitrary bear minimally igender flavors. Most Xhosa nouns have stacked nP-structures [nP1 n1 [nP2 n2+root]]; n2 is always the bearer of a semantic gender which values so-called default agreement. The facts of Bantu languages with their large number of genders argue that gender-matching plural agreement with conjoined uniform singulars is the exception rather than the rule. This paper incorporates and reanalyzes material in my lingbuzz paper of 2019 along with newer stuff, and the 2026 version is substantially revised.

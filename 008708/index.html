The grammar of gender: insights from Bantu agreement asymmetries with conjoined subjects
Vicki Carstens
February 2025
 

An exploration of agreement with conjoined DPs in Xhosa and Shona, with comparison to Greek and BCS, motivates several proposals regarding the nature and workings of grammatical gender: (i) gender-matching plural agreement with conjoined gender-matched singulars diagnoses interpretable gender content in a gender; (ii) systematic failure of such agreement diagnoses wholly ugenders; (iii) agreement with conjoined singulars relies on resolution at &P (Adamson & Anagnostopolou 2024), but conjoined plurals motivate a different derivation featuring Multiple Agree (H.uPhi, &P, DP1, DP2) and impoverishment; (iv) in the grammars of agreement with both conjoined singulars and plurals, arbitrary members of genders with icontent pattern with semantically canonical members, arguing that different “flavors” of the same gender are indistinguishable until the application of LF licensing conditions (adapting Kramer 2015). Turning to Bantu-particular findings, three Xhosa genders have icontent: classes 1/2 [human], 7/8 [inanimate], and 9/10 [animal]. Most Xhosa nouns have stacked nP-structures with icores of 1/2, 7/8, or 9/10; adapting Adamson & Anagnostopolou (2024), ugenders 3/4 and 5/6 of [sg+sg] delete from &P in the resolution process, exposing the semantic icores which value so-called default agreement. This paper incorporates and reanalyzes material in my lingbuzz paper of 2019 along with newer stuff.

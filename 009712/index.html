Generalized non-de dicto:Underspecification and discourse stability
Clemens Mayr, Viola Schmitt
January 2026
 

Embedding material under intensional operators gives rise to a variety of `non-de dicto' construals: the proposition seemingly expressed by the embedded clause does not describe subject’s the attitudinal state, yet the sentence can be judged true. This paper makes three contributions: First, we strengthen the claims by Mayr & Schmitt 2024 that non-de dicto construals are a uniform phenomenon, which does not involve dedicated syntactic structures or morphology: The distinction between de dicto and non-de dicto construals does not involve ambiguity but, and this is our second point, arises from properties of meaning per se. What can be described as `replacement' -- in non-de dicto construals the meaning of a constituent of the embedded clause is replaced by another -- results from meanings being underspecified: any expression introduces a set of meanings and intensional operators existentially quantify over such sets.s the underspecification we assume is more radical as in underspecification accounts of other phenomena, we propose a new mechanism constraining it, roughly based on pragmatic considerations. Discourse participants aim for `discourse stability': the `traditional' meanings of expressions and their replacements must be indistinguishable for all the questions that might occur in the projected discourse.

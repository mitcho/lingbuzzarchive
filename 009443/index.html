Revisiting definiteness: Insights from two types of definite articles in Tihami Arabic
Amer Asiri, John Gluckman
October 2025
 

Recent studies have examined the semantic typology of definiteness, focusing on languages that have two types of definite articles in typologically unrelated languages (e.g., Schwarz 2009, 2013, 2019; Jenks 2015). Analyses of these languages suggest that one definite article encodes only uniqueness, while the other includes an anaphoric component. This study presents data from Tihami Arabic, which has two definite articles, im- and al-, challenging this conventional dichotomy of definiteness. Instead, the two definites in Tihami align with Löbner’s (1985, 2011) classification of uniqueness, which distinguishes between inherent and context-derived uniqueness. While al- occurs with nouns that are inherently unique, im- appears with nouns whose uniqueness is derived from the discourse. We formally capture this distinction using Šimík’s (2021) situation-based semantics. Our findings support the approaches of Löbner and Šimík, extending the cross-linguistic understanding of languages with two types of definite articles.

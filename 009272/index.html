Phonemic discovery in a polysynthetic language: distributional and corpus evidence for two additional vowels in Blackfoot
Natalie Weber
August 2025
 

Phonemic analysis is a cornerstone of linguistic description. Some phonemic contrasts in morphologically complex or polysynthetic languages may go undiscovered for years because of the scarcity of minimal pairs. In this paper I present a reanalysis of the Blackfoot (Algonquian) vowel and glide inventory, based on arguments of distribution (a) in static contexts, (b) across patterns of root alternation, and (c) within the most recent dictionary. I argue that the vowel inventory includes two additional long vowels, /ɛː/ and /ɔː/. Additionally, the labiovelar glide [w] that occurs at the left edge of some roots is epenthetic. This reanalysis has several synchronic and diachronic consequences for Blackfoot and phonological theory more broadly. This project shows that minimal pairs and phonetic similarity are not always the most reliable test for discovering phonemic contrast. Instead, the phonological and corpus methods shown could be adapted to other languages without many minimal pairs.

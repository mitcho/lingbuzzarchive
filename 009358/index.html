A parametric view on exclusive focus particles
Ka-Fai Yip
October 2025
 

D-quantification and A-quantification are two basic apparatuses of meaning in natural language. There has, however, been a recent attempt to reduce the former to the latter, especially for exclusive focus particles, where adfocal only is claimed to be a "concord" marker, whereas adverbial only functions as an exclusive operator (A-quantifier) (Quek & Hirsch 2017; Branan & Erlewine 2023, among others). This paper argues that such a view is too strong and cannot be upheld universally. Specifically, I argue that both D- and A-quantification are available in the semantic domain of exclusives. Crucial data are drawn from Vietnamese and Yorùbá, where adverbial and adfocal particles are lexicalized differently (chỉ and mỗi; kàn and nìkan, respectively) and may be doubled with the same focus association ("exclusive doubling"). I show that the choice between D- or A-quantification is parameterized: while Vietnamese adverbial chỉ is an exclusive operator, Yorùbá adfocal nìkan is an exclusive (D-)quantifier, with evidence from backward association, multiple association with focus, scopal interaction with negation, and wide scope under ellipsis. Furthermore, I demonstrate a four-way typology of exclusive doubling, in which exclusive semantics may be encoded on (i) adverbial particles (e.g., Vietnamese); (ii) adfocal particles (e.g., Yorùbá); (iii) neither of them but rather a null operator (e.g., Kasem; pace Aremu forthcoming); or (iv) both of them (e.g., Mandarin; pace Sun 2021). Finally, I discuss how this typology parallels variations in negative concord (Biberauer & Zeijlstra 2012).

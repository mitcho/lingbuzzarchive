Phrase-level Opacity as Shared Activity in Kere
Sören E. Tebay
March 2025
 

In Kere (Trans-New Guina, Papua-New Guinea) three phrasal phonological processes interact in an opaque counterfeeding pattern. This is unexpected under many approaches to phonology, which predict the absence of strictly phrase-level opacity. In this squib, I show that this pattern is expected if gradient symbolic representations are assumed and spreading leads to loss of gradient activity on tone-bearing units, which share the activity of the linked tone among themselves. This reduces markedness constraint violations and causes underapplication of interacting processes. The account predicts phrase-level opacity to always involve a process modeled as spreading.

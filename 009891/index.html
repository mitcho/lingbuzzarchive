Names and signs [Имя собственное и языковой знак]
Anton Zimmerling
April 2026
 

I discuss links between the principle of arbitrariness and the theory of proper names and claim that the arbitrariness of the sign is a generalized version of the referentialist thesis that proper names are conventional tags attached to their bearers. Saussurean linguistics extends this principle to all lexical items and all morphological expressions. Depending on naming practice, proper names can be stored in the respective lexicon, which corresponds to Gardiner’s model of disembodied names, or they can be created by the speaker, as in the model of primary embodied names and Kripke’s model of rigid designators. Neither of the scenarios violates the principle of arbitrariness. Naming conventions, information retrieved from the inner form of a proper name, and background knowledge about its previous bearers are not parts of the meaning. For linguistic description, it is desirable to accept the referentialist thesis that captures the contrast between proper names versus count names and treats proper names as semantically and syntactically deficient categories incapable of expressing natural properties and classifying individuals. This accounts for the inability of English personal names to combine with the articles in the primary, i. e., purely referential contexts. The competing predicativist thesis explores the idea that names are predicates in all occurrences and are construed as DPs in syntax. This approach fits the secondary contexts, where the reference to a particular individual is complemented by the reconstruction of properties characteristic of the class of individuals bearing homophonous names.

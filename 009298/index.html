Awareness of language and learning: Vietnamese immigrants navigate bilingualism in the new culture
Thi-Nham Le, Norbert Francis
September 2025
 

Participants in this study of language awareness, related to the relationship between speech and writing, provide self-report reflection on the learning of Chinese characters. This topic is the focus of a larger ongoing project focused on second language (L2) learners’ literacy attainment. Interviews with immigrant learners of Mandarin Chinese have suggested lines of research to pursue from a new vantage point, specifically on the question of access to phonological knowledge during the decoding of Chinese characters. How might this processing during reading vary in the case of Modern Standard Chinese Writing (MSCW) between, for example, Mandarin (informants’ target learner language, L2) and Cantonese (first language, L1)? Interviews revealed a consistent perception by learners of a closer “alignment,” for learning purposes, between phonology and script for MSCW and Mandarin Chinese than for the latter, MSCW and Cantonese. On a related note, regarding phonological processing, participants commented on the productive use of Vietnamese pinyin for learning the Chinese characters. These aspects of metalinguistic awareness appear to be related to extensive early experience with bilingual learning, language and literacy, in the home country (Vietnam), a separate but interesting research question. Regarding this question, the overall language acquisition and learning context of participants is relevant to the ongoing debate on the, by hypothesis, influence—via transfer—of prior L1 knowledge on L2 learning (V. 25, I. 3-4, pp. 255-275).

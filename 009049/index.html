Interrogative Questions in Tagalog: Pseudo-Clefts or Wh-Movement?
Joseph Sabbagh
May 2025
 

Virtually all wok on the syntax of interrogative (Wh-)questions in Tagalog assume that such questions are formed in the language are structured as Pseudo-clefts. In this article, I take a critical look at the empirical and theoretical arguments that have been cited favoring this analysis, and ultimately argue that Wh-questions are best analyzed as being derived by Wh- movement. The argumentation centers on three primarily empirical domains: (i) complementarity between the nominative marker and a [+wh] complementizer; (ii) the distribution of second position pronouns and modals; and (iii) a process of obligatory pied-piping. If correct, these arguments weaken the claim that Pseudo-clefts are typologically quite common (if not ubiquitous) among Austronesian languages (see, e.g., Polinsky & Potsdam to appear). In addition, they raise the issue of whether any language might exclusively employ a Pseudo-Cleft structure to form its interrogatives.

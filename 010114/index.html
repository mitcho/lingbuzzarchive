Cross-Linguistic Frame Compatibility
Joseph Budinger
July 2026
 

Second language transfer is often described as the carryover of L1 grammar, but persistent idiom transfer, fossilization, and striking asymmetries between learner populations suggest that the transferred unit is neither a rule nor a word. This paper extends Frame Class theory to cross-linguistic acquisition by treating frames—event-anchored sentence patterns with fixed material, variable slots, and membership in frame classes—as the units speakers carry across languages. It proposes frame compatibility: an L1 frame can be redeployed with L2 lexical material when the target community has conventionalized a structurally compatible frame for the same event; where no compatible frame exists, overlay produces output that may be lexically transparent or formally possible but is not recovered as the target communicative act. The argument is developed through the overlay test, East Asian relation-mattering frames, L1-idiom-in-L2 production, fossilization, and the Korean-Japanese acquisition advantage. The paper further distinguishes early overlay success from genuine L2 acquisition, arguing that acquisition is triggered where overlay fails and that discourse-level fluency emerges only after the L2 frame inventory reaches sufficient density. The result is a testable account of transfer and L2 difficulty in which acquisition cost is predicted by frame-inventory overlap rather than by grammar-level distance alone.

Restrictions on fronting and stress shift in the encoding of Czech focus
Anna Staňková, Radek Simik, Daniela Kořánová, Tomáš Bořil
October 2025
 

This article examines two operations which are used to encode focus in Czech: word order alternation and stress shift. Based on data from a previous production experiment, we ran a rating study where participants rated the authenticity of utterances with manipulated information structure. The results showed that alternating word order to encode focus is free, supporting the claims about the flexibility of Czech word order. Shifting stress is a more costly operation -- it is possible (in terms of competence), but it is not necessarily easy (in terms of performance). Moreover, applying a word order alternation and stress shift at the same time (in object fronting cases) leads to a significant decrease in rating, which cannot be accounted for by a mere cumulative effect of noncanonical word order and noncanonical stress. We argue instead that the noncanonical structure is blocked by a more economical one, where the same information structure is conveyed with the default structure and prosodic pattern.

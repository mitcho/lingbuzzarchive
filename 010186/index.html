Proposals, rejections, and Common Ground management
Linmin Zhang
July 2026
 

This paper connects two seemingly unrelated phenomena discussed in the literature and offers a unified account: (i) Why must modal adverbs (e.g., English "probably") be positive and behave like Positive Polarity Items (PPIs) (see Bellert 1977, Nilsen 2004, Piñon 2009, Wolf 2015)? (ii) Why do corrective constructions (e.g., Chinese "bìng bù", English "not p but q") obligatorily involve propositional negation (see Toosarvandani 2014, Yuan and Liu 2025, Wu 2025, Zhang 2025)? I argue that modal adverbs and corrective constructions are speaker-oriented, Common Ground (CG)-management operators, signaling two speech acts distinct from assertions: proposals and rejections. (i) Based on a proposition p’s being sufficiently likely in speaker S’s epistemic worlds, S uses a modal adverb to propose p, supporting (i.e., expressing a bias toward), but not requiring, its being considered for inclusion in the CG. (ii) Speaker S uses a corrective construction to reject (i.e., negate) a proposition p, thereby requiring its exclusion from the CG. The speaker’s support (bias) and requirement can be modeled as existential and universal modality.

Deixis, entropy, and the architecture of grammar
Txuss Martin
March 2026
 

We propose a principled account of deixis as a structural property of grammar that is independently required for the constitution of propositional meaning and whose systematic encoding becomes indispensable under the constraints imposed by externalization. Building on an Un-Cartesian model, we assume that grammar operates over intensional representations derived from world-indexed states. Once such representations are externalized into a temporally unfolding signal, encoding under constrained resources does not preserve all aspects of the original representation, yielding irreversibility in the sense of Shannon’s information theory. As a consequence, linguistic signals underdetermine their interpretation, giving rise to a recovery problem. We argue that this problem specifically concerns the anchoring of content to a context of use, and that, since this is precisely the dimension lost in encoding, recovery requires reintroducing these parameters, which grammar encodes through deictic features such as person, time, and location. On this view, deixis is not a peripheral class of expressions but a fundamental component of grammar that both enables propositional content and ensures its recoverability under externalization.

Classifiers and Plurality in Languages of the Americas
Carol Rose Little, Virginia Dawson, Matthew Chandler
December 2025
 

In this paper, we discuss three languages of the Americas--Ch'ol (Mayan, Mexico), Mi'gmaq (Algonquian, Canada) and Maihiki (Tukanoan, Peru)--in light of their classifier and plural marking systems. Crucially, all three languages allow the co-occurrence of classifiers and plural marking. While Ch'ol and Mi'gmaq have been discussed before in the theoretical literature on classifiers (Bale and Coon 2014, Bale et al. 2019, Little et al. 2022), as prime examples of classifiers-for-numerals languages, Maihiki has not received much attention. For Ch'ol and Mi'gmaq, we contextualize our discussion not just on classifiers, as previous works have, but also on the existence of plural marking, adding in new data from Ch'ol on plurals. In Maihiki, we present a different type of classifier system from those of Ch'ol and Mi'gmaq---one in which classifiers modify nouns---but see again that classifiers and plurals co-occur. Together, the co-occurrence of classifiers and plurality in these three different systems suggest an even more complex empirical landscape than previously thought.

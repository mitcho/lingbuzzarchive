Lean on QUD when you're not strong: A squiggle-based approach to "must"
Jonathan Pesetsky
September 2022
 

This paper shows that the classic arguments for a strong “must” can be replicated with other expressions such as “I guess P” and “I suppose P”. In these cases, the embedding predicates couldn’t be lexically strong since they also have weak uses, and even a compositional account would struggle to make sense of the grammatical and contextual factors that influence the choice of reading. Thus, regardless of what we say about “must”, these predicates would demand pragmatic principles allowing weak operators to act as strong. I propose one such account in which pragmatic strength is one of two strategies for bridging gaps in QUD congruence, and show that it applies to the case of "must" as well.

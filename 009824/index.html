Ellipsis in contact: VPE and sluicing in Spanish heritage speakers
Erin Mauffray, Victoria Mateu, Rodrigo Ranero
February 2026
 

This study investigates the evaluation of verb phrase ellipsis (VPE) and sluicing by English-dominant Spanish heritage speakers (HSs). Spanish permits VPE only with modal remnants (ModVPE), while English allows both ModVPE and auxiliary-stranding VPE (AuxVPE). Sluicing, in contrast, behaves similarly in both languages inasmuch as it is constrained by voice-matching requirements stemming from the universal identity condition on ellipsis. We thus test whether HSs’ difficulties with silent elements (“the Silent Problem”) are observed across structures, or whether they are particularly notable when transfer from the dominant language is available (i.e., AuxVPE in English transferring into Spanish). In a written/aural acceptability judgment task, HSs (n = 39) and Spanish-dominant controls (n = 33) rated expected grammatical (ModVPE and Voice Match Sluicing) and ungrammatical (AuxVPE and Voice Mismatch Sluicing) sentences. Both groups rejected voice-mismatched sluicing equally. However, HSs penalized AuxVPE less than the baseline group, suggesting English transfer. The results support the view that HSs exhibit reduced inhibition of the dominant language which may lead to the [E]-feature that licenses ellipsis in English AuxVPE to ‘seep’ into Spanish. We suggest that constraints on silence that are universally observed are maintained more robustly in HSs’ grammars whereas aspects that we expect to vary a priori are susceptible to divergence in such grammars.

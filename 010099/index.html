Large-Scale Data Collection in Fieldwork Contexts: A Case Study of Clause Embedding in Zulu
Leon Zong, Mark Kimani, Jane Loney, Audrey Stenger, Aaron Steven White, Asia Pietraszko
August 2026
 

Some research questions require large-scale acceptability judgment datasets to answer them. Constructing such datasets can be challenging when the researcher has limited familiarity with the language. We investigate how to optimally design acceptability judgment collection protocols in such contexts. Taking clause embedding in Zulu as a case study, we collect acceptability judgments using protocols that manipulate a combination of setting, modality, prior exposure, and question format and measure how much data collected using these different protocols converges in the acceptability it implies. We find that unguided surveys can recover the same acceptabilities as elicitation, but need roughly three times as much data—up to about ten times at the highest precision—to match it. We attribute this cost to protocol-induced variability: elicitation, and group elicitation in particular, buys the most acceptability per judgment.

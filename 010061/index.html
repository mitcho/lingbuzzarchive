Unconditional Questions: Semantically Legitimate, Pragmatically Degraded
Yurie Hara
June 2026
 

Unconditional questions such as ``Whether or not the party is at Emma's place, will it be fun?'' are often judged degraded, despite the acceptability of their declarative counterparts. This paper argues that their degradation is pragmatic rather than semantic. Building on inquisitive semantics and stack-based suppositional semantics, I develop a compositional account in which unconditional questions are interpreted relative to multiple hypothetical contexts corresponding to antecedent alternatives. I then propose two pragmatic constraints on their use. First, extending Theiler's (2021) Epistemic Bridge, I argue that every antecedent alternative must sustain an inquisitive issue with respect to the consequent question. Second, when the antecedent and consequent issues are independent, unconditional questions are semantically equivalent to ordinary questions. Their use therefore invites the inference that the antecedent issue is irrelevant to resolving the consequent question, a form of relational indifference in the sense of Rawlins (2008, 2013). The analysis explains why unconditional questions improve when the antecedent issue is salient but degrade when one antecedent alternative effectively settles the consequent issue. More generally, the results provide additional support for a suppositional approach to conditionals.

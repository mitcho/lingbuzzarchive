Beyond Subjects: Topic Situations, Multiple Agree, and the Diachrony of Switch-Reference Morphology
Enrico Higginbotham
May 2025
 

This thesis proposes a parametric, Multiple-Agree analysis to account for cross-linguistic variation in the canonical and non-canonical manifestations of switch-reference morphology, with an emphasis on diachronic evolution within Uto-Aztecan languages. Switch reference traditionally tracks subject identity across clauses; however, instances of non-canonical usage also extend to situational shifts. Utilizing empirical data from Hopi (canonical), Serrano (non-canonical), and Tohono O'odham (exhibiting remnants of Uto-Aztecan switch-reference morphology), this analysis argues that differences arise from a parameter determining whether the switch-reference probe copies the indices of subjects or topic-situation pronouns. This approach explains observed variation and contributes to deriving diachronic paths, such as those seen in Tohono O’odham, where switch-reference markers evolved into coordinating conjunctions due to Feature Economy and language-specific morphosyntactic conditions. By unifying canonical and non-canonical switch-reference phenomena within a coherent syntactic framework, this work highlights the explanatory power of parametric models in both synchronic and diachronic linguistics.

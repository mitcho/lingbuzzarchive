The or That? Evaluating language models’ sensitivity to discourse structure in anaphora
Jennifer Hu, Ankana Saha, Kathryn Davidson
September 2025
 

We test whether language models (LMs) exhibit sensitivity to fine-grained discourse features that influence referential form choice in human language. Using a controlled paradigm from prior behavioral studies, we evaluate 19 LMs on their ability to distinguish between demonstratives (e.g., that) and definites (e.g., the) when referring back to previously mentioned entities, using expert-created stimuli across four languages (English, German, Turkish, Mandarin). In human data, demonstratives show selective licensing effects based on the presence or absence of contrastive noun phrases and discourse continuity. We find that only larger models replicate these effects reliably, with stronger correspondence to human judgments and higher item-level correlation. These results suggest that pragmatic constraints on reference are increasingly achievable at scale, and they introduce a new cross-linguistic testbed for probing the pragmatic competence of LMs.

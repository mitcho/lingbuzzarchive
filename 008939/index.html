Unifying compound specific phonological effects in Malayalam - a case for moraic affixation
Katie McCann, Jochen Trommer
April 2025
 

This article tackles the morpho-phonological complexities of compoundspecific phonological effects in Malayalam. We argue that all effects of lengthening and their interaction with epenthesis underlie a single source, namely a linking element realised as a moraic affix. The realisation of this moraic affix is sensitive to lexical, morphosyntactic structural and phonological factors. Despite the non-phonological influences on the moraic affix’s realisation, we contend that the phonology neither needs to reference lexical information nor is it necessary to invoke non-restrictive interleaving of morphology and phonology to account for the structural influence. Instead, three assumptions determine the distinct surface patterns of the moraic affix. First, the linking element has two lexically conditioned allomorphs which are both realised by a floating mora but vary in their underlying strength, modeled as gradient symbolic representations. Second, lengthening is sensitive to prosodic structure, which differs between compound types due to morphosyntactic structural differences. Third, general phonological well-formedness interacts with how the floating mora is realised. These three assumptions allow all configurations of lengthening and insertion at the compound boundary to be unified by a single instance of a linking element.

Bare vs. Demonstrative Anaphoric Definites in Korean and Their Cross-Linguistic Implications
Min-Joo Kim, EunHee Lee
August 2023
 

This paper aims to provide a new perspective on the typology of anaphoric definiteness marking by presenting data from Korean (and Japanese). English collapses unique/weak definites and anaphoric/familiar/strong definiteness by using the same definite article the. In other languages, the two types of definites may be morphosyntactically distinguished by using strong definite articles or demonstratives (Dems) (e.g., German, Lakhota, Hausa, Icelandic) (Schwarz 2013). Recent works on article-less languages have expanded the study of anaphoric definiteness. The prevailing view is that, apart from occurring as unique/weak definites, bare nouns can occur in all anaphoric/familiar/strong definite environments. However, consensus is yet to be reached as to exactly when anaphoric bare nouns may be used as opposed to Dem-NPs. In this paper, we show that the choice between an anaphoric bare noun and a Dem-NP in article-less languages is not a matter of preference, contra authors like Ahn (2019), Park (2022), Simpson and Wu (2022), and Dayal and Jian (2023). We suggest that, typically, bare nouns indicate what Kim (2021b) calls situation-internal viewpoint, and Dems indicate what she calls situation-external viewpoint, but we propose more refined licensing conditions for anaphoric bare nouns and Dem-NPs than Kim does (compare Kim 2023). In addition, we offer formal semantic treatments of anaphoric bare nouns and Dem-NPs in Korean, with implications for other (bare noun) languages including Mandarin and Japanese.

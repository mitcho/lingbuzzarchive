Form-meaning mismatches in Nanosyntax
Karen De Clercq
November 2023
 

This study reflects on my work between 2013 (PhD) and 2023 through the lens of form-meaning mismatches. It discusses cases of syncretism and fusion, doubling and multiple exponence, as well as zero-to-one and one-to-zero mismatches, drawing on data from degree morphology, negation and derivational morphology. I show how two central tools of Nanosyntax (Starke 2009, Caha 2009), namely decomposition into syntactico-semantic features and phrasal lexicalisation, allow to account for these phenomena. The study argues that form-meaning mismatches do not require exceptional mechanisms, but follow naturally from the way hierarchically organised feature structures are packaged and stored as lexical items. The same architecture underlies cases in which form and meaning appear to correspond one-to-one. Form-meaning mismatches are nevertheless particularly revealing, as they provide a clear window onto the underlying structure of language.

Another View of Access by Chomsky (2024): An Analysis of Movement Constraint about Subject
Norimasa Hayashi-Katsu
October 2025
 

This paper investigates restrictions on subject movement within Chomsky's (2024) MC framework, which replaces successive-cyclic movement with an interpretive operation, Access. We decompose Chomsky's Access into two parts, proposing a phase-level operation, Mark, which determines which syntactic objects are available to the interpretive systems. We argue that restrictions on subject movement are derived from the interaction of Mark with theta-domains, assuming that the CP phase delimits the domain of Mark. Based on this idea, we analyze the that-trace effect, subject island constraints, and the movement of subjects in passives, raising predicates, and copular constructions. We further extend the framework to account for island and the that-t effects in multiple wh interrogatives. The analysis suggests that movement restrictions fall into two categories: syntactic constraints on Mark, and constraints that block reconstruction across islands. Comparing our proposal with Chomsky's (2024) and Kitahara's (2024) frameworks, we argue that the accessibility for Access is detemined by whether a syntactic object is located in a theta-domain, thereby offering a novel perspective on the division between predicate and discourse structures in human language.

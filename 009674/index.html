An Antisymmetric Syntax of Word Order in Farsi: The Predicate Domain
Komeil Ahari
December 2025
 

This dissertation closely reexamines the structure of the predicate domain in Farsi/Persian and evaluates whether its surface OV order is best explained by the widely adopted head-final VP/vP analyses or by an alternative framework that universally derives all linear orders from the same base structure. In favor of the latter approach, I argue that the traditional head-final verb phrase accounts face significant theoretical issues highlighted by three empirical domains: (i) complex predicates and their interaction with auxiliary verbs and phenomena such as ellipsis and verb-preposing, (ii) postverbal clausal complements and their subextraction, binding, and ellipsis patterns, and (iii) the licensing of post-nominal arguments and modifiers, particularly within the ezâfe construction. Crucially, such analyses cannot straightforwardly account for the core data without relying on additional assumptions, such as inconsistent directionality in head-adjunction, reliance on head-excorporation out of complex heads, and head-directionality parametrization within the VP depending on the (non)-clausal status of the verb’s argument. Alternatively, an analysis within the Antisymmetry framework of Kayne (1994) is developed that captures the full range of ordering facts with the single, consistent derivational mechanism of leftward movement, eliminates rightward adjunction and excorporation, and unifies the treatment of predicate-argument and predicate-modifier configurations. A main claim of this dissertation is that Farsi complex predicates (combinations of Non-Verbal elements (NV) and Light-Verbs (LV)) and larger verb clusters in general form phrasal XP constituents derived from a head-initial structure and various leftward movement operations. In the current analysis, such movements are defined as the raising of predicate-internal elements from their base position to dedicated licensing functional projections to the left. Focusing on complex predicate constructions and their interaction with auxiliary verbs, I propose that not only the internal and external arguments, but also the Non-Verbal (NV) predicates of complex verbs undergo movement. This analysis systematically accounts for Auxilary-Verb ordering facts and the leftmost position of NV elements in any verbal cluster. It also straightforwardly deals with verb cluster movement in the context of data from verb-stranding vP ellipsis and Topic/Focus fronting by treating such cases as the movement of derived verbal phrases to the relevant left-peripheral specifier positions. Further evidence in favor of the developed antisymmetric analysis comes from clausal arguments of verbs, which always surface postverbally in contrast to non-clausal arguments, which precede the verb. Previous head-final vP analyses require either exceptional rightward selection or rightward extraposition of CPs, both of which are inadequate in the context of complex predicates and auxiliary constructions. I propose that in such constructions the same leftward movements licensing the NV predicate as well as the DPs and PPs still apply. However, the crucial claim is that CP arguments remain in their base position and are not subject to the same licensing requirements above the predicate. Since the base-generated position of CP arguments is always hierarchically lower than the projections of the verb and the auxiliary head, it follows that they are always in a final position in the clause independent of whether the verb is simplex, complex, or partof a larger verbal cluster. Consequently, this proposal correctly accounts for the subextraction, binding, and ellipsis facts that are indicative of the low interpretation site of the CP. A final puzzle for the traditional accounts is raised by post-predicate constituents in the non-verbal domain of copular and complex predicate constructions, where internal arguments or modifiers appear to the right of the predicate, often linked by the ezâfe morpheme. Based on a close investigation of the relevant patterns, I adopt and extend Kahnemuyipour’s (2014) roll-up phrasal movement analysis, originally proposed for postnominal dependents in the NP/DP domain, to also cover predicate–modifier and predicate–argument orders in the verbal domain. Under this proposal, both modifiers and arguments are base-generated in specifiers of functional projections above the predicate head (whether nominal, adjectival, or prepositional). Roll-up movement of the predicate (or a projection containing it) across these specifiers derives the observed [Predicate –Ezf– XP] orders, while the ezâfe morpheme spells out the agreement relation established in this process. Crucially, I claim that this derivation represents a licensing operation available to arguments and modifiers in certain constructions and is further evidence that elements must be functionally licensed within the antisymmetric framework pursued in the dissertation.

Agreement variation and optionality in Spanish existential clauses
John David Storment
August 2025
 

This paper offers a novel framework for understanding agreement variation and optionality in Spanish existential clauses involving the verb haber. In these existential sentences, though the prescriptive norm is for verbal agreement to always surface as third person singular, a significant number of speakers allow for conditional agreement with the postverbal DP associate/pivot (Rodríguez Mondoñedo 2006). I show that, for these speakers, verbal agreement may optionally occur with a third person plural associate, and is obligatory with a first or second person plural associate. These agreement relations are established via a mechanism known as defective circumvention (Storment 2025), in which a phi-probe may conditionally search past a goal that it has already agreed with and go on to agree with a second goal, given that the first goal does not bear all of the features that the probe is specified to search for. Additionally, using data from nonstandard Spanish varieites, I explain some morphosyntactic irregularities of the existential verb haber and how they follow the analysis of existential clauses given in the paper. Finally, this paper offers a new way of thinking about agreement optionality and intraspeaker variation in which multiple valid outputs are derived within a single grammar.

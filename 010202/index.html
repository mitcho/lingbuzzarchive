Sociolinguistic Dimensions of Digital Discourse: A Comparative Study of Male and Female Responses on Crimes Against Women
Noran Noran Samy Gamal Khedr
April 2026
 

Social media has become a significant space for public discourse, allowing individuals to express opinions and attitudes toward sensitive social issues, including gender-based violence against women. This study investigates gendered linguistic differences in online discourse by examining male and female comments addressing gender-based violence on X (Twitter) and Instagram. The study analyzes a dataset of 30 comments, equally divided between male and female users, collected from four social media posts. A triangulation mixed-methods approach is adopted, combining qualitative and quantitative analysis to identify recurring linguistic patterns. The analysis draws on Lakoff’s (1975) Deficit Model, Tannen’s (1990) Difference Model, and Zimmerman and West’s (1975) Dominance Model, alongside an examination of morphological and syntactic features. The findings reveal both similarities and differences between male and female discourse. Female comments demonstrate a higher frequency of features associated with Lakoff’s Deficit Model, particularly empty adjectives, hedges, and hyper-politeness, while male comments show greater use of intensifiers and emphatic punctuation. In accordance with Tannen’s Difference Model, male discourse is more frequently associated with orders, conflict, and status-oriented language, whereas female discourse emphasizes feelings, understanding, intimacy, and support. Morphologically and syntactically, both groups display considerable similarities, particularly in tense marking, derivation, compounding, indicative mood, finite clauses, main clauses, and active voice. The findings suggest that gendered linguistic patterns persist in digital discourse while also demonstrating considerable overlap between male and female language use.

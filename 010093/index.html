Left branch extraction in German fragment answers: Evidence for an in-situ approach
Miriam L. Schiele
June 2026
 

This paper investigates the role of contrastivity in Left Branch Extraction (LBE) under ellipsis, presenting experimental evidence from German to support an in-situ approach to clausal ellipsis. While prior research suggests that ellipsis can mitigate island violations, particularly in non-contrastive contexts (Merchant, 2001, 2004), recent proposals argue that ellipsis sites may be syntactically anisomorphic to their antecedents, allowing for alternative derivations that circumvent island constraints (Barros, 2014; Griffiths, 2019). However, these claims largely rely on informally collected acceptability judgments, leaving their empirical robustness in question. To address this gap, controlled acceptability judgment experiments are conducted to test whether contrastivity modulates the acceptability of remnants extracted out of islands. The findings consistently show that contrastively focused remnants force an isomorphic interpretation, leading to significant degradation when the extraction of the remnant violates syntactic constraints. In contrast, anisomorphic readings can be available as a last-resort strategy to avoid island violations. Moreover, a clear difference in acceptability is observed between wh-remnants and non-wh remnants, indicating that the remnant’s ability to move determines its island sensitivity. These results underscore the necessity of an in-situ approach to clausal ellipsis and provide evidence against a general island repair effect in LBE contexts. This paper argues that (i) ellipsis can occur ‘around’ a designated constituent, (ii) syntactic islands are not repaired by ellipsis, (iii) the ellip sis site need not be syntactically isomorphic to its antecedent if independent conditions are met, (iv) remnants A′-move in elliptical configurations only if they would A′-move if ellipsis had not applied, and (v) contrastive focus on the remnant forces an isomorphic interpretation of the elliptical clause’s syntax, without enforcing any restrictions on presentational focus.

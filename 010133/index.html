Exploring AAB patterns in degree morphology: A DM–Nanosyntax comparison
Jonathan David Bobaljik, Pavel Caha
July 2026
 

The paper investigates stem morphology in triplets like bad–worse–to worsen (positive – comparative – deadjectival degree-achievement verb). It focuses on Czech, Ukrainian, Bulgarian and Turkish, and complements a previous typologi- cal study by Bobaljik (2012), which focused on root suppletion within this triplet of forms. The study replicates the main result of Bobaljik (2012) for stems, which is that ABA patterns are virtually unattested, confirming the idea that there is a containment relation among the three forms. The study also finds a rela- tive wealth of AAB patterns, which are absent in root suppletion. The empirical record further suggests that AAB patterns correlate with a particular type of marking in the comparative, where the comparative marker is both a separate word and a pre-marker (it precedes the adjective). We argue that the theories of Distributed Morphology and Nanosyntax each naturally rely on a different facet of this data. In DM, the correlation between AAB patterns and the type of comparative marking is best explained using the notion of an independent word (where wordhood defines a locality domain, as in Bobaljik 2012). On the other hand, Nanosyntax naturally relies on the complementary characteristic, namely the pre-marking nature of the comparative (relying on the idea that pre-marking leads to the opening of a separate derivational workspace, see De Clercq and Vanden Wyngaerd 2018). We thus identify an empirical domain where future research may corroborate one or the other approach by identifying data that would differentiate between the two properties as the relevant correlate for the newly discovered AAB patterns.

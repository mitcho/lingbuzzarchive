Adding to the expressive typology: Emblematic gestures as visual expressives
Sebastian Walter
May 2026
 

This paper investigates whether emblematic gestures should be analyzed as expressive meaning contributions in the visual modality of spoken language. While formal-semantic work on gesture has primarily focused on iconic co-speech gestures, emblematic gestures have received comparatively little attention, despite their high degree of conventionalization and their ability to express speaker attitudes and entire communicative acts. Building on Cornelia Ebert and Walter (2025), we first argue that emblematic gestures, unlike iconic gestures in general, satisfy the central diagnostics for expressive meaning proposed by Potts (2007): they are nondisplaceable, perspectivedependent, descriptively ineffable, immediate, and repeatable, while their independence from truth-conditional content is sensitive to alignment and differs across individual emblems. We then show that emblematic gestures do not form a homogeneous semantic class. Some emblems, such as THUMBS UP, behave like isolated expletive use-conditional items: they can occur independently of speech, do not require an argument from truth-conditional content, and allow comparatively flexible shifted interpretations. Other emblems, such as CRAZY, pattern with functional expletive use-conditional items: they are compositionally integrated, require an argument, and strongly favor speaker-oriented interpretations. We propose a formal analysis in LTU that captures this distinction and argue that emblematic gestures provide evidence that expressivity is not restricted to the spoken modality of spoken languages.

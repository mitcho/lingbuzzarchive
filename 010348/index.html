Puzzles in pronominal structure from Nord-Jæren
Erlend Øyvindssønn Ravnanger, Pritty Patel-Grosz, Patrick Georg Grosz
September 2026
 

This paper focuses on pronouns in the Norwegian dialects spoken in the Nord-Jæren region, a geographic area in Rogaland county on the Norwegian West Coast. We present a data set focusing on three variants of the 3rd person feminine singular object pronoun ("her"): hu, 'na and henna. We raise the question how these three variants fit into pronominal typologies such as Cardinaletti & Starke's (1996, 1999) classification of pronouns as clitic, weak and strong. We present a survey where participants (n=822) were asked to choose which of the three pronouns fits best in a given sentence. The survey results indicate that 'na is the unmarked form, whereas hu and henna are preferred in a subset of contexts, such as the topic position and the complement position of a copula verb. The overall data are indicative of a markedness hierarchy (from least marked to most marked): 'na < hu < henna. Within this markedness hierarchy, both hu and henna pattern as strong pronouns in the sense of Cardinaletti & Starke (1996, 1999), whereas 'na qualifies as a weak pronoun. These generalizations lay the groundwork for further exploration into the morphosyntactic properties of the Nord-Jæren dialects, which bears on the question of how pronominal systems are organized cross-linguistically, particularly whether languages can have more than one pronominal paradigm that classifies as strong.

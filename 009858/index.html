Patterns in the phonology of Mandarin Chinese: basic color terms and beyond
Daniel Hole, Titus von der Malsburg
March 2026
 

This paper investigates non-arbitrary phonological patterns in the Mandarin Chinese lexicon, focusing on basic color terms and common adjectival antonyms. An analysis of the six primary basic color terms reveals systematic onset correspondences. Specifically, the terms for non-colors (*bái* ‘white’, *hēi* ‘black’) exhibit maximal contrast in place and manner of articulation. In contrast, warm colors (*huáng* ‘yellow’, *hóng* ‘red’) and cold colors (*lán* ‘blue’, *lǜ* ‘green’) consistently share the onsets /h/ and /l/, respectively. Computational simulations resampling the Mandarin syllable inventory demonstrate that the simultaneous occurrence of these phonological distributions by chance is highly unlikely to be by chance (p < 0.0001). An exploratory assessment of common adjectival antonym pairs further indicates a restricted use of places of articulation, predominantly drawing from alveolar, alveolo-palatal, and retroflex consonants while systematically excluding front and back sounds. These findings provide quantitative evidence for an abstract form of ideophony in Mandarin, where phonological form systematically reflects semantic class membership and sensory contrasts.

Person hierarchy effects from Φ-agreement at the left periphery in Kawahíva
Wesley dos Santos
August 2025
 

In general, person hierarchy effects result from a probe agreeing with multiple goals, typically in the low domain of the clause (Béjar 2003, Béjar and Rezac 2009:a.m.o). However, in the Tupí-Guaraní language Kawahíva, spoken in the Brazilian Amazon, the person hierarchy 1>2>3 manifests only when the verb moves to CP, overtly or covertly. Otherwise, agreement is restricted to the internal argument. To account for this pattern, I propose Kawahíva matrix clauses come with two probes along the clause spine, as displayed in (2): a probe relativized to [SPKR-PART-Φ] sits on C, and a flat Φ-probe, is on v. This analysis is used to explain i) the lack of person hierarchy effects when the verb does not move to the CP in matrix clauses; ii) the absence of subject agreement and person hierarchy effects in dependent clauses; and iii) an asymmetry between subject and object agreement markers in verbal reduplication. This proposal extends other accounts for multiple loci for Φ-agreement in the clausal spine (Coon 2013, Oxford 2015, Compton 2018).

Linguistic and Pictorial Representations of Tunisia to Tourists and Visitors A Case Study of the Grammatical Gender that is Assigned to Tunisian Tourist Sites in Tourism Advertising Materia
Massoud Saidi
April 2020
 

The aim of this study is to investigate the linguistic and pictorial representations of Tunisia in Tunisian tourist brochures written in French and English. It attempts to demystify and analyse the grammatical gender features and structures existing in both French and English brochures and see whether they portray Tunisia as a female gender. In addition, conducting a semiotic analysis and investigation aimed at unveiling the hidden semiotic signs in the corpus data showing the female representation of Tunisia. Specifically, this study aims to analyse the noun phrases, determiners, adjectives and verbs (in French and English) along with ‘reading’ the colours, shapes and signs utilized in the images to prove that Tunisia is depicted as a female gender. Furthermore, using a Multimodal discourse analysis showed how the verbal and visual aspects of the advertising message complement each other and shape the tourists’ mental perception of Tunisia’s gender. A strong theoretical framework endorsed and supported the current study’s hypothesis announcing that the governmental and private designed brochures referred to Tunisia as female gender. Indeed, adverting discourse, language and gender and visual social semiotic theories helped in executing the present research. Finally, the current study presents an extension to the advertising discourse language in Tunisia. Keywords: linguistic, semiotic, gender, representations, multimodal discourse, brochures, visual social semiotic, advertising discourse.

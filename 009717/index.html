MORPHIX: Feature Geometry, Vocabulary Insertion, and the Particle Stratification of PIE Pronominal and Adverbial Morphology
MAKS PANNER
January 2026
 

The Morphix manuscript argue that PIE 'pronominal roots' form a distinct morphological species: closed-class, non-derivable, non-compounding, non-ablauting atoms originating in prePIE deictic particles. The model represents these atoms as feature-bundle realizations organized by a geometry whose primary nodes DEIXIS, ANAPH, and Q partition the functional space; optional INDEF and ALTERITY extend the system to indefinite and 'other' series. Vocabulary insertion under the Subset Principle selects the most specific exponent compatible with the syntactic feature bundle, generating syncretisms and blocking effects. Empirically, the manuscript compiles daughter-language reflex lists across major branches and derive adverbial paradigms by composing Morphix exponents with a small suffix system (-r/-d/-m/-o), as well as specific diachronic pathways that connect PIE distal to- to the English th- series. This article integrates the manuscript into a single journal-style treatment, adds explicit comparative tables, diagrams, and falsifiable diagnostics, and frames a research program: cross-corpus assays of particle+suffix compositionality, typological channel hierarchies, and computational reconstruction pipelines grounded in the Morphix geometry. Novelty upgrades in this revision include (i) an executed pilot Morphix+suffix assay on Old English and Vedic/Sanskrit samples with quantitative coverage and cell-recovery metrics (§7.1), (ii) a minimal cost-based model comparison against a root-list baseline (§6.9), and (iii) explicit typological, learnability, and computational evaluation protocols that make the geometry falsifiable (§8–§9)

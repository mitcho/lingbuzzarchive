Agreement by proxy
Norvin Richards
September 2026
 

Proposes some new uses for an old idea (Butt (1993, 1995), Bhatt (2005), Legate (2005), Baker and Willie (2010)): that if X Agrees with Y and Y Agrees with Z, X should be taken for all syntactic and morphological purposes to be in an Agree relation with Z. I refer to the relation between X and Z as 'agreement by proxy', and argue that recognizing agreement by proxy allows for very simple statements on the conditions on locality of Agree. The resulting theory derives a version of the ban on improper movement and on A-movement out of CPs, and predicts correctly that these bans can be circumvented under the right circumstances. It also derives a version of the notion of 'phase unlocking', and accounts for some facts about the interaction of long-distance movement with agreement in Passamaquoddy-Wolastoqey (Algonquian).

Language, Structure, and Medical Knowledge: Why Homeopathic Repertorization Remains English-Dominant in Bengali-Speaking Regions
Shahin Mahmud
November 2025
 

Homeopathy maintains significant institutional presence in Bangladesh, yet despite thousands of Bengali-speaking practitioners, all repertories—comprehensive reference volumes containing tens of thousands of symptom entries—remain exclusively in English. This paper examines why repertorization resists translation into Bangla, revealing an "alphabetical paradox": repertories depend on alphabetical organization for rapid clinical navigation, but translation necessarily disrupts this entire structure. Maintaining English alphabetical order renders Bangla translations functionally useless for monolingual speakers, while reorganizing by Bangla alphabet destroys international compatibility and two centuries of established navigational frameworks. Additional challenges include medical terminology gaps, regional dialect variations, and software development barriers for digital tools. English dominance thus reflects not merely historical inertia but fundamental structural incompatibility in organizing complex medical knowledge across linguistic boundaries. Viable solutions require reconceptualizing knowledge organization through semantic search technologies, conceptual categorization, or hybrid cross-lingual systems—demanding institutional support, interdisciplinary collaboration, sustained funding, and years of clinical validation.

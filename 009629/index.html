Going Through Social Red Lights: Combatting Hate Speech, Prejudice, and Incitement
Awni Etaywe
October 2024
 

Language is fundamental to and culturally. All too often, language is used as the shorthand for defining cultural commonality and difference – and when tensions flare up, language is the first tool of violence. It, therefore, holds immense and transformative power, capable of both building and fracturing communities. And in the urgent fight against hate speech and prejudice, prioritising genuine human rights concerns and employing rigorous linguistic analysis are paramount. Language not only mirrors reality but actively shapes it, moulding our experi- ences and converting perceptions into meanings. Selective linguistic choices perpetuate sexual difference, racial otherness, and social inequalities.1 Individuals utilise language and other semiotic tools to express hatred and provoke hostility, posing significant societal challenges. By understanding these at a socio- linguistic level, we can illuminate the pivotal role of language in fomenting violence, providing insights into offenders’ motivations and involvement in incitement.

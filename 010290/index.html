When competing grammars fight it to a stalemate: The verbalization of the infinitive in Hungarian
Tamás Halm
September 2026
 

In this paper, we analyze the appearance of the 1>2SG suffix on the infinitival paradigm in Modern Hungarian. This development is challenging for the existing accounts of verbal agreement in Hungarian because the 1>2SG suffix has originally been restricted to the finite paradigm. We argue that the full gamut empirical facts can be accounted for by recourse to three competing grammars for agreeing infinitives: N (where infinitivals are nominal), Vdeficient (where infinitivals are verbal, with a paradigm gap in cell 1>2SG) and V full (where infinitives are verbal, with no paradigm gap.) This results in there being two tribes of Modern Hungarian speakers: Tribe 1 (where N and Vdeficient compete very discreetly) and Tribe 2 (where Vfull is crowding N out). Grammar N is in line with É. Kiss’s (2002) proposal; whereas Grammars V can be plausibly modelled in moderately revised versions of Den Dikken’s (2004) and Bárány’s (2017) system (both of which were originally developed with the finite paradigm in mind). Finally, we argue that the failure of the innovative paradigm (Vfull) to spread fast and wide is due to three factors: (i) the relatively minor advantage offered by Vfull to its alternatives, (ii) the fact that infinitival agreement in general is fading in the language and (iii) the spread of the Balkan subjunctive, which provides a more established outlet for the verbalization of subordination.

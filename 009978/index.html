From 'also' to 'even': Function alternations of the Mandarin particle ye
Yimei Xiang
May 2026
 

This paper addresses a cross-linguistic puzzle concerning the frequent association between additive expressions and scalar interpretations. It focuses on the Mandarin additive particle ye and compares it with dou, which has a similar 'even'-like use in lian-sentences despite differing substantially from ye in other constructions. I argue that the presuppositional properties of ye, including focus-sensitivity, additivity, and scalarity, derive from a single definedness condition, termed 'Identity to Anti-Exclusion'. Parallel to the distributor-to-'even' alternation of dou, the 'also'-to-'even' alternation of ye arises when exclusivity is defined in terms of likelihood rather than logical strength. The paper further investigates the behavior of ye and dou across three complex scalar constructions. I argue that ye functions as a genuinely 'even'-like scalar operator in minimizer-licensing constructions, but retains its identity as the non-scalar additive operator 'also' in evaluative statements and scalar concessives. By contrast, dou, which permits a non-additive scalar interpretation, patterns with ye in minimizer-licensing constructions but diverges from it in evaluative statements and scalar concessives. Finally, the paper revisits the independence requirement on additive expressions, arguing that this requirement does not arise from lexical semantics, but rather from pragmatic constraints on the question-under-discussion.

Grounding as a Discursive Achievement: A Cognitive Grammar Analysis of the Definite Article in a Pedagogical Lect
Atrayee Mukherjee
February 2026
 

Pedagogical discourse relies on coordination of mental spaces between interlocutors. In Cognitive Grammar, this is achieved through grounding. The definite article acts as a cognitive command to establish mental contact. Formal approaches to uniqueness often view uniqueness as static, this study argues it as a discursive achievement. This paper proposes a seven type schema model of grounding operations. By the analysis of 7 classroom lectures, this study demonstrates the use of the epistemic control cycle to negotiate attention. Notably, the analysis highlights the emergence of reference point constructions- such as the “lizard..tail” phenomenon–reveals cognitive anchoring strategies for joint attention being prioritized over artifactual conventional schemas when categorizing usage events. It concludes that grounding operations are governed by universal cognitive strategies of joint attention management rather than the formal constraints. It contributes to usage based understanding of how grammar functions as a tool for intersubjective alignment in complex conceptual negotiations.

Attitudes of fear and expletive negation in French
Furkan Dikmen, Lena Baunaz, Anne-Li Demonie
July 2025
 

This paper examines the distribution of expletive negation (exn) in French fear-clauses and proposes a refined ontology of fear-related attitudes. We argue that fear-predicates select two semantically distinct types of complements, cognitive and conative, reflecting two types of fears. Cognitive fears are truth-apt and exhibit a mind-to-world direction of fit, similar to beliefs. Conative fears, in contrast, resemble wishes or commands. They are realization-apt and exhibit a world-to mind direction of fit instead. This distinction explains why some fear -clauses in French are truth-apt, whereas others can only be realized. Although fear-predicates can in principle introduce either type of complement, we argue that the syntax of the embedded clause constrains the choice. Specifically, the presence of ExN introduces an object of belief associated with the fear, enforcing a cognitive interpretation. In its the absence, the embedded structure is understood to be conative. Our proposal has two cross-linguistic implications. First, languages may realize distinct fear objects differently (e.g., English). Second, we predict structural variation in embedded clauses under fear, depending on their semantic type (e.g., Turkish).

The inherent modality of verbs of perception, recognition, and disposition in Hungarian
Tamás Halm
January 2026
 

This paper argues for an extended notion of sublexical modality through an in-depth analysis of three verb classes in Hungarian: verbs of involuntary perception, verbs of involuntary recognition and verbs of physical disposition. Verbs falling into these natural classes pattern together across four, seemingly unrelated, phenomena: the incompatibility with an ability modal auxiliary, the existence of a dispositional middle voice form, the appearance in so-called root infinitives of circumstantial modality, and susceptibility to reinterpretation as auxiliaries. I argue that this is because of their unique, enriched semantics: such verbs have ability modality hard-wired into their lexical semantic representation. While sublexical modality has long been argued to play a role in the semantics of defeasible causatives; I will argue that this concept has a much broader and more general validity, spanning a wider range of verb classes and more flavours of modality than previously thought. I will further argue that cross-linguistically, the lexical encoding of ability modality is an optional design element in the architecture of language which modulates the division of labour between (i) the various modules of language (syntax and semantics) and (ii) between speaker and hearer.

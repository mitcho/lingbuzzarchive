Dissertation Chapter Modifiability Graph Diagnostics for Operator Architecture
MAKS PANNER
January 2026
 

This article formalizes a graph-theoretic diagnostic for nominal architecture. Within a fixed Level‑ k inventory of part-of-speech (POS) items or POS-classes, we build a directed modifiability graph whose edges encode attested direct head-modification under a strict criterion (head targeting, constituency, no category shift, and exclusion of phrase-level scope). Graph partitions—isolates, strongly connected components (SCCs), and stacking partial orders—are coupled to a three-tier architectural taxonomy: High (operators), Middle (repair/packaging mechanisms), and Low (argumental primitives such as reciprocals, nominal WH heads, and free relatives). We provide a closure-sensitivity feature system and prove three results: (T1) an Operator‑ Isolation Theorem for D/Q operator heads under strict head-modification; (T2) a No‑ Post‑ Q‑ Closure Theorem deriving contrasts such as almost every NP vs *the every NP from selection/typing constraints; and (T3) a Repair‑ Completeness Lemma enumerating the only admissible sources of missing ⟨ e,t⟩ material (ellipsis, pronounization, prop‑ noun/classifier repair, or typed coercion). We present a diagnostic battery that can be executed across languages with a small parameter set (article realization, classifier availability, and ellipsis/pro‑ drop), and we sketch computational induction and neurocognitive predictions distinguishing closure from repair operations.

Negation and bias in Czech polar questions: evidence from a naturalness rating study
Anna Staňková
July 2026
 

Negative polar questions (PQs) display bias patterns that have proven difficult to capture within one account. This paper proposes a novel analysis of negation and bias in Czech PQs. While previous literature has distinguished between inner and outer negation readings, we introduce a third, novel category: medial negation. We model the three readings using a unified semantic framework where bias modals operate on the non-at-issue tier, and the negative operator interacts with them from either the at-issue or non-at-issue level. Inner negation operates at-issue, interacting with a contextual bias modal; medial negation modifies the contextual bias modal on the non-at-issue tier; and outer negation operates non-at-issue and triggers a speaker bias modal. The obligatory focus on the negated modal statement in Czech outer negation PQs triggers exhaustivity, encoding a speaker anti-bias (i.e., that the speaker finds both p and neg p equally accessible) that crucially contrasts with the bias patterns observed in English outer negation PQs. The proposed analysis provides a uniform treatment of negation and question bias while allowing for cross-linguistic variation in question meaning. Finally, we support our theoretical analysis with data from a naturalness judgment task, demonstrating that Czech native speakers are sensitive to all three proposed negation readings.

Refining the internal structure of expressive binominal structures: a view from Italian
Edoardo Cavirani, Cora Cavirani-Pots, Guido Wyngaerd
September 2026
 

Several languages display Qualitative Binominal Noun Phrases (QBNPs), i.e. binominal structures where the first noun phrase encodes an evaluation by the speaker of the referent denoted by the second noun phrase, and at the same time conveys the emotional state of the speaker. An example of such a structure is given in (1), which conveys the fact that the speaker is annoyed by a professor, for whatever reason. (1) That idiot of a professor has disappeared again! We argue that Italian provides evidence for the existence of (at least) three different classes of Expressive Nouns (EN), which differ from each other in terms of (i) semantic and pragmatic contribution to the proposition, (ii) syntactic distribution, and (iii) concord patterns. The QBNPs we discuss feature ENs such as cazzo ‘dick’, merda ‘shit’, and stronzo ‘turd’ (2). We propose that the different ENs we identify occupy different syntactic positions, i.e. they can occur in the specifier of a functional projection hosting evaluative adjectives or in the Deg-head hosted in the extended projection of such functional projection. We also propose that the expressive component of these ENs is determined by both their syntactic position and the degree to which they are grammaticalised. More specifically, we argue that their different behaviour with respect to concord derives from their morpho-syntactic structure, namely from the hypothesis that ENs can be semi-lexical to different degrees, and from the EN having or not having interpretable ϕ-features that can be either valued or unvalued. As for the prepositional element occurring between ENs and RNs, we propose that it is a context-dependent realisation of a morphosyntactic structure that is formally and functionally similar to adjectival concord markers.

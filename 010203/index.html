Quantifiers are in Different Positions: all and both in English, German, and Yiddish
Dorian Roehrs
August 2026
 

This paper argues that the quantifiers for all and both can occur in different positions in the three closely related languages English, German, and Yiddish. Such differences have to be taken into consideration in the explanation of ungrammatical cases, as briefly exemplified here. All these languages allow these two quantifiers to be in the DP-layer. However, they vary as regards the predeterminer and the postdeterminer positions. Since Yiddish is the most tolerant language – it allows both quantifiers to occur in all three positions, the restrictions in the other languages can presumably not involve a strong account.

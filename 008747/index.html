Delete the Rich: On the non-existence of ‘weak’ schwa deletion
Charles Reiss
January 2025
 

It is widely assumed that a ‘weak’ schwa, a vowel with a minimal amount of featural specification, is often phonologically deleted across languages. There are, however, conceptual problems with this perspective, and under fairly standard assumptions, it turns out to be logically impossible to delete weak schwa to the exclusion of more highly specified vowels. In this light, the lack of robust empirical evidence for weak schwa deletion is not surprising. Given basic feature logic, richly specified segments can be deleted to the exclusion of more weakly specified segments. However, the underspecified segments can be inserted to the exclusion of more highly specified ones.

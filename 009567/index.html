Did Someone Happen it, or Was it Happened? Evidence from Corpus Data and Acceptability Judgments of Passive Unaccusatives
Yu Tazaki
December 2025
 

This study investigates passivization errors with unaccusatives in L2 English, known as passive unaccusatives (e.g., *The apple was fallen from the table.). Two competing accounts are considered: the verbal passivization account (VPA), which attributes passive unaccusatives to misanalysis of unaccusatives as lexical causatives, and the structural passivization account (SPA), which explains them as overgeneralization of passive morphosyntax (be + -en). The two accounts yield distinct predictions concerning passivized intransitives and lexically causativized intransitives. To test these predictions, both a corpus analysis and an acceptability judgment task were conducted. A corpus analysis of the NICT JLE Corpus (1,281 Japanese learners) revealed that passive unaccusatives were more frequent than their non-target lexical causative counterparts (e.g., *Brian fell the apple from the table.) and passive unergatives (e.g., *Leslie was walked.). An acceptability judgment task with 30 Japanese-speaking learners (TOEIC 550–745) likewise showed higher ratings for passive unaccusatives compared to lexical causative unaccusatives and passive unergatives. Taken together, these results support the SPA, suggesting that L2 learners do not causativize unaccusatives but instead overgeneralize NP-movement associated with passivization. The converging corpus and experimental evidence therefore reject the VPA and provide empirical support for the SPA in explaining non-target passivization in L2 grammar.

Copy control and other control properties in Mandarin
Chit-Fung Lam, Mary Dalrymple
January 2026
 

The possibility of overt controllees has long challenged control theories. This paper investigates copy control in Mandarin Chinese, where the embedded subject of a control construction may be overtly realized. Based on acceptability-judgment experiments, we establish empirical generalizations concerning how copy control interacts with exhaustive control, partial control, and raising. We show that copy control is available in Mandarin, but only under tightly constrained conditions: it is licensed with exhaustive and partial control verbs in specific configurations, but excluded with raising predicates and transitive subject-control verbs. We argue that these patterns are not best captured by finiteness-based approaches, which dominate existing accounts. Instead, we propose a formal analysis within Lexical Functional Grammar (LFG), distinguishing functional control (structure sharing) from anaphoric control (pronominal dependency). Copy control arises precisely in anaphoric-control environments, while being systematically excluded under functional control. The paper contributes to cross-framework comparison (LFG vs Minimalist approaches), distinctions between grammatically licensed copy control and processing-based resumptives, lexical specification under interspeaker variation, and the typology of anaphoric control. We argue that a hybrid model integrating structure sharing and anaphoric dependency provides a principled and empirically grounded account of complex control phenomena.

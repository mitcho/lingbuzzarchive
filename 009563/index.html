The Impact Of Mindset On Perceived Intelligence And Academic Resilience
Maureen Otieno
September 2025
 

Mindset theory suggests that viewpoints about the phenomenon of intelligence, whether it is stable/fixed or plastic profile an individual learner’s approaches to learning, flexibility, and educational consequences (Dweck, 2006; Blackwell, Trzesniewski & Dweck, 2007). The current research investigates how tertiary level learners’ mindset orientation connects with their self-estimated IQ, responses of their behavior to educational obstacles, and school achievement. Data from 214 students exhibited that the learners who have a growth-mindset stated minor powerlessness, robust and flexible study approach, and extra adaptable self-estimates of intelligence in comparison to fixed-mindset colleagues, corresponding to results by Yeager and Dweck (2012) and Claro et al. (2016). Mindset exhibited an ordinary relationship with GPA, mirroring broader meta-analytic patterns stated by Sisk et al. (2018). The findings emphasize the significance of beliefs underpinning motivation in determining persistence in academic challenges.

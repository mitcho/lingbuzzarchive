Flattening Semantic Representations: Modelling Structural Ambiguity in Formal Semantics
Akpoué Josué
August 2026
 

Formal semantics typically assumes that the interpretation relation linking a linguistic unit to its denotation is functional, treating ambiguity—especially structural ambiguity—as a phenomenon to be resolved grammatically. However, if ambiguity were fully resolved within the linguistic system, widespread and well-documented misunderstandings would not arise. We instead model the interpretation relation as non-functional in the strict mathematical sense. Accordingly, ⟦·⟧ᴵ is redefined as a function that collects the set of possible denotations of a given linguistic unit under a relation I, while disambiguation is handled pragmatically via a choice function γ. Misunderstanding arises when γ differs across interlocutors. We further argue that constituency ambiguities motivate flattened semantic representations: ⟦·⟧ᴵ operates over LCA(S), and FA* computes type-filtered sets of denotations. The full characterization of γ is left as an open research program.

Language Models as Function Approximators of Text Data: Disrupting Comprehension Through An Adversarial Attack
David J. Lobina
November 2024
 

Large Language Models such as ChatGPT are examples of Machine (Deep) Learning models (ML), and as such, they are best regarded as “function approximators”, and thus as vast correlation mappings. That is, given a dataset, however big, of inputs and outputs (in particular, pairs of inputs and outputs), an ML network will construct a function that best describes (approximates) the distribution in the data, and from whence it can generate similar input-output pairs if and when prompted. In the case of Language Models such as ChatGPT, the dataset was composed of text – the products, or externalisations, of the language faculty. In this sense, a Language Model does not model a natural language per se, the latter a sound-meaning system composed of syntactic, semantic, and phonological components (among others), but the statistical distribution of text, the latter treated as recorded, static data. A fortiori, the claim that some of the results emanating from the development of Large Language Models bear any consequence for the study of language, especially regarding linguistic competence and acquisitional studies, appear to be the case of committing a category mistake.

Restricting allomorphy without spell-out opacity
Maša Bešlin
July 2026
 

This short paper argues that restrictions on contextual allomorphy do not require spell-out-induced opacity. Standard approaches derive locality restrictions from cyclic spell-out, supplemented by additional stipulations that render previously spelled-out material inaccessible for later allomorphic interactions. I argue instead that the relevant locality condition is featural adjacency: two elements may interact allomorphically only if no projecting head intervenes between them. This proposal derives the familiar restriction against root-conditioned allomorphy across categorizing heads while eliminating the need for interface-specific opacity conditions. It also accounts for apparent counterexamples involving adjuncts and root-root compounds, which remain transparent for allomorphy because they do not introduce intervening projecting heads. The proposal captures empirical patterns that are problematic for linear adjacency, geometric structural adjacency, and accessibility-domain approaches, while unifying locality restrictions on allomorphy with independently motivated locality conditions on c-selection.

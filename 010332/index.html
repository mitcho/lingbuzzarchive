External arguments in the short passive? The view from Spanish
John David Storment, Francisco Ordóñez
September 2026
 

In this paper, we examine the status of the syntactic projection of the implicit external argument in short periphrastic passive derivations in Spanish. To do this, we apply tests mainly from Collins (2005, 2024) concerning the licensing of agent-oriented secondary depictive predicates, the argument control of big PRO subjects of embedded clauses, the binding of reflexive pronominals, and the non-anaphoric status of non-reflexive pronominals. The results of these tests, placed in a theoretical context of a Minimalist framework (e.g., Chomsky 2021; Chomsky et al. 2023), strongly endorse a view in which the external argument is syntactically-projected in the Spanish short passive. We additionally present novel evidence from Spanish that the reflexive pronominal elements licensed in Spanish short passives cannot plausibly be exempt logophors. In accordance with these theoretical and empirical considerations, we straightforwardly adopt Collins’ (2005, 2024) smuggling approach to the Spanish passive. We further show that several notable properties of the Spanish passive are naturally explained under this smuggling analysis, such as the preverbal, nominative status of the structural subject, the obligatory agreement in phi-features between the participial verb and the subject, and the impossibility of inalienable-possession readings of the short passive, as noted by MacDonald (2017).

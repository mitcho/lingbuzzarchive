Becoming a Linguist: Advice from Key Thinkers in Language Studies (book review)
Norbert Francis
May 2026
 

This collection of chapters offers advice, from a wide range of authors, to students of language and linguistics. Chapters 1 and 2 begin with surveys of the field from Noam Chomsky and William Labov. The editors made a good choice as the two introductory chapters, while they approach the study of language from different theoretical frameworks, apply the methods of science to the respective objects of study. This was a good way to begin. In addition, the second chapter expanded the discussion to consider how linguistics as a scientific endeavor can be applied to addressing important issues of educational and, more broadly, social inequality, for improving school-related language instruction for example. A number of the subsequent chapters follow up on this application. A greater emphasis, more prominently in the final chapters, shifts the focus to non-scientific analyses notably associated with the openly anti-scientific philosophy of postmodernism. The outright ideologization of linguistic questions, for example in response to scientists who resist political indoctrination, resorts to unusual and often strange characterization, as in the invention of incoherent categories such as “Western science” with the purpose of promoting new, politically committed, lines of discourse in academia. Interestingly, the postmodern current in language studies has come to gain popularity in a number of sub-fields.

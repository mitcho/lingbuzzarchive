The representation of intonation
Amalia Arvaniti
July 2025
 

Two approaches dominate the study of intonation with respect to its representation: tunes are treated either as configurations or as composites of primitives (which are either configurations themselves, such as rises and falls, or level tones, such as highs and lows, and composites thereof). Here, tracing the historical provenance of these two approaches shows that many relevant observations go as far back as the early 20th century. Additionally, the essential tenets of each approach are presented together with relevant empirical evidence. This evidence overwhelmingly confirms that configurational approaches are not observationally adequate. On the other hand, treating tunes as composites of primitives which independently contribute both to the tune’s form and to intonation meaning offers significant advantages: it is observationally adequate, facilitates cross-linguistic research, and allows researchers to capture important generalizations that help predict the form and understand the pragmatic function of tunes. The compositional approach is best exemplified by the autosegmental-metrical theory of intonational phonology, the main principles of which are presented in some detail, together with an overview of recent developments that have led to a better understanding of the relationship between phonological representation and intonation phonetics.

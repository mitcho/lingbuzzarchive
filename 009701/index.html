Operators, Quantifiers, and NP-Internal Structure: An Expanded Cognitive and Cross-Linguistic Account of Nominal Architecture
MAKS PANNER
January 2026
 

This article develops and expands a cognitively grounded, cross-linguistic model of nominal phrase architecture that distinguishes three structural layers: (i) an NP-core containing conceptual content (nouns, modifiers, possessives, relational material, interrogative determiners, and prop-nouns such as English one), (ii) a quantifier layer comprising true quantifiers that denote cardinality, proportion, or amount and can satisfy NP-headedness and substitution diagnostics, and (iii) an operator layer comprising higher-order scope instructions—including every, each, and no—that take a domain argument but are predicted not to behave as quantifier heads. The preprint makes the diagnostics explicit, expands the typology into a parallel elicitation grid, and adds two sets of falsifiable consequences: (a) distinguishing predictions for stacking, partitives, and anaphora/ellipsis (Tables 10–11; Figure 5), and (b) explicitly marked neurolinguistic and computational extensions that map the layer split to processing stages and NLP annotation/assembly pipelines (Xakimqulova 2024; Barwise & Cooper 1981; Szabolcsi 2010).

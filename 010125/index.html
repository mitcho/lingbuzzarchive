Floating tones
Nicholas Rolle, Larry M. Hyman, Florian Lionnet
July 2026
 

Floating tones are tones that are not associated with any tone-bearing unit (e.g., segmental material), and they have been used widely in the analysis of a variety of tone systems since the second half of the 20th century. Floating tones are inherently abstract and do not constitute an empirical object (one cannot hear a floating tone). Rather they are an analytical device, one that we show is indispensable for tonal analysis. This chapter gives an overview of floating tones, their nature, and the variety of tonal phenomena they have been used to account for (downstep, blocking effects, etc.). While most of our examples come from African languages (primarily from the Niger-Congo stock, or those within its orbit), our discussion should be relevant to tone languages in general.

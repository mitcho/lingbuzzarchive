The ingredients of hyperraising: insights from Tamil
Daniel Greeson, Sandhya Sundaresan
September 2025
 

On the strength of novel data from Tamil, which notably lacks hyperraising but optionally allows (classic) raising, this paper argues against two prominent analyses of hyperraising – in terms of the Activity Condition and in terms of CP intervention – which, we will show, overgenerate the possibility of hyperraising in this language. In contrast, we show that proposals that seek to explain the availability of hyperraising in terms of the Williams Cycle (WC, Williams, 2003) acting in conspiracy with the Phase Impenetrability Condition (PIC, Chomsky, 2001, et seq.) accurately predict its absence in Tamil. Further independent empirical support for this line of analysis comes from Spanish, Tiriki and varieties of colloquial English, showing that a hyperraised subject in these languages occupies a higher-than-canonical position that is at least as high as Spec, CP. On the strength of these findings, we propose that hyperraising is possible in some language L if and only if long subject movement can obtain in L without violating the PIC or WC, and the subject A-moves to an athematic position. A language that manifests hyperraising must conjunctively satisfy all and only these input conditions. Since there are many ways to violate these conditions and exactly one way to obey them, our proposal also predicts the crosslinguistic rarity of this phenomenon.

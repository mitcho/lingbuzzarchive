One grammar, two agreement options: Existential 'there' in New York City English
John David Storment, Sophie Brown
August 2026
 

In this paper, we discuss optional agreement with third-person plural postverbal subjects of existential there clauses in New York City English. Data were obtained from the CUNY Corpus of New York City English, (CUNY-CoNYCE), an approximately two-million word corpus of extemporaneous speech from 202 participants raised in the New York City Dialect region (Tortora et al. 2025). The results of our study, along with the native-speaker grammaticality judgments of the author(s), suggest that both singular and plural verbal agreement is freely available for speakers of NYC English with 3pl postverbal subjects of existential clauses. We model the syntax of this optionally-realized agreement in terms of a single grammatical system—i.e., not as the result of multiple competing grammars (e.g., Kroch 1989, a.o.)—based in the Strong Minimalist Thesis (SMT) (Chomsky 2021; 2024). Ultimately, we analyze this instance of variable agreement as the result of the grammar allowing for multiple interpretable outputs generated in the narrow syntax. Additionally, this system permits language users’ extragrammatical preferences to constrain the frequency of use of optional forms based on interaction with variables such as tense. We provide a novel set of tools for analyzing cases of intra-speaker variation that cannot be clearly attributed to underlying multilectalism.

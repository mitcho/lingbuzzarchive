Sublexical modality
Fabienne Martin
April 2026
 

This chapter focuses on sublexical modal verbs, in particular lexical causatives such as teach, send, or offer that covertly encode a modal layer, rendering them defeasible: they imply a result without entailing it. I first outline the various treatments of low verbal modality (in the VP) and propose that modality is introduced by the verbalizing head. A central puzzle addressed is the agent requirement for failed attempt readings: cancelling the result is felicitous with agentive subjects but typically yields a contradiction with non-agentive (causer) subjects. After examining some limitations of previous approaches, I develop an account of this requirement in an anchor-based semantics (Kratzer 2013, Phillips & Kratzer 2024). I show that coupled with Varasdi’s (2014) analysis of descriptions of incomplete events and Martin et al.’s (2025) treatment of the event-state relation in lexical causatives, this account can explain the aforementioned puzzles in a more satisfactory way. Furthermore, I explain which property of agency is crucial for the failed attempt interpretation, based on a typology of atypical agents as they are grammatically encoded across languages. The conclusion will be that foreseeability appears to be the essential agentive dimension for failed attempt readings. The last section examines the conditions facilitating these readings: the low perceptibility of the target outcome, the surface indistinguishability of successful and unsuccessful action tokens, and the presence of manner information in the lexical semantics of the verb.

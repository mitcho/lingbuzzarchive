When AI Speaks English: Marginalized Languages in Malaysia
Guang L
May 2025
 

This article investigates the sociolinguistic consequences of English-centric Artificial Intelligence (AI) models in multilingual Malaysia. As AI technologies integrate into daily life, their linguistic biases challenge non-Anglophone populations. Using a mixed-methods approach, this study examines AI performance disparities in English, Bahasa Malaysia, and Tamil. Findings reveal significant inadequacies in the models’ handling of local languages, with higher error rates in key tasks. These technical shortcomings translate into tangible social and economic burdens, creating digital inequality and reinforcing English hegemony. We argue this technological linguistic marginalization functions as a modern mechanism of exclusion, limiting access for non-English speakers. The study concludes by calling for a de-centered, sociolinguistically informed approach to AI development that prioritizes linguistic equity.

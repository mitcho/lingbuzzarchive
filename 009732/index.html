Yes/no questions across Chinese varieties and English
Xuhui Hu, Danfeng Wu
February 2026
 

This article provides a morphosyntactic and semantic analysis of nine yes/no question constructions across English and Chinese varieties (e.g., Mandarin, Yixing, Nanjing, Suzhou, Hefei, Kunming, Liangshan, many of which are understudied), and shows that the apparently complex crosslinguistic variation is in fact complete systematic variation generated by just three binary parameters. The first parameter is whether or not the question involves a polar Disjunction Phrase (DisjP) in syntax. The questions with a polar DisjP vary in two more parameters: whether there is overt or covert wh-movement, and whether the DisjP is realised as a single Vocabulary Item or multiple. Our study has two theoretical implications. The first is for question semantics and typology: we provide syntactic and morphological evidence for two types of interrogative C0s in Hamblin’s (1973) question semantics; we also argue that the yes-no questions without DisjP do not involve any wh-element, and thus no (c)overt wh-movement or even genuine wh-in-situ, thus enriching the typology of questions. Our second theoretical contribution lies in theories of parametric variation. The three parameters proposed in this article have three different sources, all of which should be taken into account in parametric theories: linearization, phonological realization of features during Lexical Insertion, and the (un)availability of features in the lexicon.

The representation of selection in syntax
Elise Newman
December 2025
 

In this paper, I revisit some puzzles about selection, and argue that they suggest principled restrictions on the content of syntactic features. I take up two guiding principles, which jointly comprise what I will call a Sparse Feature approach to selection and Merge. The first principle is that Merge-inducing features should be abstract, referring to properties that distinguish sets of lexical items from other sets of lexical items, without referring to individual lexical items themselves. The second principle is that Merge-inducing features should refer to syntactic properties. I argue that when selection looks driven by semantic or lexical factors, the only corresponding syntactic feature that meets these two criteria is an underspecified feature, which makes predictions about the distribution of phrases selected this way. I propose that the predictions are borne out, and provide insight into the locality of selection, the existence of smuggling, and old puzzles about c-selection first discussed by Grimshaw and Pesetsky.

Anaphora in Static Orthodox First-Order Logic
Oleg Kiselyov
March 2026
 

The paper demonstrates representing pronomial anaphora in the orthodox first-order predicate logic with the completely standard syntax and semantics. Specifically, we show how to represent not yet resolved pronouns in conventional logical formulas, and then record the result of their resolution (coindexing). Our proposal is static, simply-typed, minimalist, with the orthodox quantification, binding and scope. It relies on the 1920s logic, and applies classically and intuitionistically -- in particular, treating implication as is. It is inspired by and extensively relies on intuitionistic Skolemization. We demonstrate our approach on intra- and inter-sentential pronomial anaphora; bound-variable anaphora; conditional, relative-clause and proportional quantifier donkey sentences; quantificational subordination. The approach aims to separate out pragmatic concerns from meaning composition: being able to map a syntactic structure to a logical formula representing the meaning, phrase-by-phrase and clause-by-clause, without prior coindexing and regardless of context. Pronoun resolution is to be done in the next, pragmatic step, based on the obtained formulas. Although coindexing is delegated to pragmatics, the logical formulas constrain the coindexing, helping the resolution and delivering predictions, of unavailability of particular antecedents.

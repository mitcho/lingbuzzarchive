The emergence of “weak” definite articles
Giuseppe Magistro, Alexandra Simonenko
February 2026
 

This paper investigates the emergence and distribution of weak definite articles in German prepositional phrases (e.g. vom) at the expense of strong articles (e.g. von dem). While reduced weak forms are already attested in Old High German, their use was initially rare and largely restricted to noun phrases that are very likely to have a singleton extension in any situation. Over time, weak articles expanded to a wider range of noun phrases, yet failed to extend to anaphoric environments with an indefinite antecedent, where strong forms remain obligatory in Modern German. By combining historical corpus evidence with agent-based simulations grounded in computational pragmatics and reinforcement learning, we aimed to derive this distribution from independently motivated assumptions about reference, economy, and language acquisition. In particular, we assumed that the weak forms, in contrast to strong ones, can give rise to referential uncertainty since their interpretation depends on the properties of the topic situation, as construed by the listener. The uncertainty is low with NPs which are known to mostly denote singletons in any situation and greater with other NP types. We also built into our model an intuition that an indefinite NP lowers the estimate that a contextually salient situation has a singleton with the NP-property in question. Simulation results closely mirror the stages of the historical development attested in the corpus, suggesting that pitching the economy (i.e. preferring a reduced form) vs. clarity (preferring a strong form unless the communicative success chances are high enough) is a fruitful way of modeling article evolution.

Violations for Continuous Candidates in Constraint-based Phonology
Alexandre Menezes Barroso
April 2026
 

Constraint-based phonology normally assigns violations to discrete candidates. At the phonetics-phonology interface, however, many observables are continuous, so pre-evaluative discretization can impose arbitrary boundaries and false local homogeneity. This article argues that continuous candidate spaces therefore require an explicit theory of violation computation. I propose a model in which candidate availability is represented by an empirical density over acoustic space, perceptual and articulatory constraints are defined as continuous functions, and violations are derived by linking constraint values to empirical density. Among several transparent link functions, an exponentialized relation provides the most coherent violation object under the present criteria. A two-formant implementation over Brazilian Portuguese pretonic /e/ shows that weighted harmonic and MaxEnt-style evaluation can operate directly over a continuous vowel category, yielding region-sensitive well-formedness and a fitted output density without pre-evaluative binning while preserving category-internal gradient structure in phonological evaluation itself.

Defocus Rephrasing: Right dislocation and syntax-prosody mismatches
Ka-Fai Yip, Xuetong Yuan
November 2025
 

This study adopts a rigorous interface approach to investigate the role of defocus, the systematic lack of focus, in syntax-prosody mapping. Despite the abundance of work on focus prosody, how defocus may affect the mapping and whether its effects should be distinguished from those of focus remains relatively unknown. We argue that defocus can, and should in fact, be separated from focus for prosodic phrasing, drawing crucial support from right dislocation (RD) in Cantonese and Mandarin. In RD, elements are displaced or copied to the right of a sentence, a position that resists semantic, alternative-based focus. We show that although RD consists of two syntactic clauses, it only has one intonational phrase, constituting a syntax-prosody mismatch. We provide evidence from both phonological processes and phonetic cues with acoustic experiments. We propose that defocus cannot receive head prominence and triggers rephrasing to avoid illegitimate headless intonational phrases, leading to the mismatch in RD. Importantly, such a mismatch is not due to any preceding focus, which independently does not trigger rephrasing in both languages. We offer an Optimality-Theoretic account with constraint ranking to capture the asymmetry between focus and defocus. The findings illustrate the intricate interplay of semantic notions with syntax and prosody, and have implications for the modeling of prosodic (non-)prominence.

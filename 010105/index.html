Cognitive Indexical Usage of Demonstrative "Ku" in Korean and a Split DP Analysis
Min-Joo Kim
July 2026
 

This paper takes up a hitherto unnoted phenomenon in Korean in which the distal demonstrative (DEM) “ku” occurs modifying an adjective phrase (AP), seemingly violating the well-established weight-based adjective ordering restriction (AOR), namely, ‘heavy N modifier > light N modifier > N’. I show that such occurrence of “ku”, which I call KU, is not a run-of-the-mill DEM, and I explain its behavior by calling it ‘a cognitive indexical DEM’. The upshot of the analysis will be that KU selects for an AP and locates the property denoted by its complement in the mental domain of the speaker, pointing to it as if it were visible to the speaker, contributing a speaker comment. To implement this idea within a formal syntactic framework, I propose a tripartite Split-DP analysis which builds on Laenzlinger’s (2005) analysis of French AOR. By doing so, I offer a Rizzian (Rizzi 1997) cartographic analysis which captures the relative surface ordering between N dependents in Korean without violating the universal AOR mentioned above. The proposed analysis will be shown to capture the syntactic, semantic, and phonological properties of KU in comparison with the more “ordinary” DEM “ku”. It also has implications for the syntax, semantics, and pragmatics of DEMs in other languages including Slovenian and English. [Published in 2016; updated in 2026 with some typos fixed. See also Ch. 3, 4, 5 and 6 of Kim 2019 “The Syntax and Semantics of Noun Modifiers and the Theory of Universal Grammar: A Korean Perspective”.]

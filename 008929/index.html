English Tri-syllabic Shortening
Luigi Burzio
April 2025
 

If one surfs the Internet today -and we are in 2025- one finds references to the rule of ‘Tri-syllabic shortening proposed by Chomsky and Halle in 1968 to deal with cases like natural, divinity and many others, where the vowel in the antepenultimate syllable (third from the end) is short compared with the long ones of the unaffixed stems nAture, divIne, etc. Harder to come by are references to the alternative account I first gave at the LSA meeting in Chicago on January 4, 1991 (October 1990 Johns Hopkins manuscript) and then in much subsequent published work, which proves superior if correct. In that account, there are no ‘rules’ that re-write the structure as in the old-style Phonology, but only ‘output’ or surface constraints that interact by ranking, as in the Optimality Theory of Prince and Smolensky, that appeared in 1993. Thirty-four years on, let me briefly review the evidence again.

Reverse Entailment in Nominal Categorization
MAKS PANNER
September 2025
 

This position paper introduces the concept of noun reverse entailment, a bidirectional inference mechanism that formalizes how derived noun forms (e.g., plurals, possessives, nominalizations) entail backward to confirm the base form’s nominal properties. I propose the Reverse Entailment Diagnostic (RED), a tool for proving that lexical and syntactic entities (including phrases and parts of speech) can act as syntactical noun entities in dynamic linguistic contexts. Using examples like “square/quadrilateral,” “to book carefully,” and “her house,” I demonstrate how RED reveals category flexibility, challenging rigid lexicalist frameworks and supporting usage-based models. This framework has implications for syntax, semantics, and cognitive linguistics, emphasizing listener interpretation and holistic sentence processing

A alternative analysis of the morphological structure of Japanese nominal adjectives — NI-adjectives
Shinya Makino
October 2025
 

This paper will be fundamentally rewritten in line with the author’s recent study on the Japanese so-called copula -da/-dearu (https://ling.auf.net/lingbuzz/009376) -------------------- Japanese nominal adjectives (NAs) are often analysed as "so-called NA stems + the copula =da" in parallel with "NPs + the copula =da" — e.g. sizuka=da "it is quiet/silent" and gakusei=da "it is a student". However, this analysis has limitations in that it cannot account for the fact that in the latter there exist inflected forms ending in -ni — e.g. sizuka-ni — whereas in the former there are none, since gakusei=ni is a postpositional phrase headed by the postposition =ni. The present paper argues that it is precisely this -ni which constitutes the defining constituent of NAs, because -ni "in/at" converts the NA radical sizuka- — which refers to a conceptual place "quietness" — into a location "in/at quietness", thereby making it possible for the theme denoted by the subject to be located at that place, and only at this stage does sizuka, when merged with -ni, function predicatively in the sentence, that is, as a predicative stem, more specifically as an NA stem.

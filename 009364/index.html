A alternative analysis of the morphological structure of Japanese nominal adjectives — NI-adjectives
Shinya Makino
June 2026
 

This paper reanalyses the morphosyntactic–semantic structure of Japanese nominal adjectives (NAs), also referred to as na-adjectives. The core of NAs lies in NA stems ending in '-ni', as in 'jukai-ni' "pleasant" and 'suki-ni' "like". These NA stems are lexical predicates selecting either one argument or two — a theme in 'jukai-ni' and an experiencer–theme pair in 'suki-ni'. However, outside small clauses serving as complements of cognition or perception verbs, as in 'watasi=wa [sore=o jukai-ni] omot-ta/kanzi-ta' "I found/felt [it pleasant]", they cannot function as predicates in their bare '-ni' form. Consequently, an NA stem forms a nominal-adjective phrase containing PRO, as in [PRO jukai-ni], which constitutes the complement of the adjunctive-conjunction suffix '-te', yielding a ConjP such as [[PRO jukai-ni]-te]. This ConjP is then adjoined to — or, metaphorically, "parasitises on" — the VP headed by the one-argument existential verb 'ar-' within a (topic-marked) tensed construction, as in [soreᵢ=wa tᵢ ar-Ø-Ø] "it exists" ('-Ø-Ø' represents the sequence of the covert allomorph of the non-past suffix '-ru' followed by the phonologically null functional head Topic⁰Ø). Only in this configuration can the NA stem function as one of the two predicates of an independent simple sentence, as in [soreᵢ=wa [PROᵢ jukai-ni-te] tᵢ ar-Ø-Ø] "(lit.) it, being pleasant, exists". This construction surfaces as 'sore=wa jukai-d-a' [soreᵢ=wa [PROᵢ jukai-d] tᵢ a-Ø-Ø], schematically representable as EXIST(it) ⋀ PLEASANT(it) — whose default negation is, as expected, the scope-limited EXIST(it) ⋀ ¬PLEASANT(it), realised as 'sore=wa jukai-de=wa/jukai-dj-a nai' "(lit.) it, not being pleasant, exists", crucially with the scope-limiting '=wa' or its more colloquial allomorph '-a'.

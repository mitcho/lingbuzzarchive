Scalar values in downward and non-downward entailing contexts
Riny Huijbregts
February 2026
 

We discuss (i) the nature of a lexicalization condition on scalar implicatures that precludes lexicalizing negation of strong scalar elements, and (ii) parametric variation in the scope properties of conjunctive and disjunctive operators relative to negation. The empirical cases discussed show that logic differs from natural language, an internal cognitive system and part of human biology, therefore empirically constrained, in this case by conditions on polarity and scalar implicatures that are absent from formal logic. The interpretation of disjunction and conjunction is universal as in propositional logic following de Morgan’s laws. Hence, the conjunctive interpretation of disjunction in negative statements comes naturally. Still, how these laws apply in propositional logic and how humans attain knowledge of these laws and their use in language are different empirical problems for a biological organism to solve. It’s an intriguing empirical question which obviously needs an answer. Guided by the subset principle, children at first adopt the scope interpretation that yields true sentences in the narrowest range of circumstances, only “unlearning” what comes natural to them when forced to do so by positive evidence only. Biologically driven human capacities such as innate logic and the faculty of language, interacting with general third-factor principles of learnability and computational efficiency, guarantee that not everything need be learned and what need be learned can be learned, providing a principled solution to the poverty of stimulus for the acquisition of language.

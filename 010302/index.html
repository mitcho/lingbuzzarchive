The landscape of negative biases: modality, exclamative-questions and exclamatives
Alda Mari
September 2026
 

The interaction between modality and evidentiality has received considerable attention, but much less is known about the interaction between modality and speech-act operators. This paper argues that this interface is crucial to understanding how evaluative meanings arise with epistemic modals, particularly in non-information-seeking interrogatives and exclamatives. We develop an account of modal bias as a manipulable component of modal meaning and propose that question and exclamative operators can themselves act as bias manipulators. The empirical domain is Italian epistemic future, whose exceptional flexibility makes visible the mechanisms underlying this interaction. Building on previous work, we analyze the \textbf{Italian future} as a universal epistemic modal with no default bias, rather than as an evidential or a marker of subjective credence. Its flexibility derives from a metaevaluative layer at which the speaker’s evaluation of the prejacent is encoded and can be modified. The analysis distinguishes two mechanisms of negative bias: in `mica' future questions, bias emerges through the interaction of modality, QUES and the particle; in future exclamatives, it is introduced directly by EXCL. More generally, the paper argues that speech-act operators participate in semantic composition with modal meaning by manipulating its metaevaluative dimension, while remaining distinct from modality itself.

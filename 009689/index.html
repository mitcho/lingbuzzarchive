Gradient Conventionalization: A Unified Framework for Lexical Semantics and Social Epistemology
Tariq Mozahid
July 2026
 

This paper proposes a unified framework that reconceives the relationship between lexical meaning and world knowledge as a gradient of conventionalization rather than a categorical distinction. Meaning components are distinguished by their level of conventionalization, operationalized as degree of speaker agreement, rather than by membership in "lexical" versus "encyclopedic" categories. The framework introduces a five-level gradient — reference, denotation, sense, cultural connotation, private connotation — corresponding to decreasing intersubjective agreement, and a typology of entailments (strong, default, weak) correlated with these levels. This reconceptualization clarifies semantic prosody, explains why some arguments converge while others polarize, and connects lexical semantics to social epistemology. Central to the epistemological application is the diagnosis of alpha misattribution: the error of treating a term whose meaning is genuinely low-agreement as though it carried the stability of a high-agreement term, an error that converts unresolved conceptual disagreement into the appearance of bad-faith resistance to consensus. The framework generates testable predictions about semantic change, argument dynamics, and the conditions under which making agreement levels explicit can reduce polarization.

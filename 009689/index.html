Gradient Conventionalization: A Unified Framework for Lexical Semantics and Social Epistemology
Tariq Mozahid
January 2026
 

This paper proposes a unified theoretical framework that reconceptualizes the relationship between lexical meaning and world knowledge as a gradient of conventionalization rather than a categorical distinction. Through analysis of entailment strength and intersubjective agreement patterns, I argue that meaning components should be distinguished by their level of conventionalization—operationalized as degrees of speaker agreement—rather than by categorical membership in "lexical" versus "encyclopedic" knowledge. The framework introduces a five-level gradient (reference, denotation, sense, cultural connotation, private connotation) corresponding to decreasing levels of intersubjective agreement, and develops a typology of entailments (strong, default, weak) correlated with these conventionalization levels. This reconceptualization resolves longstanding puzzles in semantic prosody, explains why certain types of arguments converge while others polarize, and provides a unified account connecting lexical semantics to social epistemology. The framework generates testable predictions about semantic change, cross-linguistic variation, language acquisition, and the dynamics of disagreement in scientific versus philosophical discourse.

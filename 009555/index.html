Deriving the diminishing effect
Linmin Zhang
March 2026
 

The diminishing effect, a kind of norm-sensitivity, is observed in many natural language constructions: negative comparatives (e.g., He is no taller than I am), only-sentences (e.g., he is only 49 years old), and discourse-softening markers (e.g., Korean cina-ci anh- and Chinese bú guò ‘not beyond, nothing but, merely’), etc. All of these constructions give rise to the inference of ‘being below an expected norm’. I focus on the construction ‘no + comparative’ and propose that the diminishing effect arises from the interaction of two elements: (i) negation and (ii) universal quantification over items exceeding a certain degree d along a scale. I argue that (i) negating all values above d amounts to an even-sentence, (ii) when interpreting an even-sentence, we accommodate a degree QUD, such that the prejacent of even provides a maximally informative answer to this degree QUD, and (iii) a maximally informative degree is a superlative value with regard to a certain comparison class and is therefore necessarily norm-sensitive (see also Krifka 2000). I further extend this analysis to the diminishing effect of only.

Functional Extensionality and Its Discontents. The Curry–Howard–Lambek Correspondence and Natural Language Semantics
Miriam S. Schmidt
April 2026
 

What does it mean for two expressions to have the same meaning? In formal semantics, the standard answer is functionally extensional: two expressions mean the same if their functional outputs are equal on all possible inputs. This answer, we argue, is not merely a methodological choice but a consequence of the formal foundations most semantic theories rely on. The Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence (CHLC), unifying logic, computation, and category theory, carries an implicit commitment to functional extensionality that propagates into the semantic analysis of natural language. The problem is that language does not play by these rules. Speakers routinely distinguish meanings that extensional criteria cannot capture immediately. Distinctions may resist reduction to truth conditions and thus necessitate additional or more complex input parameters. We argue that this is not a peripheral puzzle but a structural mismatch between the formal language and the object language it describes. The remedy comes from using the category theoretic dimension of CHLC that has so far been neglected. Grounding meaning in the relational Yoneda perspective shifts the notion of identity from one of equal outputs to one of structural position: an expression means what it does by virtue of how it relates to everything else.

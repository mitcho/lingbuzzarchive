The Morphosyntactic–Semantic Unity of the Early Middle Japanese Realis Form Izenkei: the -ru ≈ -re allomorphy and the so-called kakari-musubi
Shinya Makino
September 2026
 

This study proposes a unified morphosyntactic–semantic account of the suffixal form -re found in the Early Middle Japanese realis paradigm — the so-called izenkei — and related phenomena. It argues that -re is a contextual allomorph of the suffix -ru, which marks a clause as [+ACT(UAL-WORLD-COMMITMENT)] and thereby presents the eventuality expressed by that clause as belonging to the actual world. It surfaces as -re when the clause is set as a premise. This premise-setting environment is supplied overtly by the suffixes -ba [+COND(ITIONAL)] and -do(mo) [+CONCESSIVE], and covertly by the phonologically null suffix -Ø [+PREM(ISE-SETTING)] in the =koso type of kakari-musubi. Thus, =koso does not itself condition the allomorphy -ru → -re, but merely focalises a constituent not containing the finite predicate within a clause already set as a premise by -Ø [+PREM]. In the =zo/=namu type, by contrast, a clause with a topic–comment articulation is first constructed, and its topic constituent containing the finite predicate is nominalised by -Ø [+NOM(INAL)] so that it can be treated as a topic NP. The clause is then set as a premise by -Ø [+PREM]. The subsequent addition of the focus particle =zo or =namu to the comment maps the inverted comment–topic configuration directly onto the focus–background configuration: the comment constitutes the focus and the nominalised topic the background. This type is therefore analysed as a COPULA-LESS INVERTED PSEUDO-CLEFT — the Japanese structural counterpart of an inverted wh-cleft — in which the comment/focus precedes the nominalised topic/background. Since the intervening -Ø [+nom] blocks the local relation between -ru and -Ø [+prem] required for the allomorphy, -ru remains unchanged. The =koso type, by contrast, contains no such nominalised topic phrase and is therefore NEITHER a PSEUDO-CLEFT NOR an IT-CLEFT. Accordingly, =koso occurs with -re, whereas =zo and =namu occur with -ru, simply because the two constructions have different morphosyntactic and information-structural configurations. The particles themselves do not determine the predicate forms. Rather, the configuration is constructed FIRST and only THEN determines both the predicate form and which focus particle may occur. Traditional particle–predicate concord called kakari-musubi is therefore reanalysed as a surface correspondence between two consequences of the same structure, constructed independently of the so-called concord.

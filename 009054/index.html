Chomsky's MERGE and the Duality of Semantics: Reconceptualising Copy Visibility under FormCopy
Evie Burrows
January 2025
 

This thesis critically examines recent developments in the Minimalist Program, focussing on Chomsky’s reconceptualisation of MERGE. I explore the duality of semantics, FormCopy (FC), and their interaction with labelling theory. I discuss two definitions of Duality: EM for theta-roles/argument structure + IM for discourse (Duality1), and EM for theta-positions + IM for non-theta-positions (Duality2). I address challenges to both versions, including discourse-related EM, IM unrelated to discourse, and EM to non-theta-positions. Duality2 and FC contribute to the copy/repetition/PRO distinction. FC forms copy relations between IM'ed and EM'ed elements, deriving Control. The empirical issues raised by this approach suggest inconsistencies in Chomsky’s work concerning which phenomena are visible to the syntax. The interaction between FC-theory and Preservation, another operation keeping track of copy relations, raises questions. I argue that ‘copy’ refers to multiple phenomena in MERGE, with inconsistencies about whether copies arise automatically from IM. This relates to questions surrounding the Markovian nature of MERGE, as syntactic operations (including FC) are Markovian, but those pertaining to interpretation (including Preservation) are not. Lastly, I examine the coherence of these ideas with labelling theory, identifying a conflict between FC-theory and copy invisibility in labelling theory. I propose that labelling conflicts are resolved by FC rather than by movement. Nevertheless, labelling theory presents semantic issues regarding copy invisibility and feature sharing. I adapt Bode’s (2020) analysis of copies as a potential solution, but without resolving these issues. I argue that this illustrates a fundamental flaw in labelling theory. Finally, I illustrate the problems arising when unifying FC-theory and labelling by applying my approach to structures which have been analysed in FC-theory.

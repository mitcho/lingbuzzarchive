Hindi-Urdu wh-scope markers license sluices: a new argument for indirect dependency
Malhaar Shah
April 2025
 

In Hindi-Urdu ‘wh-scope marking’ constructions, a morphologically independent wh-word kyaa ‘what’ appears in a matrix clause, to indicate interrogation of a wh-XP in an embedded clause. Theoretical debate has focused on their semantic relation to wh-movement constructions where the embedded wh-XP raises into the matrix clause. ‘Direct dependency’ theories treat the difference between scope-marking constructions and wh-movement constructions as a matter of the scope of the embedded wh-XP with respect to the embedding predicate (e.g., Manetta 2010). I introduce novel data from ellipsis that pushes in a different direction: wh-scope marking is semantically distinct from wh-movement in more ways than scope. It introduces explicit existential quantification over propositions. This is because scope-marking constructions are able to antecede more sluices than their parallel wh-movement constructions which do not have two wh-XPs. This, I argue, supports an ‘indirect dependency’ theory (as in Dayal 1996; Lahiri 2002), which gives the scope-marker a denotation that includes an existential quantifier over propositions. Under standard assumptions about sluicing, this explains the difference in the sluices scope-marking constructions can antecede.

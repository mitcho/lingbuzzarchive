The Name-Leash Hypothesis: Maternal Separation and the Origins of Displaced Reference
Sergey Maslov
September 2026
 

Displaced reference — the use of a signal for what is not in the perceptual field — is a defining feature of human language (Hockett 1960) and, as Bickerton (2009) argued, the central problem for any account of its origin. The standard scenario derives it from the need to recruit others to distant food; but where pointing, leading and pantomime would serve, an offline representational system is not obviously worth its cost. This paper proposes a different first case. In the cooperative-breeding arrangement reconstructed for early Homo (Hrdy 2009; Burkart et al. 2025), a mother handed her infant to an allomother and left to forage, so that mother and infant were repeatedly placed in perceptual situations that no longer specified one another. What is at issue is not distance but a partition between situations: how to go on holding one specific, non-fungible individual when one's own situation supplies no index of it. A perceptually grounded gesture has nothing to work on there; an individualized vocalization does. The first displaced signal, on this account, was a proper name rather than a food call. The semiotic core is the broken index. An index signifies through a connection that holds at the moment of signifying; when the referent goes, that connection lapses and cannot be restored by producing the signal again. What takes over its office is a sign of another type: a vocal form tied to one individual, which keeps the singularity of pointing while working without perceptual contiguity. Four stages that "proper name" otherwise runs together are separated — an infant-associated form, its displaced use by the mother alone, a mapping organized by the referent rather than by any caller, and a public name — with Kripkean rigid designation characterizing the last rather than the first. The token is relational at the outset, which makes the portable name the product of a first decomposition rather than an atom. On this reading the proper name is not a defective case within a system whose norm is the common noun, but the paradigm from which the principle of arbitrariness was tacitly generalized (Zimmerling 2025); and the relational handle the decomposition leaves behind is what seeds recombination. Evidence is drawn from cooperative breeding and life history, vocal-respiratory control, development, comparative vocal individuation in dolphins, chimpanzees and marmosets, and neural work on cross-modal identity representation. Three levels of decoupling are distinguished — from arousal, from here, and from now — and the mechanism is placed at the third, which the published comparative record does not document. The account is offered as a reconstruction, not a demonstration. This is version 4, revising version 3. The hypothesis is unchanged: §1.2 now states the mechanism in one pass, the four stages are separated, and the evidential status of each claim is marked.

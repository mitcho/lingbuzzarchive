Integrated versus independent processing of auditory features in speech sounds
Alex Chabot, Ellen Lau, Philip Monahan, Bill Idsardi
February 2026
 

Two MMN experiments investigate integrated versus independent processing of complex auditory information in linguistic sound. We hypothesise that neurophsyiologically evoked responses to multiple distinctive cues are stronger than evoked responses to single cues. We developed a novel methodology combining a fixed-deviant design with roving standards, allowing direct comparison of multiple deviant contexts within a single session. We ran two magnetoencephalographic (MEG) protocols which are identical except for the order of presentation of standard and deviant stimuli. We compare the results of the novel fixed-deviant design to a more traditional design which uses a fixed standard stimulus with a roving deviant. Our study asks if electrocortical techniques can be used to find feature additivity in the perception of linguistic sound, and tests the efficacy of a roving-deviant design versus a fixed-deviant design. Our results suggest that this novel methodology may be useful in testing questions of feature additivity. This work has applications relevant to both the cognitive neuroscience of language, as well as linguistic theory.

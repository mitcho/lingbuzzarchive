Current AI systems can detect ambiguities in a pre-registration but can offer misleading design-specific advice about random-effects structure
Shigeto Kawahara
April 2026
 

This study tests whether AI systems can identify underspecification in a pre-registration and provide appropriate design-specific methodological advice. In a pre-registered experiment, three AI systems (ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini) were presented with a deliberately ambiguous statistical analysis plan for a Bayesian mixed effects model, and asked for feedback. Each system was tested in two modes (fast/thinking or default/extended), with 10 trials per condition, yielding 60 total trials. The results reveal both a strength and a weakness of current AI systems. On the positive side, all three systems detected all four embedded ambiguities---i.e. unspecified random effects structure, vague prior specification, undefined posterior evaluation criteria and missing link function---without fail. On the negative side, the AI systems sometimes suggested by-item random slopes for condition despite the fact that items were nested within conditions. Error rates varied from 0% to 90% depending on system and mode. Interestingly, thinking/extended reasoning modes did not always improve recommendation accuracy for the case at hand. The stochastic nature of these misleading outputs exemplifies a particular peril---users cannot predict whether any single consultation will yield correct or incorrect advice. Throughout the paper, I underscore the importance of pre-registration by practice.

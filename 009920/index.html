Intransitive verbs: unergatives, unaccusatives, middles (part of: Syntax of Hungarian: Verbs and Verb Phrases)
Tamás Halm
April 2026
 

This chapter covers the syntactic, semantic and morphological patterns displayed by intransitive verbs in Hungarian. The discussion will be theory-neutral but informed by various theoretical approaches. No new theoretical claims will be made: rather, the aim is to provide a survey of the facts and to provide pointers to the existing theoretical literature. Our focus is on intransitive verbs: one-argument verbs which lack any arguments in addition to the the syntactic subject (since in Hungarian, nominals are overtly case-marked, and there is subject agreement, identifying the subject of a sentence is straightforward). We will broaden this definition to consider verbs which are arguable zero-argument: subjectless verbs (weather verbs and time-of-day verbs).

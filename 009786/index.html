Chain Reduction as substitution: A PF-based account of anti-locality
Marta Ruda
August 2026
 

In current minimalist theorizing, the elimination of copies created by Internal Merge is typically attributed to copy deletion, most prominently formalized by Nunes (2004) as Chain Reduction. This paper explores an alternative hypothesis that Chain Reduction proceeds via substitution rather than deletion. I show that this reconceptualization makes it possible to derive complement-to-specifier anti-locality effects (the ban on P-stranding) from labeling ambiguity that arises in head/head configurations (Chomsky 2013, 2015). Under the timing of the Labeling Algorithm adopted here, such anti-locality violations are predicted to be resolvable through ellipsis. While a detailed discussion of this point lies beyond the scope of this paper, data coming from a growing body of research on P-omission under sluicing and in fragment answers suggest that this indeed may be the case.

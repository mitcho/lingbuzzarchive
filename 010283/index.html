The Sound of Reference
Nirmalangshu Mukherji
September 2026
 

Noam Chomsky and his colleagues have proposed that human languages are basically designed for construction of inner thoughts so that individual humans can plan ahead, thus giving selectional advantage to the species. The sound part of language in this view is ancillary, it is meant only for the externalization of thought. Call it a ‘thought-only’ view of language. We challenge this view by showing that the thought-only conception of language cannot accommodate the referential function of languages. We probe the issue with a focus on what philosophers call Singular Terms that enable a language-user to pick out, identify, and draw attention to an individual object. The presence of singular terms in a human language not only gives a shape to human thoughts about individuals, it ensures that such thoughts are shared between conspecifics. Since such sharing cannot be accomplished without explicit articulation, the sound of singular terms is essential for human language, it is not ‘ancillary.’

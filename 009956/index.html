The Hand That Takes: Hittite dā- as pre-PIE *de + postposed -a, with iconographic evidence from Anatolian hieroglyph CAPERE
J. M. Chavis, A.I. Claude
May 2026
 

The first three papers of this project reconstructed pre-PIE *de- "hand, open palm" and decomposed both the body-part noun *der- and the give-verb *deh₃- into the same bare hand-element extended by different morphemes. The present paper carries the framework into Anatolian. We argue that Hittite dā- "to take" preserves a fossilized univerbation of pre-PIE *de- with a postposed directional element *a "toward (the speaker)," contracted by hiatus to long *ā in pre-Anatolian, at a stage before the verbal extension *-h₃- had been added. The argument's centerpiece is the Anatolian X+a parallel: *de + a > dā- fits a documented Anatolian morphological type rather than constituting an isolated speculation. The Hittite Set 1 directional system — anda < *en + a, katta < *ḱm̥t + a, parā < *pro + a, šarā < *uper + a — is a productive class of fixed adverbs and preverbs formed by univerbation of a local element with the directional particle *-a. The same *-a is the productive Hittite allative case ending. The standard view treats dā- as an isolated irregular ḫi-verb; the framework treats it as a regular member of a productive class and provides a structural source for the take/give polarity that the standard account handles only semantically. The framework's chronological architecture aligns with three independently published positions: Norbruis (2021) on "to take" as the original meaning of *deh₃-; Bauhaus (2019) on the directive *-o remaining productive into Anatolian while the locative *-r did not; and the four-paper architecture identifying three pre-Anatolian derivational outcomes on *de- that Anatolian preserves plus a fourth that is post-Anatolian. The Luwian lā- problem is engaged substantively: the standard derivation via *dh₃- > l- has no general sound law in Luwian to support it, depends on a contested glottalization framework, and rests on the most vulnerable point of laryngeal theory. The conditional probability is assessed at 30–42%.

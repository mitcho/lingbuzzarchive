Decomposing habituals
Furkan Dikmen, Ömer Demirok
April 2023
 

The goal of this chapter is to provide a nanosyntactic hierarchy of features for imperfective categories in natural language. Building on Starke’s (2021) proposal to derive progressives from habituals, we propose that habituals are not uniform but come in two shapes. Reporting data from languages that provide two formally distinct categories of habituals, we identify their semantic correlates. In particular, we argue that the habitual category that expresses incidental generalizations are structurally more complex, containing the habitual category that expresses rule-like generalizations that project into the future. Hence, the paper provides an empirically well-grounded enrichment on the fseq, making predictions about possible types of syncretisms in the domain of imperfectives.

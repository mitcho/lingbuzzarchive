Distributional Learning as Grammaticalization: Modals in the History of English
Charles Charles Yang, Carola Trips
July 2024
 

In this paper, we approach the phenomenon of grammaticalization from the perspective of child lan- guage. Building on recent findings in infant language research and learnability studies, we propose that syntactic categories are distributionally constructed by children: the classification of words into syn- tactic categories may change in response to changes in the linguistic data over time, resulting resulting in grammaticalization. Using the modal verbs in the history of English as a case study, we describe a distributional learning model that invokes the Tolerance Principle (Yang 2016), and its recursive appli- cation, as a formal mechanism for syntactic category formation. We show that throughout the history of English, the categorization model can reliably discover two distributional properties, the distribution of negation and inflectional morphology in the syntactic frame, that separate modals from lexical verbs and other auxiliaries. The placement of negation became especially salient after 1650 when the independent loss of verb movement was complete, which helps explain why, among the Germanic language family, only English developed a fully distinctive class of modal verbs. Taken together, our method and results provide fresh impetus for the long-standing research program that places child language acquisition at the center of language change.

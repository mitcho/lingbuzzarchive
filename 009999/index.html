Beyond clausal ellipsis: a relational approach to exceptive constructions
Ludovico Franco
June 2026
 

We defend a phrasal, relational analysis of exceptive markers against clausal-ellipsis analyses (Vostrikova 2021; Seguin 2024). Universal Grammar, we argue, makes available one dedicated device for exception: a relational head — an Axial Part in a Double-Relator configuration (Franco 2024) — that subtracts the exception from a nominal restriction and surfaces as a PP. A language either grammaticalises such a head (Italian tranne, English except) or approximates exception with a Boolean coordinator plus negation (Italian ma non, English but not), which fails to enforce containment. Containment is therefore our central diagnostic: a marker that imposes it cannot be the Boolean periphrasis and must be relational. We define a true exceptive semantically — by containment, the Quantifier Constraint, and the inference flip — derive that cluster from domain subtraction plus exhaustification with no silent clause, and show that the markers enforcing it have relational, not coordinating, morphosyntax: oblique case on pronominal Grounds, the strict NPI alcuno, and che as a category-sensitive D-linker rather than a complementizer. We further argue that a parte and oltre a are exceptive–additive duals, exceptive under negation and additive elsewhere. The account extends to English (but versus except) and to a typology of exception spanning macro-variation (Spanish versus Italian) and micro-variation (northern versus Tuscan Italian), rebutting Vostrikova's conditional analysis and Seguin's diagnostics for a clausal eccetto while conceding genuine clausal 'reserve' Grounds as CP complements of the relational head.

Analyzing discrepancy between post-festum and heuristic analogy in a real time pedagogical context
Atrayee Mukherjee
February 2026
 

This study explores how analogical reasoning in a multilingual Indian classroom reflects broader cultural linguistic and epistemic dynamics. It draws discourse in the classroom and post instructional interviews to highlight the contrast between heuristic analogies generated by students with post festum analogies introduced by instructors. Using a cultural-linguistic lens, the analysis examines how language ideologies form conceptual misalignments and therefore shape the learners access to canonical scientific knowledge. This study situates analogy not merely as a cognitive tool but a site of epistemic negotiation- where the prior experiences of the student, linguistic repertoires and socio-cultural positions influence how science is understood or resisted or reinterpreted. The findings highlight the need to recognize analogical reasoning as a culturally mediated practice that both enables and constraints participation in science education.

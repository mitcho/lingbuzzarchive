Restricting resolutions: effect-driven semantics of numerical approximation
Pavlo Kuchmiichuk
September 2026
 

Approximators such as approximately or exactly have been analyzed either as operators that manipulate a numeral's denotation, or as devices that select a granularity level at which the numeral is interpreted. A single numeral, however, can host several approximating devices at once. In Ukrainian, an overt adverb, a preposition, an indefinite, and approximative inversion may co-occur with one numeral, yet the result conveys the same interval as any one alone, differing only in an intensified hedging effect. Pragmatic halo theories correctly predict that imprecision in such cases does not compound, but fail to rule out mixed-precision stacks like approximately exactly; granularity-based accounts predict that stacking is infelicitous altogether. I propose an approach where a single mechanism derives the idempotence of same-precision stacking alongside the phenomena prior views miss. I argue that a bare numeral introduces a family of resolution descriptions—the precision levels at which it may be read—and approximators function like indefinites and definites over this family: approximately restricts it to its non-exact members without choosing among them, and exactly retrieves the unique exact member. Stacking is then intersection of conditions, idempotent for same-precision devices and contradictory for mixed-precision ones. I implement the account in effect-driven semantics and lift it to probabilistic dynamic semantics. Treating the choice of resolution as uncertain yields an interpretation peaked at the numeral; roundness and magnitude phenomena then reduce to how the prior over resolutions varies with it. The mixed-precision clash is rescued in discourse as an instance of ordinary anaphora.

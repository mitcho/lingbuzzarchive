Could the Ban on Improper Movement be about binding after all?
Peter Grishin
August 2026
 

I show that the approach to the distinct binding properties of A vs. Ā movement proposed by Sauerland (1998, 2004), Ruys (2000), and especially van Urk (2015) derives the Ban on Improper Movement (the generalization that Ā movement cannot feed A movement). I argue that this is a good result because the Ban on Improper Movement makes correct predictions, and moreover alternative generalizations like the Generalized Ban on Improper Movement/Williams Cycle and the Generalized A-after-Ā Constraint fail to generate certain kinds of attested syntactic phenomena (e.g. raising to object in some languages, Ā-movement-fed long-distance agreement).

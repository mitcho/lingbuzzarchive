The slur-epithet direction: the case of Italian
Andrés Saab, Jan Casalicchio
October 2025
 

We offer a new empirical argument from Italian in favor of the thesis that epithets (both in simple and bi-nominal configurations) contain an indexical ingredient. The argument is based on the observation that Italian, unlike other Romance languages like Spanish, forces the use of weak demonstratives with epithets. We show that the use of these demonstratives correlates with the presence of a variety of empty nominal anaphora constructions. Epithets, then, form a natural class with this variety; concretely, they contain an empty noun headed by an index. Slurs, instead, are regular nominal heads in the extended projections of DPs or related nominal phrases. Consequently, they are compatible with the full array of determiner heads that the language allows. From this perspective, the difference between epithets (i.e., pure expressive) and slurs (mixed expressive) boils down to a difference in syntactic structure. We argue that epithets, particularly those derived from slur words, are merged in the functional spine of a pronominal DP, whose nominal head is instantiated by a mere index for an assignment function ranging over individuals. We conclude by showing the importance of the weak demonstrative test to distinguish at least two types of qualitative binominal constructions in Italian.

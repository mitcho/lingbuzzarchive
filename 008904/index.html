Improper verb doubling
Qiuhao Charles Yan, Tom Meadows
March 2025
 

This paper presents novel data from Mandarin verb doubling to support the Williams Cycle (Williams 1974, 2003, 2011, 2013). We first demonstrate that long-distance verb doubling to a clause-medial position is only possible out of a limited class of embedding contexts, unlike its counterpart to the clausal periphery. Treating the doubling effects as consequence of VP-fronting, we propose that the varying locality profiles of verb doubling are constrained by the interaction between (i) the size of the embedding context from which the VP moves, and (ii) the height of the fronted VP’s landing site in the clausal spine. We show that this constraint is captured by a strong version of the Williams Cycle, and we derive it from the Level Embedding approach developed by Williams (2003), Poole (2022), and Meadows (2024). We further show that the proposed analysis can be extended to shed new light on long-distance object shift in Mandarin Chinese.

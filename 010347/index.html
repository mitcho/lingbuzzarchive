Saving FACE: Fragment answers, copy theory, and radical trace conversion
Ido Benbaji-Elhadad, Omri Doron
September 2026
 

Jacobson (2016) observes that fragment answers (e.g., Q: Which linguist is on the committee? A: Jill) must denote elements in the restrictor of the wh-phrase in the question immediately preceding them, while the corresponding element in an overtly clausal answer, possibly elided via VP-ellipsis, does not. This fact presents a challenge for an analysis of Fragments as Clausal Ellipsis, or FACE, according to which fragment answers are full clauses in which movement of the overt fragment to a left-peripheral position, followed by ellipsis of the vacated TP, silences everything but the moving fragment itself (cf. Merchant 2005 and references therein). This is because, prima facie, it is unclear what properties of movement and ellipsis might conspire to give rise to Jacobson's observation. We show that current FACE-compatible proposals for deriving this observation suffer from conceptual or empirical problems, and offer our own FACE-saving alternative, which we argue overcomes these problems.

In case "falls" is relevant
Magdalena Kaufmann
October 2024
 

We discuss the German conditional connective "falls" and offer an explanation for why its use is more restricted than the use of German "wenn" or English "if". Specifically, "falls" appears to be infelicitous in adverbially quantified conditionals and in subjunctive conditionals. We argue that "falls" presupposes that the question of whether the antecedent proposition holds is an open issue for an agent at an index. Various possibilities for satisfying the components of this presupposition (which agent(s), at what index/indices) explain different effects with "falls", and specifically why "falls" can sometimes occur in adverbially quantified conditionals and in subjunctive conditionals after all. We conclude with a comparison with purposive clause-style uses of "falls" and the behavior of closely related items in English and German ("in case"; "im Falle, dass"; "für den Fall, dass").

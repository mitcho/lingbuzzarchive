Atayalic subjects and the nature of nominative
Michael Yoshitaka Erlewine
January 2026
 

This paper investigates the nature of subjecthood properties in the Atayalic group of Austronesian languages of Taiwan. Subjects in these languages are associated with a clause-final, high structural position, topic properties, and nominative case. In particular, nominatives are morphologically reduced as compared to non-nominative case-marked nominals, as is common across so-called Philippine-type Austronesian languages. I therefore propose a model of Austronesian voice alternations where subject promotion involves structure-removal of the subject nominal’s K layer. What is traditionally described as nominative-marking, then, reflects the D head of a K-less nominal which occupies a topic position. The analysis is motivated further from the limited availability of nominative-marked non-subjects, attested in Squliq Atayal and Seediq.

The Motivated Sign in Arabic: Iconicity across Morphology and Grammar
Zoubir Dendane
June 2026
 

Abstract This article challenges the Saussurean arbitrariness principle by demonstrating that the Arabic linguistic sign is systematically motivated, and that the relationship between phonological form and meaning in Arabic is non-arbitrary at multiple simultaneous levels. Drawing on evidence from four interconnected domains, we advance a theoretical framework of phonosemantic iconicity in which articulatory properties of sounds iconically pre-figure semantic content. First, we demonstrate that the Arabic root-and-pattern verb form paradigm is diagrammatically iconic — the phonological operations of gemination, vowel extension, prefixation, and infixation enact the semantic operations they encode. Second, we show that the {m-}-prefix nominalization system is grounded in the universal articulatory iconicity of the bilabial nasal. Third, we examine a set of Arabic grammatical particles that show transparent formal and semantic correspondence with related lexical roots. Finally, we present a minimal pair analysis of /q/ ق and /ɣ/ غ as verb-initial consonants, demonstrating that contrastive phonetic gestures encode contrastive semantic matrices in a structured iconic opposition. Together, these four domains suggest that Arabic constitutes an unusually rich case of systematic iconic motivation in morphology and grammar, posing a significant challenge to strong interpretations of the Saussurean arbitrariness principle.

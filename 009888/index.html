The change specification problem
Kyle Gorman
February 2026
 

In recent work Gorman and Reiss (2026) give a formal definition to the feature specification problem (FSP), the problem of determining the natural class representations of the targets and triggers of phonological processes. They argue that the received wisdom---that the FSP is solved by finding the minimal (in terms of the number of valued features) specification---is incorrect, and suggest a simple alternative, in which natural class specifications are determined via intersection. In addition to targets and triggers, traditional phonological rules are also defined by the change, or the modification applied to the targets of the rule. In Logical Phonology (Bale et al. 2014, Bale and Reiss 2018, Bale et al. 20220), these changes are defined by an operator and its right-hand side argument, a set of valued (binary) features. However, whereas the proper solution to the FSP is contentious---see Gorman and Reiss 2026---the change specification problem (CSP) is comparatively simple. I provide a solution for LP's theory of possible rules, but the basic observations generalize to other theories, such as the rule system of classical generative phonology (e.g., Chomsky and Hale 1968).

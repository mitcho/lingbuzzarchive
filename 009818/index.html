The potential of kinematic analysis for gesture theory: A case study of intensified head nods
Rosa Fritzsche
March 2026
 

Quantitative motion-tracking methods are widely used in computational linguistics, psychology, and the cognitive sciences to measure fine-grained behavioral dynamics. By contrast, theoretical linguistics—in particular emerging work on gesture and multimodal meaning—has only recently begun to exploit their methodological potential. This paper argues that such tools are particularly well suited to addressing theoretical questions that turn on non-categorical, scalar variation in linguistic expression, where relevant distinctions are encoded in continuous properties of form, as is the case with gesture. As a case study, the paper investigates intensified head nods as gestural response elements in multimodal assertion. When used as standalone responses, head nods can function analogously to affirmative particles such as 'yes', yet their realization varies continuously in kinematic features such as amplitude, velocity, and number of repetitions. The central hypothesis is that this gradient modulation functions as a visual analogue to intensified response particles in speech (e.g., 'absolutely') by strengthening perceived speaker commitment. To test this hypothesis, nod intensity is operationalized through computational kinematic measurement and evaluated in a controlled perception experiment. The results show that increased kinematic intensification systematically correlates with stronger commitment attribution. This paper illustrates how computational approaches can support multimodal measurement in addressing theoretical semantic questions and advancing the empirical study of gesture.

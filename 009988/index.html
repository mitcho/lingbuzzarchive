Animacy, intention, and null objects in English
Raffaella Folli, Juliana Gerard, Heidi Harley, Balthazar Lauzon, Morgan Macleod
April 2026
 

It has long been known that the objects of at least some English verbs that are normally transitive can be omitted in at least some circumstances. In recent work, the animacy of the subject has emerged as a potential factor influencing sentence acceptability. While different theories make different predictions about where and how such effects emerge, little quantitative research exists on the extent and nature of animacy-related effects upon object omission. This paper presents a series of three experiments to elicit acceptability judgements on sentences differing in subject animacy and in the presence or absence of an overt object. It was found that not only are animacy and object presence significant factors in their own right, but a significant interaction between animacy and object presence also exists for at least some sentence pairs. It was also found that a similar degradation of object omission exists for animate subjects not in control of their actions, as opposed to animate subjects in control. This suggests that a key difference between animate and inanimate subjects may be the former’s intentional status.

ATB movement and non-structural Case in Modern Greek
Doreen Georgi, Lefteris Paparounas, Martin Salzmann
September 2025
 

Recent literature treats nominals bearing non-structural case either as ordinary DPs or as DPs encapsulated in a structural shell (PP or KP). This paper argues that both representations must be recognized in the grammar, since both co-exist within a single language. Novel observations from ATB movement in Greek reveal that the class of non-structural genitives is syntactically heterogeneous in this language: not all genitives can combine with each other in ATB movement, despite their systematic identity in form. The paper argues that assigning some genitives the status of plain DPs and others the status of PPs correctly accounts for the asymmetries in ATB movement, which is independently subject to a category-matching constraint in Greek. This analysis additionally allows for a unification of the ATB observations with previously noted asymmetries between the three non-structural genitive classes regarding cliticization, clitic doubling, and the availability of a PP alternant. Alongside the crucial categorial difference, this unified account relies on independently motivated thematic differences associated with the distinct genitive classes in Greek.

Ellipsis as leverage for dependent case theory
Yağmur Kiper
June 2026
 

This paper uses low sluicing in Turkish as a diagnostic for the timing of ellipsis licensing and structural case assignment. In low sluicing, wh-remnants bear case, auxiliary and tense morphology, but structural cases such as accusative and genitive fail to survive the ellipsis, even though they are present on the correlate in the antecedent clause. I argue that this disconnectivity cannot be reduced to a copular clause source: low sluicing allows multiple wh-remnants and adjunct remnants, and it is island-sensitive. Instead, I propose that low sluicing has a full clausal source, but that the ellipsis-licensing head renders its complement inaccessible for later case computation. Under a dependent case approach, this prevents wh-remnants from entering the configurational relations required for accusative or genitive case assignment, causing them to surface with unmarked nominative case. The analysis shows that ellipsis licensing can affect syntactic computation and provides new evidence for a configurational theory of structural case.

Long Distance Dependencies in Turkana
Leonie Barabas-Weil
August 2025
 

This dissertation investigates long distance movement and long distance dependencies in Turkana, an extremely under-researched Eastern Nilotic language spoken in Kenya. All relevant Turkana data come from original fieldwork. Turkana is a VSO language (Dimmendaal 1983, Finer 2013) with an optional pre-verbal focus position. This position has not been discussed in detail in the literature before. Wh-elements can either move to this position or remain in-situ. I demonstrate that wh in-situ does not undergo covert movement but it is truly in-situ. In contrast, wh-ex-situ is a result of movement and not because the wh-element is externally merged high in the syntactic structure. I show that the pre-verbal wh-position is not located in the specifier of CP, as usually assumed, but in the specifier of TP. Ex-situ and in-situ wh-elements exhibit the same locality patterns: They can move or be licensed via crossing a single CP but never more than one CP. This is a challenge for current theories of phasality. To account for this intricate data, I propose that only CPs function as phases in Turkana, and that phasality obeys PIC2 leading to a relatively weak version of phasality. I further adapt Keine's (2020) selected opacity approach making it more restrictive by allowing for very small horizons. Specifically, I propose that T and v in Turkana can carry probes with unrestricted horizons, while C carries an edge feature whose horizon is limited to V. This horizon mimics the locality restrictions of vP acting as a phase, but without the absolute locality constraints typically associated with full phasehood. This suggests that not only horizons, but also the very architecture of phasal domains, are subject to cross-linguistic variation. Turkana thus offers compelling evidence that the phase system is less rigid and more diverse than traditionally assumed. These findings highlight the importance of investigating a broader range of languages beyond the Indo-European languages to get a full understanding of locality restrictions.

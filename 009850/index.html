Argument ellipsis in Russian verb-stranding: an acceptability study of semantic constraints
Gleb Tumin
March 2026
 

Russian verb-stranding constructions, in which a finite verb appears without its internal argument, have been analyzed as instances of either Verb-stranding VP Ellipsis (VVPE) or Argument Ellipsis (AE). Recent work has established that AE is restricted to arguments of semantic type e, providing a diagnostic: if verb-stranding shows degraded acceptability with non-e-type arguments, AE rather than VVPE is the operative mechanism. This study extends prior experimental evidence for AE in Russian (Kasenov & Rudnev, to appear) by decomposing the non-e-type category into four subtypes: naming complement, predicative nominal, predicative adjectival, and argumental adverbial. In a pre-registered acceptability judgment experiment (N = 260), the e-type baseline (dp object) was rated significantly higher than three of the four non-e-type conditions, with medium-to-large effect sizes (d = 0.57–0.83). The contrast between dp object and predicative nominal did not reach significance (d = 0.25, p = .133); however, the post-hoc analysis indicates that the study was underpowered to detect effects of this magnitude, leaving open whether predicative nominals pattern with e-type or non-e-type arguments. The results of the study provide further support for AE as the primary analysis for Russian verb-stranding and raise the question of whether predicative nominals admit an individual-denoting reading that renders them compatible with argument ellipsis.

Tag questions
Craig Sailor
February 2026
 

This chapter consolidates and augments what is known about tag questions (TQs) of both the 'invariant' type (e.g. Kima is graduating, right?) and the 'dependent' type (e.g. Kima is graduating, isn't she?), focusing almost exclusively on their morphosyntax and crosslinguistic distribution. Progress is made in two major areas: (i) expounding the empirical and theoretical status of TQs as ellipsis phenomena, and (ii) confirming the status of tag clauses as polar questions based on the Q-marking strategies they involve crosslinguistically. Concerning (i): after reviewing and strengthening the arguments that dependent TQs involve predicate ellipsis, I sketch a novel analysis of invariant TQs as the product of clausal ellipsis. This provides a means of understanding why TQs distribute the way they do across languages, which I review in depth (particularly for dependent TQs, previously claimed to be rare crosslinguistically). Several theoretical issues arise from this, including the validity of TQs as structural and/or ellipsis diagnostics, their potential relevance to the ongoing V-stranding ellipsis debate, etc. Concerning (ii), I confirm the interrogative status of tag clauses with a novel microtypology drawn from Northwestern Kuki-Chin languages: tag clauses are marked like polar questions in each language, even though the languages all differ from each other in the type of marking strategy they use. Along the way, several directions for future research are identified, e.g. identifying the necessary vs. sufficient conditions for a language to have dependent TQs, and the implications of tag-specific morphology for the analysis of tag clauses.

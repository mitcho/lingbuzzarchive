The reconstructed pronunciations of the Middle Chinese Yu initial, Ri initial, lingual initials, and the Zhi rhyme group, along with their synchronic retentions and diachronic reflections in Chinese dialects
Yanguang炎光 Guo郭
August 2026
 

During the investigation of Chinese dialect speech databases, it is discovered that in southeastern dialects, a set of voiced fricatives is preserved in the pronunciation of the Yu initial (喻母) and Ri initial (日母) characters,should be identitied as voiced palatal fricative & labialized voiced palatal fricative,and a voiced fricative with a similar manner of articulation but a more anterior release position. Based on a comparative analysis of Yu initials transcriptions in the Menggu Ziyun and Dunhuang Sino-Tibetan materials, the retained pronunciations of Yu initials characters in other Chinese dialects, and the topological position of the Yu initials in the Yunjing, the author proposes that the Middle Chinese Yi initials(以母) should be reconstructed as labialized voiced palatal fricative, Yun initials(云母) reconstructed as voiced palatal fricative,Ri initials(日母)reconstructed as a voiced anterior laminal alveolar fricative, distinct from [z], [ʒ], and [ʑ]. Given the numerous instances of variant readings (异读) between Yi initial and Ding initial (定母) in the Guangyun, and based on archaeological philological findings such as the theory that "Yu si initial originates from Ding initial" (喻四归定), it can be inferred that labialized voiced palatal fricative (the reconstructed pronunciation of Yi initial) evolved from voiced palatal africate, therefore, the latter is the reconstructed pronunciation of Ding initial. Furthermore, since the initials of Zhi group (知组) are in complementary distribution with the third and fourth division (三四等) initials of Duan group (端组), it can be inferred that the Middle Chinese initials of Zhi group correspond to sounds of palatal africate group. Based on the phonetic transcription records in the Dunhuang Chinese-Tibetan transcriptional materials, it can be inferred that although the majority of Duan group initials (端组) in the Northwestern dialect of the Tang and Five Dynasties period had already evolved into dental/alveolar plosives, a small number of labialized voiced palatal africate still remained. At the same time, this paper examines the transcription methods of the Middle Chinese Yu initial (喻母), Ri initial (日母), and the lingual sounds in the Dunhuang Chinese-Tibetan transcriptional materials and the Menggu Ziyun (蒙古字韵). In addition, this paper also explores the reconstructed pronunciations of the four rhymes Zhi (支), Zhi(脂), Zhi(之), and Wei(微) under Zhi rhyme group (止摄) in the Qieyun system: Zhi (支) rhyme is apical vowel of Yu initials, Zhi(之) rhyme is apical vowel of Ri initial, Zhi(脂) rhyme is apical vowel of Shu and Xin initials(书母、心母), Wei(微) rhyme is close front unrounded vowel [i]. The Grade III final of the zhi rhyme group is precisely the fricative vowel /iɿ/ or /iz/ commonly found in Wu dialects. Apart from its retention in Chinese dialects, it is also recorded in Dunhuang Tibetan-Chinese transcriptions, the Menggu Ziyun (Phags-pa Script Dictionary), and the Xiru Ermu Zi (西儒耳目资), where it is transcribed using the Tibetan letter ཨེ, the 'Phags-pa letters ꡠ、ꡭ, and the Latin letter ei respectively. This paper also discusses when the e initial(额母) entered the Middle Chinese phonological system and its subsequent evolution, and based on this, identifies the phonological characteristics of Tang dynasty official language and its influence on Chinese dialect phonologies.

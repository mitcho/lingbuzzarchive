Dimension Classifiers Mediating Nominal Modification
Morozova Irina, Sjef Barbiers
January 2026
 

We present a novel analysis of attributive modification patterns like a green colour car, based on new data primarily from Dutch and Russian. We argue that the nominal element colour is a Dimension Classifier (DimCl) and is in a possessive part-whole relation with the noun car. These constructions show properties of inalienable possession. We show why DimCls cannot be viewed as silent nouns (contra Kayne 2005) and argue that these structures start out as of-modification of the type a car of green colour, with car the possessor. We propose that the postnominal constituent optionally moves to a prenominal functional projection that turns mass nouns into count nouns (i.e., SpecDivP; Borer 2005). This explains the novel observation that DimCl is impossible with mass nouns and pseudopartitives. We introduce a battery of tests to distinguish between a DimCl and a lexical N in these postnominal constituents and hold that DimCl involves nouns that pick an objective inherent dimension of the modified noun and must be overtly licensed. Being able to occur in the prenominal modification structure is a distinguishing property of DimCls. Kind nouns, although allowed prenominally, are shown not to belong to DimCls. Based on the divergent behaviour of material, origin and other lower modifiers (cf., the adjectival hierarchy of Scott 2002), we show that these modifiers contain a bound DimCl that is generated directly in SpecDivP, without involving a possessive relation. Finally, we present an overview of various semantic classes of modifiers showing their distribution in relation to DimCls.

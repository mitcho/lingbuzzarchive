Chuzhditsa: a phonological citation register for Cyrillic
Anastas Stoyanovsky
July 2026
 

Between ordinary orthography and specialist transcription lies a layer that alphabets fill only patchily: a conventional way to write a foreign word in the native script so that a reader can recover an adequate approximation of its source pronunciation without technical training. No major Slavic Cyrillic orthography occupies that layer -- foreign words enter through ad hoc transcription, with systematic indeterminacy as the result (Bulgarian Вашингтон ~ Уошингтън, Данчев 1982). We present Chuzhditsa (Bulg. чуждица, 'foreignism'), a computable, Cyrillic-based phonological citation register for foreign words, names, and pedagogical transcription. Japanese katakana proves that a visually distinct foreignness register can operate at the scale of a national orthography -- though katakana adapts loans into native phonology, where a citation register must extend the inventory to preserve source contrasts. Chuzhditsa supplies (i) a compositional grammar of diacritic operations deriving letters for non-Slavic phonemes from attested Cyrillic material -- revivals, imports, and rule-derived residue; (ii) a prosodic layer for stress, tone, and length; and (iii) a register-marking typeface whose substitution and positioning rules (GSUB/GPOS) are the orthography's fusion and attachment rules, executable rather than merely described. We evaluate coverage against twenty languages under declared reference varieties and a three-way classification (exact, conventionally merged, declared loss), generalize the Bulgarian pilot to the Slavic Cyrillic superset with an external stress test on Albanian, and offer the laryngeal series' articulatory-to-visual gradient as a candidate heuristic for alphabet extension.

Measurement-shifting in the syntax: A rational solution to an agreement-interpretation puzzle?
Cooper Roberts
July 2026
 

Fraction partitives which contain a plural argument (e.g., "a third of the apples") exhibit a semantic alternation tracked by verbal agreement in some but not all Indo-European languages. One of the readings, which I call a MEASURE interpretation, is unexpected given established perspectives on how measure functions are defined for pluralities. I work to offer an account of the phenomenon specifically for Italian but secondarily seek to explain why the additional reading is absent in other languages. Building off of a suggestion from Falco & Zamparelli (2019), I resolve the alternation to the presence or absence of a covert operator which (i) is Agreed with over the partitive complement, and (ii) shifts a parameter of interpretation by which variable measure functions are defined. While a non-trivial deviation from established literature on the semantics of measurement (see Bale & Barner 2009, Wellwood 2019, a.o.), I argue it crucially outperforms alternatives in predicting where the interpretations may arise and what kinds of predicates Italian FPs are compatible with, among other things.

Selection and Adjunction
Hedde Zeijlstra
August 2026
 

Abstract: This article develops a theory of syntactic selection and adjunction (based on Zeijlstra 20920) in which labeling follows directly from Merge and feature percolation, rather than from an independent labeling algorithm. Syntactic objects carry two types of formal features: independent features, which determine categorial status, and dependent features, which encode syntactic dependencies. When two objects merge, matching independent and dependent features under sisterhood do not percolate, whereas all other features do. This derives projection by selection while also accounting for adjunction: adjuncts are analyzed as elements that select their hosts while preserving their categorial status. Extending the analysis to PPs and clausal arguments yields a restricted theory of c-selection in which verbs syntactically select DPs (including nominalized clauses), whereas PPs and non-nominalized clauses function as predicate modifiers that select the verbal expressions they combine with. Finally, the proposal unifies local selection with long-distance Agree. Whereas independent features cannot percolate beyond their maximal projections, dependent features may percolate until they enter a local sisterhood relation with a matching independent feature. Upward Agree thus reduces to postponed local selection. The resulting architecture provides a unified account of labeling, selection, adjunction, and Agree.

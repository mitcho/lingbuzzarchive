Restrictions on the formation of stative passives
Alison Biggs, David Embick
August 2025
 

This paper examines restrictions on English stative passive participles, which at first appear to be Root-specific (e.g. √SNEEZE, √LAUGH cannot form stative passives, while √FLATTEN, √EMPTY can). We argue instead that their distribution follows from structural and interpretive constraints. Specifically, the Stative Passive Theme Restriction requires stative passives to denote a complex change of state whose argument must be interpretable as a Theme. A second principle, No Agent/Theme Adding/Changing, blocks coercion of stative passive participle arguments into Agent roles, ruling out unattested dual-role readings (e.g. *Wanda is smoked thin). Our analysis adopts a 'small' syntax for stative passive participles in which arguments are introduced by i*. The approach captures a number of otherwise unexplained effects without appealing to derivational relationships between participles, while clarifying limits of contextual coercion in thematic interpretation.

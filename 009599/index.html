Deriving clause incorporation in Inuktitut
Michelle Yuan
September 2026
 

While previous research on incorporation has mainly focused on how (typically uninflected) nouns come to be incorporated into verb complexes, it is less well known that some languages also permit the incorporation of complex verbal constituents (e.g. Pittman, 2009; Barrie and Mathieu, 2016; McKenzie, 2022). In this paper, I investigate a previously underdescribed construction in Inuktitut (Inuit-Yupik-Unangan), in which an embedded clause is morphologically incorporated into a higher affixal verb of possession 'have', creating an existential. The embedded nominal arguments are stranded and case marked like antipassive objects rather than with the expected ERG and ABS morphology. I argue that the incorporated element is a pseudorelative clause, a CP-sized predicate of a small clause related to its nominal subject by control (e.g., Cinque 1995), based on structural parallels with pseudorelatives found cross-linguistically. Clause incorporation as opposed to noun incorporation arises in Inuktitut because the incorporating verb takes the entire pseudorelative structure, rather than a nominal, as its complement; this, in turn, motivates a postsyntactic model of incorporation that requires the syntactic structure as its input (e.g. Compton and Pittman, 2010; Yuan, 2025). Additionally, the case patterns found in incorporated clauses reveal that both ERG and ABS case assignment are tied to the extended CP domain (e.g. Compton, 2018; Carrier, 2020; Yuan, 2022), and are not associated with lower clausal fields such as the vP and TP domains.

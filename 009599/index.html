Deriving clause incorporation in Inuktitut
Michelle Yuan
December 2025
 

While previous research on incorporation has mainly focused on how (typically uninflected) nouns come to be incorporated into verb complexes, it is less well known that some languages also permit the incorporation of complex verbal constituents (e.g., Pittman 2009, Barrie and Mathieu 2016, McKenzie 2022). In this paper, I investigate a previously underdescribed construction in Inuktitut (Inuit-Yupik-Unangan), in which an embedded clause is morphologically incorporated into a higher affixal verb, stranding its nominal arguments. I argue that the incorporated clause is a pseudorelative CP, a clausal predicate of a small clause related to its nominal subject by control (e.g., Cinque 1995), based on structural parallels with pseudorelative clauses cross-linguistically. Clause incorporation as opposed to noun incorporation arises because the incorporating verb takes the entire pseudorelative structure as its complement. This follows from Yuan’s 2025 theory of Inuktitut incorporation applying to structurally adjacent heads, and provides a novel empirical challenge to the phasal theory of polysynthetic wordhood of Compton and Pittman 2010 (which does not readily predict CP incorporation). Beyond incorporation, the case patterns found in incorporated clauses reveal that both ERG and ABS case assignment are tied to the extended CP domain (e.g., Yuan 2021; Carrier and Compton to appear), and are not associated with lower projections such as vP and TP.

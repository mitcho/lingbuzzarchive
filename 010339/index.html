Embedded Reciprocal Equatives in Mandarin and Semantic Weakening
I-Ta Chris Hsieh
July 2026
 

In this paper I present a novel observation on reciprocal equatives (henceforth, RE) in Mandarin: a reciprocal equative, when embedded under a necessity modal or a predicate of desire, gives rise to an unexpected, at-least-equal-to interpretation (henceforth, ≥-interpretation). I show that the meaning of a modal sentence or a desire report of this kind may be derived through the postulation of two covert operators: one is akin to at least and serves to weaken the meaning of the embedded RE; the other is an exhaustification operator, which gives rise to the inference of sufficiency. Along with Alonso-Ovalle and Hirsch’s (2022) analysis, the proposal draws a connection between the sufficiency modal construction (e.g., To get good cheese, you only have to go to the North Ends) and examples of embedded reciprocal equatives investigated in this paper. To the extent that the proposal is on the right track, it provides further support for the postulation of a semantic weakening operator in natural language meaning composition and sheds light on the issue how this weakening operation may be constrained (e.g., Alonso-Ovalle and Hirsch, 2022; Crniˇc, 2011; Schwarz, 2005).

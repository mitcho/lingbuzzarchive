A Nanosyntactic account for the opaque morphology of Tibetic verbs
Kitty Wenying Liu
July 2026
 

The verbal morphology of Classical Tibetan is notoriously complex, where a com- bination of concatenative and non-concatenative processes create paradigms of stem alternants with opaque many-to-many form-function mappings, where TAM features appear to be ex- poned by overlapping and discontinuous phonological material. This paper represents the first attempt at providing a generative, decompositional analysis of the most morphophonologi- cally complex Classical Tibetan verb paradigm (illustrated with √kab ‘to cover’), and of its di- achronic development into Themchen, a modern Amdo Tibetic variety. This analysis uses the Nanosyntax (NS) framework, where I argue that the lexicalisation algorithm can generate the paradigm’s overlapping and discontinuous exponence for free. Overall, this paper presents a case study for giving a decompositional analysis to phenomena that appear to resist it, and the utility of decompositional models that admit an intervening level of representation between form and function. By applying NS to data that it has not seen before, this paper also explores the consequences for prefix-suffix interaction within NS.

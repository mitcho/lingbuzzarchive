A Theoretical Architecture for Cyclic Prosodification
Nadja Fiebig, Paula Fenger, Sören E. Tebay, Philipp Weisser
May 2025
 

We put forward a strictly cyclic model of the syntax-prosody interface in which syntactic structures are prosodified in a stepwise fashion as soon as the respective terminal nodes receive phonological content, i.e. during Vocabulary Insertion. Going from there, we present two case studies from two radically different empirical phenomena where this property makes the precisely the right predictions: verbal number reduplication and shifting coordinators. In both of these case studies, we contrast two syntactic heads, which are very local to each other and trigger the same process that refers to prosodic domains (partial vs full reduplication or prosodically determined second position effects of clitics). Crucially, even though these heads are syntactically quite local to each other, they still differ with respect to their position above or below a cyclic node. And in both cases, we find crosslinguistically robust generalizations that process triggered by the higher syntactic head can refer to a bigger prosodic constituent than the process triggered by the lower syntactic head. What we take this to mean is that the bigger prosodic constituent is only constructed (and therefore becomes accessible) upon hitting the cyclic node. This, in turn, means that prosodic structure must be built cyclically and in a bottom-up way. We argue that non-cyclic, representational theories cannot account for these phenomena in a principled way.

Varieties of headless relative clauses in Tigrinya
Gioia Cacchioli, Ivano Caponigro
March 2026
 

This paper presents the first systematic description of headless relative clauses in Tigrinya (Semitic; Eritrea and Ethiopia; SOV). Drawing on novel fieldwork data, it examines three types of headless relatives, namely light-headed, super-free, and free-choice relatives. The data show that, unlike many other languages, Tigrinya forms both headed and headless relative clauses using the same morphosyntactic strategy, which involves the relativizer z(ɨ)- without overt wh-elements. Headless relatives in Tigrinya do not rely on interrogative wh-structures, in contrast to languages such as English. Notably, free relatives are not attested. Instead, all headless relative clauses share a structural core with headed relatives, differing only in the presence or absence of nominal and determiner layers. By documenting this pattern, the paper identifies a previously underdescribed strategy in Afro-Asiatic. These findings refine the typology of relativization, provide empirical evidence for cross-linguistic variation in relative clause formation, and establish a foundation for formal analyses of Tigrinya and related languages.

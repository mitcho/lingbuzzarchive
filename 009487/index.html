Bei and Bei Passives
Shuangzhou Xu
February 2026
 

Since the existing theoretical models of generative grammar are far from perfect, generativists must adopt a cautious attitude towards them. Fraught with internal contradictions, they do not need to be refuted by Construction Grammar or large language models; they confuse themselves. For the Minimalist Program, there is a tension between the acquired nature of the lexicon and the innate status of syntax. Such a tension is not fully solved in Distributed Morphology. In DM, meaning is supposed to be obtained rather late in the derivation, while it is obvious that morphemes have syntactico-semantic features. Lexicon is claimed to be distributed, but it seems that lexicon is everywhere. We need to reinterpret and refine these models. In this thesis, DM is considered to characterise a mechanism of de-Merge. Meanwhile, MP and DM are treated as models for two different stages of competence. In addition to generative studies of Chinese grammar, one should also consult the work of functionalist scholars to gain a typological and historical understanding of the language. I hope that someone can explore further the possibility of top-down de-Merge because I do not have enough time. The thesis is a generative study of the categorial status of bei in Standard Chinese and the derivation of its related passive structures. It first covers the fundamentals of Chinese grammar and provides an overview of previous research on bei and bei passives. This enables the reader to develop a preliminary understanding of this passive structure and empowers them to form their own judgments. By taking a progressive view of language competence and drawing upon insights from different theoretical frameworks of generative grammar, the thesis aims to provide an explanation for a dynamic view of the categorial status of bei and how it can function as a lexical verb, a functional verb, or a preposition under different circumstances. In the context of grammaticalisation as a result of competence development, this study diverges from prior research primarily in the proposition of the categorial status of bei being subject to conditioned computation. Such an approach is expected to shine new light on our understanding of bei and somehow unify the analysis of the derivation of bei passives featuring such issues as the chameleonic character showcasing both control and raising hues, the mono- or bi-clausal controversy, the difference between long and short bei passives, etc.

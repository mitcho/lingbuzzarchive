Licensing ACC-Marked External Possession in Korean
Min-Joo Kim
September 2025
 

This paper looks at the accusative (ACC) marked external possession construction (EPC) in Korean, seeking to identify its licensing conditions, an outstanding problem in the literature. I propose that ACC-marked EP sentences in Korean are licensed if they answer the question under discussion (QUD) and if the possessum (PM) represents what I call the Target of the event under description in which the possessor (PR) is the Theme or if the PR is what I call the Receiver of the event in which the PM is the Theme. I also propose that there are at least three structural possibilities for ACC-marked EP sentences which correlate with the argument status of the PR and the PM at issue and the inalienable versus non-inalienable possession relation that holds between them. The proposed analysis will be shown to account for various properties of the ACC-marked EPC in ways that improve on the existing analyses including the affectedness-based analyses. It also provides reasons to treat truly inalienable EPCs differently from non-truly inalienable ones even in languages like Korean.

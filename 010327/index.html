Experimental evidence for partial conjunct agreement in Turkish
Özge Bakay, Faruk Akkus
September 2026
 

In several languages, when a head agrees with a conjoined phrase, it may express either the sum of the features of all conjuncts (i.e., full agreement) or the features of only a subset of conjuncts (i.e., partial agreement). Investigations into (partial) agreement with conjunction have played a crucial role in linguistic theorizing of agreement. Here we aim to contribute to this literature by experimentally investigating verbal agreement strategies with coordinate and disjunctive subjects in Turkish. Using three acceptability judgment tasks, we test the availability of partial verbal agreement with preverbal and postverbal coordinate subjects and preverbal disjunctive subjects. For coordinate subjects, we find that full agreement is the most acceptable strategy, while partial agreement with the last (or second) conjunct ‘I’ is somewhat available in both word orders. For disjunctive subjects, acceptability is overall degraded, suggesting a grammatical gap in assigning number with disjunction. Nevertheless, similar to coordination, full agreement remains the most acceptable option, followed by partial agreement with the last conjunct.

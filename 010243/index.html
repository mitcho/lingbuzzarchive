Translation Literacy in Asylum Interpreting: A Pilot Study of TRACE and Structured Reflection
Mohammad Shehadeh
August 2026
 

Interpreting quality can shape how an asylum applicant's account is understood, while UK inspection and guidance documents continue to identify challenges involving language matching, interpreter preparation and quality assurance. This pilot study operationalises translation literacy as explicit reflection on translation choices, semantic range, alternatives, evidence, consequences and evaluation. Twenty-one Arabic-English bilingual adults completed unguided pre- and post-framework translation-evaluation tasks after guided exposure either to TRACE, a five-step mnemonic reflection scaffold, or to an active structured control checklist. Eighty-four responses were scored on a six-dimension rubric by the original marker and three independent blinded raters; analyses used the mean of the four ratings. TRACE did not produce a statistically significant advantage over control on combined change scores (TRACE +1.48/24; control +2.33/24; U=47.0, p=.597). Alternatives showed the clearest TRACE-aligned descriptive pattern, but the between-group change difference was non-significant. The findings therefore do not establish TRACE superiority or a general causal effect of structured reflection. They instead identify a feasible measurement approach and hypotheses for larger, better-balanced studies using professional interpreters, delayed retention testing and spoken high-stakes tasks.

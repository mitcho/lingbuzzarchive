The Syntax of Wh-in-situ
Wei-Tien Dylan Tsai
February 2025
 

Since Huang's (1982) seminal work on comparative syntax, the research on the structure and properties of in-situ wh-expressions has gone a long way. This chapter distinguishes four types of wh-constructions in terms of Q(uestion)-operator binding and the morpho-syntactic makeup of individual languages. It is pointed out that building a wh-dependency in a language without wh-movement is driven by interpretive needs, and can be achieved through covert movement in LF, unselective binding, Q-particle raising, or invisible operator movement in core syntax. In particular, an operator-variable pair can be built on a sentential scale in a robust analytic language such as Chinese. As a result, the quantificational dependency is established not through movement, but through (unselective) binding, which licensing strategy is correlated to the extensive usage of indefinite/polarity wh’s across the board. In addition, various factors contributing to the asymmetries of wh-construals are laid out to account for an assortment of locality effects observed across languages, including argument vs. adjunct, referential vs. nonreferential, noun vs. adverb, D-linked vs. non-D-linked, phrase vs. feature, and sentential vs. verbal. Furthermore, the notion of “operator-in-situ” has become a prominent feature in the recent literature to characterize a class of wh-adverbials, which not only take a sentential scope, but also express eventuality causation according to their “height of interpretation”. The usage typically involves why- and how come-like expressions, sometimes bordering on the speaker-oriented construals in the top layer of the left periphery. The role-playing nature of wh-in-situ thus leads to a deeper understanding of the inner working of UG, as to how the convergence of a quantificational dependency can be ensured for a particular operator-variable pair in a particular language.

Grammatical tone in African languages
Lee S. Bickmore
May 2026
 

In this chapter, we examine grammatical tone data from approximately thirty different African languages from all over the continent, organized for convenience around the four Greenbergian phyla Niger-Congo, Nilo-Saharan, Afro-Asiatic, and Khoisan. We start by establishing the key differences between lexical and grammatical tone, and then show that any inflectional or derivational property which can be expressed by segmental morphemes can also be expressed by tone. One important patterning is that grammatical tone on its own might signal the grammatical property being conveyed, while in other cases it might be one of several co-exponents of the grammatical expression. We also lay out the distinct ways grammatical tone can be implemented, e.g. addition, subtraction, replacement, shifting, exchanging, spreading, among others. In highlighting the myriad grammatical properties that grammatical tone is used to express, we emphasize that accurate documentation and description is absolutely indispensable for a full understanding of most African languages.

A Better Hand: The PIE contrastive *-tero- and the pre-PIE hand-noun
J. M. Chavis, A.I. Claude
May 2026
 

The PIE contrastive suffix *-tero- is, in its oldest documented function, the systematic morpheme for spatial-direction marking across the Indo-European family. Almost every PIE local particle has a *-tero- derivative naming its spatial contrastive — Latin citra/ultra/intra/extra/contra, Sanskrit ádhara-/uttara-/ápara-/ántara-, Avestan and Old Persian fratara-/apatara-/upatara-, Hittite kattera-, Greek próteros/hústeros. The Handbook of Comparative and Historical Indo-European Linguistics states explicitly that outside Greek and Indo-Iranian this spatial-particle function is the original PIE function; the gradational "more X" comparative is a Greek and Indo-Iranian innovation. This paper develops two parallel derivational outcomes on the same pre-PIE root. The contrastive suffix *-tero- is reconstructed compositionally as *de- + *-r- + *-o- with regular suffix-initial devoicing of *d- to *t-, where *de- is the polyfunctional pre-PIE hand-element documented in Paper 1 and *-r- is the locative element documented in Paper 3. The numeral *du̯o-/du̯i- "two" is reconstructed as a third derivational outcome — *de- + *-u̯- + *-o-/-i- — paralleling the structure of *deiu̯-o- "god." Dunkel (2014, Lexikon der indogermanischen Partikeln, s.v. *u̯í) supplies the key published evidence: his etymology unifies the prefixal *u̯i- and the numeral *du̯i- under a single morpheme dissimilated in compound first-member position, and his reconstruction of the inherited compound *u̯i-tero- — attested as Vedic vítarám, Avestan vítara-, and Old Church Slavonic vŭtorŭ "the second" — brings the two derivational siblings together with the predicted binary-ordinal semantics. Hittite kattera- anchors the OCP phonology; a distributional test across the inherited PIE positional adjective paradigm argues against the "irregular extension" framing of earlier treatments. The conditional probability is assessed at 35–45%.

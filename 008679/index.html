Unscrambling German parasitic gaps
Timea Szarvas
December 2024
 

Parasitic gaps are discussed by Fanselow (2001) in the context of diagnosing movement properties of scrambling in German. Although this is not what most readers will remember the paper for, the relevant two pages offer a rather unique and easily mischaracterized idea for the treatment of what is (not) to be considered a parasitic gap. Touching on my own experimental work, I flesh out the idea implied by Fanselow that there are two types of parasitic gaps in German: genuine ones licensed by canonical cases of A'-movement, and pseudoparasitic ones arising alongside scrambling.

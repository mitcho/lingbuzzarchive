Two is better than one: Subject DPs in Spanish third person plural arbitrary sentences
John David Storment
June 2025
 

Spanish has an arbitrary construction with no overt subject and obligatory plural phiagreement on the verb. When a subject-oriented secondary depictive predicate adjective is added to this construction, the adjectival agreement is optionally singular for some speakers. This number agreement mismatch reveals that this construction actually involves two distinct subjects: an implicit argument agent as well as an expletive structural subject. I demonstrate that this agreement optionality for number on secondary predicates is a result of a process known as defective circumvention (Storment 2025b), by which a phi-probe can conditionally Agree past a featurally deficient goal to obtain features from a non-deficient goal. In this construction, the deficient goal is the implicit argument agent (Collins 2024), and the non-deficient goal is a plural null expletive pronoun. I further show that the optionality of agreement in these clause types is a result of a configuration which fails to fully satisfy economy conditions in the syntactic derivation of these clauses. This paper has interesting implications for the syntax of agreement optionality, expletives, and arbitrary subjects.

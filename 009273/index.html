The curious case of the two-headed phrase: The symmetrical syntax of co-compounds
Lena Borise, Tamás Halm
August 2025
 

Co-compounds (CC) are pairs of morphosyntactically parallel and semantically related elements that constitute a single morphosyntactic and semantic unit. Usually described as a type of (asyndetic) coordination in the typological literature, they have only received sporadic attention from generative syntacticians, and have mostly been analysed as a subtype of exocentric compounds (Scalise, Fábregas & Forza 2009). In contrast to this previous analysis, based on data from two Uralic languages (Hungarian and Surgut Khanty), we argue that co-compounding is a syntactic operation: an instance of two heads undergoing Merge and being dominated by a shared layer of functional projections. While an implicit assumption of endocentricity (Chomsky 1970) and the projection principle (Chomsky 1993) has been that it is exactly one head that heads and projects a phrase, we argue that as long as a few conditions (that CCs are subject to) are met, the existence of such two-headed phrases is unproblematic for the standard understanding of these two core concepts. We also address the issue of labelling that emerges when two heads undergo Merge in a symmetric fashion. We model the semantics of CCs in terms of substitution by a shared superordinate term plus universal or existential closure. We also address variation between Hungarian and Surgut Khanty in terms of (i) the required degree of semantic relatedness between the two elements and (ii) the availability of dual/plural marking and a conjunctive interpretation; we relate this variation to the very different functional load that co-compounding carries in the two languages.

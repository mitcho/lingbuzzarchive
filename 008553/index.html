Preverbal ge- in Old and Middle English: Resultativity, Aktionsart and Viewpoint Aspect
Thomas McFadden
December 2023
 

This paper aims to advance our understanding of the role of preverbal ge- in Old English (henceforth OE) and its disappearance in Middle English. Characterizing the distribution and the morphosyntactic and semantic role the prefix played in OE, as well as ts diachronic development, have proven to be extremely difficult. I approach these questions by pursuing two strategies simultaneously. First, we can vastly improve the empirical basis for discussion by taking advantage of the parsed electronic corpora that have become available in recent decades. Second, our analysis can be profitably informed by taking into account developments in the theoretical treatment of verbal prefixes and of the array of related but distinct grammatical categories often confused under the label aspect, and of how these categories are marked in other ways in OE and ME. The paper thus first reports on the empirical patterns that emerge from a large-scale corpus study using the York-Toronto- Helsinki Parsed Corpus of Old English Prose and the Penn-Helsinki Parsed Corpus of Middle English, 2nd ed. Building on this, it then develops a theoretical hypothesis about the morphosyntax and semantics of ge- in OE which explains the broad outlines of its distribution and also covers some otherwise surprising points of detail involving particular lexical verbs, clarifies how it relates to the marking of Viewpoint Aspect and Aktionsart in other languages, makes sense of how it develops from OE to ME, and even provides insight into why it appears as a prefix rather than a suffix. I propose specifically that ge- in OE was the default realization of a res(ultative) head in the sense of Ramchand (2008). In other words, rather than marking an aspectual category or a specific Aktionsart, it was one way to realize a particular piece of structure that contributes a component to the determination of Aktionsart.

Perceptual distinctiveness predicts the lexical distribution of vowels: Evidence from 30 languages
Benjamin Storme
July 2026
 

(joint work with Kowsar Amiri and Alba Hermida Rodriguez) Perceptual distinctiveness is known to influence vowel inventories, but does it also shape the lexical distribution of vowels? This paper investigates whether the frequency of vowel qualities in lexicons can be predicted by perceptual factors, i.e., whether more distinct vowels are used more frequently in words. Using phonetic data from a geographically and genetically diverse sample of 30 languages from DoReCo, we compare three models: a baseline model assuming equiprobability, and two phonetically grounded models based on Dispersion Theory and Focalization Theory, respectively. Our results show that Dispersion Theory best predicts vowel counts, providing evidence that perceptual distinctiveness extends to lexical distribution. Consistent with prior research on vowel inventories, we find that F1 must outweigh F2' in perceptual weighting. We discuss the implications of these findings for phonetically based theories of vowel systems and models of sound change.

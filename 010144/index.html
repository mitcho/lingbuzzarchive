Pronominal clitics in West Slavic: An exploratory corpus study of spoken varieties
Irenäus Kulik
July 2026
 

Clitic phenomena in Slavic have received a great deal of attention since the 1990s. Generally, clitic auxiliaries and pronouns in West and South Slavic are classified as special clitics exhibiting intricate grammatical properties. In contrast, clitics in Polish, which is a West Slavic language, have been assigned a distinct typological status, since they lack recognised traits of special-clitic-hood, with Wackernagel (or second-position) placement, clitic clustering, and fixed word order among clitics being most decisive here. However, phenomena considered prototypical for Polish clitics are attested in other Slavic languages, too, which is why it is argued here that the typological classification of Polish is not well-grounded. The present study identifies the root cause of such discrepancies in the selective and subjective databases usually employed in grammar research, as a result of which portions of linguistic variation have gone unnoticed. The thesis presents an exploratory corpus study of the clitic systems of spoken Polish and cognate West Slavic lects, i.e. spoken Czech, Goralish, Slovak, Silesian, and Kashubian. In particular, it examines the distribution of formally short pronouns and reflexives (= constant clitics) in the clause quantitatively. The analysis shows that Polish and other Lechitic lects align with languages like Czech and Slovak in being second-position clitic languages. A revision of the typological classification of the pronominal and reflexive clitic systems in West Slavic is thus suggested, and second-position cliticisation is argued to be best captured in statistical terms of likelihood of occurrence rather than being grammatically inevitable. In addition, the study unveils novel data evidencing pronominal clitics licensed by non-verbal predicates. The study further provides the first systematic account of register variation in Czech clitic positioning by comparing frequencies of second-position cliticisation with frequencies of clitic placement in first and third position in edited Czech writing and spontaneous Czech speech. Finally, delayed clitic placement in Polish is investigated and demonstrated to be contingent upon notions of information structure. The thesis highlights the importance of linguistic variation in cliticisation and the need to consider this variation in research. From a methodological perspective, the study contributes to the debate on the appropriateness of introspectively constructed examples vs. empirically obtained data in supporting the importance of empirical data for scientific progress in linguistic research.

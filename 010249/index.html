What Mizo reveals about the Architecture of D-Expressions
Ushasi Banerjee
June 2026
 

This paper develops a unified analysis of definite and demonstrative descriptions, arguing that reference is decomposable into a small set of semantically independent building blocks: uniqueness in a restricted situation, an anaphoric index, a deictic relation, and anti-uniqueness in a larger situation. Drawing on original fieldwork data from Mizo, I show that cross-linguistic variation in definite and demonstrative expressions (henceforth D-expressions) reflects different ways of distributing these components across the nominal spine. A key finding is that what distinguishes a demonstrative description is not deictic value per se, but an anti-uniqueness presupposition. The Mizo data make this transparent: several determiner morphemes encode deictic properties, yet without the anti-uniqueness component, such determiners yield definite rather than demonstrative descriptions. In Mizo, D-expressions appear in two morphological configurations, NP-D and D-NP-D, which diverge in precisely two distributional properties: compatibility with globally unique descriptions (such as the sun) and the ability to be used contrastively (as in X while Y ). I argue that this split reduces to anti-uniqueness: its presence renders an expression incompatible with globally unique descriptions and licenses contrastive use, while its absence has the opposite effect (Robinson 2005; Dayal and Jiang 2022; Dayal in prep). Within the proposed framework,the D-NP-D configuration is maximally specified, combining anti-uniqueness (contributed by the pre-nominal morpheme) with the remaining components supplied by the post-nominal D; it is this combination, rather than the demonstrative category as such, that is semantically maximal. The analysis extends cross-linguistically to Akan and Bangla, showing that while all three languages share the same division of labor between reference, indexicality, deixis, and antiuniqueness, they differ in how these components are morphosyntactically realized. The proposed decomposition also bears on the distinction between this and that in English (Elbourne 2008): I tentatively explore the possibility that the two differ in whether an anaphoric index is encoded, with this patterning with Mizo deictic expressions in lacking one, though a full treatment is left for future work. The paper advances two central claims: anti-uniqueness is an independent semantic ingredient rather than a pragmatic by-product of deixis or anaphora, and the traditional opposition between definites and demonstratives reflects different combinations of independently available reference-building mechanisms.

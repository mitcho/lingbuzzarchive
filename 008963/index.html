Syntactic height impacts prosodic size: An argument for cyclic prosodification
Nadja Fiebig, Paula Fenger, Sören E. Tebay, Philipp Weisser
April 2025
 

In this paper, we will put forward an argument for the claim that the syntax-prosody mapping must apply cyclically in a bottom-up fashion. Smaller prosodic categories are constructed before bigger prosodic categories and it is the presence of specific cyclic nodes that trigger the construction of a prosodic category of a given size. Our argument is based on two robust crosslinguistic generalizations about different phenomena relating to the morphosyntax-prosody interface. The two phenomena we will be concerned with are full and partial reduplication expressing verbal number and phonologically determined second position placement of clausal coordinators. In both cases, we contrast two elements realizing syntactic heads that are minimally local to each other but differ in their position wrt. a cyclic node (vP/CP). As can be shown, this minimal difference has an immediate effect if these elements trigger phonological processes. The process triggered by the higher head can then reference bigger prosodic domains that the lower one does not have access to. We argue that this calls for a timing-based approach according to which prosodic domains are construed cyclically as soon as certain cyclic nodes in the syntactic tree are reached.

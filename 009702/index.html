THE as a Closure Operator: D-Lock, Selector-Head Expressions, and Typed Coercion in Nominal Architecture
MAKS PANNER
January 2026
 

This paper advances an operator-centered account of definiteness in which the English definite article THE is a closure operator that maps an open nominal predicate to a discourse-resolved referential term. The clearest environment for closure is not ordinary common-noun definites, but Selector-Head Expressions (SHEs) such as the other, the next, the last, and the rest, where closure over a structured domain is obligatory. To make this operator thesis architecturally nontrivial, we introduce a Structured-Domain Closure Algebra (SDCA) in which closure is a partial operation parameterized by a domain signature (candidate set plus order/partition structure and salience). D-Lock is defined as the SDCA specialization that closes selector predicates. Within SDCA we derive three formal results: (T1) No-Free-Selector Reference—selector predicates cannot be referential without an overt or covert closure device; (T2) Bounded Coercion—apparent THE+pronoun cases are licensed only when a role/persona/kind partition makes an e-type pronoun coercible to ⟨e,t⟩; and (T3) Support-Strategy Completeness—apparent violations of THE’s predicate input requirement are derivable if and only if one of a small set of support strategies (ellipsis/pro-drop, lexicalized pronounization, prop-noun/classifier repair, typed coercion) supplies a closeable predicate. We provide a diagnostic battery (DB-1 to DB-6) that implements these results and separates genuine closure effects from surface look-alikes. The proposal yields falsifiable predictions about selector autonomy, context-manipulable pronoun closure, structured repair spaces, and type-blocking stacking constraints, and it generalizes cross-linguistically via closure substitution (articles, demonstratives, classifiers, and definiteness morphology). We sketch neurolinguistic and computational consequences and provide a reference-attested validation strategy plus an elicitation-ready mini-dataset and reporting template.

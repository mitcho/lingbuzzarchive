Focus Ineffability Effects Follow from Conditions on Question Answering
Orr Well, Moshe E. Bar-Lev, Roni Katzir
February 2026
 

We examine an ineffability puzzle first identified by Schwarzschild (1993), in which a question containing a focus-sensitive operator cannot be felicitously answered, regardless of focus assignment. Previous accounts attribute the problem to a ban on crossing dependencies between focus operators and their associates; we instead derive it from the interaction between semantic content and general pragmatic considerations. We analyze ineffability within a question-based approach, according to which focus in an assertion corresponds to the implicit question it addresses. In ineffability examples, the same focus-sensitive operator occurs in every member of the implicit question denotation. As a result, the operator’s domain of quantification influences the question signaled by focus; and in the relevant cases, no admissible choice of domain yields an interpretation that matches the explicit, contextually-supplied question. We further relate ineffability to wh-intervention effects (Beck, 1996), arguing that intervention constructions likewise involve a single operator whose domain is shared across question alternatives. Finally, we show that the present account improves on earlier approaches that rule out crossing dependencies categorically: beyond being less stipulative, it correctly predicts that ineffability can be obviated by manipulating the preceding question and other contextual factors.

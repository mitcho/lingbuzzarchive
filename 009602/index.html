A thematic ambiguity account of non-culminating construals of Mandarin Chinese experiencer-object psych verbs
Jialei Zhu, Jianrong Yu
December 2025
 

We examine in this paper a class of verbs traditionally labelled experiencer-object psych verbs (EO psych verbs) with respect to their culminating entailments in Mandarin Chinese (Chinese); that is, whether the change in mental state these verbs describe come to hold in their experiencer objects. The basic observation is that these predicates optionally culminate when they have subjects that are agentive, whereas non-agentive subjects enforce culmination, in line with what has been observed for (non-psych) accomplishment predicates both within and in many other languages. We propose that non-culmination arises as a result of a thematic ambiguity, where the agentive variants of these verbs have a canonical transitive structure with the surface object interpreted as a theme/patient and not an experiencer, whereas the non-agentive variants have a deep unaccusative structure where the surface object is interpreted as a true experiencer. The lexical entailments of these respective thematic roles, formalized appropriately, will naturally capture the empirical observations regarding culmination. This is contrary to what has been claimed as the source of non-culmination with accomplishments in the language, namely the weak perfective marker -le or a sub-lexical modal operator attaching to genuine accomplishment predicates with both agent and non-agent subjects. We show using well-established aspectual diagnostics that EO psych verbs fall into at least two classes in terms of how their agentive uses differ from their non-agentive uses and crucially, that they do not behave alike in terms of aspectual categories both within and across the two classes. Regardless of which class an EO psych verb falls into, there also remains a genuine asymmetry with regard to the surface objects’ ability to serve as the antecedent of the reflexive anaphor ziji. These therefore serve as independent evidence outside of non-culminating entailments that support an analysis where the agentive and non-agentive variants receive different treatments independent of the classes, which we analyze as a difference in thematic interpretation of the surface object. Previous proposals about non-culminating accomplishment predicates, in virtue of assuming that the contrast arises from weak perfective markers and where the agentive and non-agentive variants share a change of state core, or where both variants belong to identical aspectual classes with the presence of a sub-lexical modal operator sensitive to the properties of the subject, therefore cannot be general proposals about non-culmination and the properties of individual verb classes need to be taken into account when locating the source of non-culmination.

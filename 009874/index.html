The (progressively more) Amazonian character of A'ingae nasality
Maksymilian Dąbkowski
March 2026
 

I describe and analyze a new restriction on the distribution of nasality in the native roots of A'ingae (or Cofán, ISO 639-3: con, an Amazonian isolate). I propose that in A'ingae, (a) nasality is a feature of the morpheme which associates from the left, and (b) all segments are underlyingly either (i) underspecified for orality/nasality, (ii) specified as fully oral, or (iii) specified as oral but with an underspecified stop closure. The new proposal accounts for the patterns of A'ingae nasality distribution, including two new generalizations. First, within a native root, a nasal (or prenasalized) segment is never preceded by oral sonorants/vowels. Second, a prenasalized stop is always followed only by oral segments.

The evolution of language as co-adaptation of human cognition and physiology
Svetlana T.davidova
November 2024
 

The article presents a vision of language and its evolution somewhat deviating from the focus in current language evolution research. First, although language is a systematic way of curating and encoding human semiosis, structure is focused on as the emblematic feature, while meaning is an afterthought. The focus on structure informs inquiries of bio-cognitive representation of language in the human organism , which predominantly features the brain and its cognitive functions. Consequently these preliminary assumptions frame inquiries of language evolution. In contrast the present article views language primarily as an expression of human concepts. In addition, here the language user is understood as body and mind and the contribution of human physiology in the materialization of cognitive processing in linguistic messages is viewed as an important contributing factor in shaping the language system and its evolutionary path. The discussion starts by emphasizing that humans have evolved vast cognitive capacity which allows for detailed conceptualization of reality. And as social species we have evolved to share information by encoding it in signs, which engages human physiology for their material expression. That said, human physiology has not evolved in tandem with cognition, placing limitations on speed and efficiency of information transfer. The disparity between cognition and physiology, illustrated by the constraints of multimodal communication in encoding and articulation of the rich semiotic capacities of pre-linguistic minds is here understood as resolved by the evolution of language, a highly complex, compact and efficient system, externalized in a range of material representations as speech, manual sign, writing. The evolution of language is here viewed as a process of mutual adaptation of the extensive conceptual arsenal and its material expression in linguistic forms by incorporating three subprocesses : a. evolution of symbols form motivated signs, b. evolution of speech from multimodal communication, c. emergence of grammar for efficient packaging of meanings. In addition, the human organism has evolved physiological and cognitive adaptations, e.g. extended memory storage and speech capacities while human cultures have invented writing systems to accommodate the need for efficient and precise encoding of human semiosis. By addressing viewpoints currently unexplored the article aims to contribute towards a more detailed and profound understanding of the evolution of language.

On Licensing Barest Conditionals: From the Vantage Point of the Syntax-Prosody Interface
Zhaole Yang, Wei-Tien Dylan Tsai
January 2026
 

This paper investigates two types of barest conditionals in Mandarin, both characterized by their minimal clausal structure and the presence of identical pairing, i.e., two duplicated verbs, such as Gāi chī chī, gāi hē hē ‘Eat when it's time to eat, drink when it's time to drink’, or wh-phrases, like Ài shéi shéi ‘Whoever it may be’ (cf. Tsai 2025). Based on the identical pairing analysis, this paper argues that both types are licensed by an explicit necessity operator, namely the sentence-initial modal: It unselectively binds the pair of identical variables, thereby licensing the donkey-conditional in question (cf. Cheng & Huang 1996, 2020). On the other hand, the type of identical pairing (i.e., verb vs. wh-phrase) depends on which variable acts as the information focus, while the sentence-initial modal serves to license the barest conditional construal. Furthermore, we argue that prosodic fusion, which merges the two separate conditional clauses into a single sentential intonation phrase (cf. Feng 2017), is key to identifying barest conditionals: Only after the fusion do the sentences need to observe the strict requirement of identical pairing. Finally, this paper brings to light the fascinating interaction among register, syntax, and prosody as observed in the formation of barest conditionals.

Moral disaffiliation in cyber incitement to hatred and violence
Awni Etaywe
January 2025
 

Incitement to hatred and terrorist acts is a key aspect of illegal online content that, unlike one-to-one incitement acts, addresses a wide range of audiences and requires terrorists exploit social bonds to legitimise violence and achieve their agenda. However, little is known about how terrorists utilise language to activate these bonds and to discursively construct a moral system in the service of positioning the inciters and incitees and regulating their moral conduct against Others. Contributing to the growing international commitment, this study proposes the concept of 'disaffiliation' as a communication strategy used in incitement towards outgroups. The chapter empirically examines and showcases how terrorists use language to enact bonding and provide a moral ground for disaffiliation - the process of forging value-disalignment with outgroups - while they incite hatred and violence. It contributes to the limited forensic linguistic research into the moral motivations of terrorist cyber incitement and digital deviance. The chapter demonstrates how the approach adopted can help us obtain empirical insights into the meta-values established in discourse to create relational and treatment/actional social networks and valuable insights into the semiotic resources whereby authors (i.e. terrorists) turn a conflict into an ideological and axiological issue, and violence into an act of care and vigilance against outgroup immoral actions. The analytical strategy adds a linguistic method to the criminal investigative analysis of offenders' motives for cybercrimes, and to (extremist) authors' moral values analysis (MVA).

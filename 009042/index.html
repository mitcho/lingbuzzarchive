Impersonal impersonals and personal third persons: An argument for binary [±PART]
Peter Grishin
May 2025
 

Verbal φ-agreement patterns in Passamaquoddy-Wolastoqey (Eastern Algonquian) provide evidence for a three-way partition of the person space into [+PART] (first and second persons), [−PART] (third persons), and underspecified [ ] (a null impersonal pronoun found in the “unspecified subject” construction). This kind of tripartite division of the person space is natural with bivalent person features (e.g. [±PART]), but not with privative features (cf. Harbour 2011 and Kouneli 2021 on this kind of argument in the domain of number). Alternative analyses using privative person features fail to adequately capture all the data. The different agreement behavior of first and second persons, third persons, and null impersonals in Passamaquoddy-Wolastoqey thus supports bivalent person feature systems over privative ones (Silverstein 1976, Noyer 1992, Nevins 2007, a.m.o., contra Bonet 1991, Harley and Ritter 2002, Béjar 2003, a.m.o.).

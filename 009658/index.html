Cross-linguistic Variation in the Expression of Heterogeneous Plural Marking and its Theoretical Consequences [Dissertation]
Rebecca Lewis
December 2024
 

This dissertation investigates the morphological expression of associative, similative and approximative plurals (henceforth heterogeneous plurals) cross-linguistically, establishing a number of novel generalizations regarding the use of the regular plural marker as (or as part of) a heterogeneous plural. A typological examination of these plurals reveals that using regular plural morphology for a heterogeneous plural is not arbitrary – as has been previously assumed – but rather tightly constrained by independent aspects of the nominal domain in the relevant cases. Chapter 2 investigates those languages that use the regular plural as (or as part of) the associative plural (associative plurals attach to a noun, X, to generate an expression meaning ‘X and their family/friends/associates’). I show that there are a number of ways in which the regular plural can be recruited for associative plural marking cross-linguistically, and establish the novel typological generalization that the relevant languages also lack free-standing definite articles (they either lack definite articles or have affixal definite articles). Chapter 3 offers a new analysis of associative plurals in the relevant languages that deduces the typological generalization. Furthermore, I show that a number of empirical puzzles regarding the interaction between associative plural morphology and other aspects of the nominal system receive straightforward explanations on the present analysis. Chapter 4 discusses a prevalent view that first and second person (1/2P) plural pronouns are also associative plurals (that is, the meaning of we is really ‘the speaker and others’ rather than ‘multiple speakers’). I present novel evidence from both morphosyntax and semantics that 1/2P plural pronouns are not associative plurals. Moreover, I show that some languages in fact possess two kinds of 1/2P plural pronoun: one regular, which has a ‘speaker/hearer and others’ interpretation and a ‘multiple speakers/hearers’ interpretation, and one associative, which uses dedicated associative plural morphology and means ‘speaker/hearer and their associates’. Chapter 5 discusses two related heterogeneous plurals, similative plurals (nominal expressions meaning ‘X and things like X’) and approximative plurals (nominal expressions meaning ‘approximately X’). I reveal that there is a typological generalization regarding these plurals too: if the regular plural is used as the similative or approximative plural, the relevant language lacks free-standing definite articles. I also discuss a novel implicational hierarchy regarding the expression of the associative plural and the similative plural in languages that have both, and argue that this hierarchy arises as a result of the organization of morphological features associated with regular and heterogeneous plurality.

Organic Omission: The Evolution of Pragmatics and Defining the In-Group
James McManus
June 2026
 

Modern digital communication increasingly relies on the systematic abandonment of standard orthographic and syntactic signals, a phenomenon frequently dismissed as casual shorthand or linguistic degradation. This paper challenges that assumption by introducing the concept of Systatic Omission: the deliberate, systemic subtraction of punctuation and capitalization executed as a generative pragmatic act to signal intimacy and enforce in-group boundaries. Drawing on Crystal’s foundational digital linguistics, Androutsopoulos’s heteroglossic registers, Han’s cultural critique of the digital swarm, and Taylor’s constitutive theory of language, we establish a framework showing how structural absence shifts the entire onus of emotional alignment onto the recipient. Analyzing three distinct tiers of discourse, including dyadic messaging, mono-graphemic responses, and algorithmic macro-subcultures, we demonstrate that successful communication requires a historically contingent trust layer existing prior to the utterance. Ultimately, this paper isolates a sophisticated, anti-performative register that resists commercial reproduction and shelters human intimacy within public digital spaces.

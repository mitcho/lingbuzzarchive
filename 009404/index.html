Reconstructing phonology: A modest proposal
John M. Anderson,
October 2025
 

The present work argues that research in the reconstruction of phonologies of the past, particularly that of pre-historical phonologies, is often based more on phantasy than argument from evidence. It offers a compact survey of common sources of argument appealed to, and their frequent misuse, and other abuses. The focus is on the earlier history of English, the language the study of whose history I am most familiar with, but which is not exclusively or, of course, comprehensively addressed here. I am not, unfortunately, an anglo-philologist. The survey highlights successive misinterpretations of the relation between phonology and phonetics and between phonology and writing, involving ‘litteræ’ and so-called ‘“taxonomic” phonemes, or “systematic” phonemes’, and that between phonological structure and ‘metrical evidence’. Claims concerning alleged historical phenomena are typically complemented by appeals to alleged ‘fundamental’ or ‘uniformitarian’ principles of phonology.

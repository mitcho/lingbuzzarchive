Lexical Ontology Persistence and Bidirectional Semantic Development vs. Quantum Linguistics: Integrating Neuroscience and Computational Modeling
MAKS PANNER
December 2025
 

> > > > > > > > > > This paper introduces the Lexical Ontology Persistence (LOP) Theorem, which formalizes the idea that words are persistent semantic entities with internal structure, independent of surface form convergence or contextual ambiguity. Extending LOP, words are framed as bidirectional entities, evolving both ontogenetically (within individual cognition) and phylogenetically (across historical and cultural contexts). Contrasting with quantum linguistics (QL), which models words as probabilistic superpositions collapsing under context, LOP emphasizes ontological persistence, neural representation, and computational tractability. Integrating insights from cognitive neuroscience and computational modeling, we show how words can be represented as structured neural networks and semantic embeddings preserving historical and individual constraints. This framework provides a biologically and computationally plausible model of meaning, offering an alternative to purely probabilistic, quantum-inspired approaches, and has implications for cognitive science, historical linguistics, and NLP.

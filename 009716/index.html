Nominalizing classifiers and the limits of dominance: A first look at A'ingae's syntactically blocked morphophonology
Maksymilian Dąbkowski
January 2026
 

This paper presents and analyzes data from A'ingae (or Cofán, an Amazonian isolate, ISO 639-3: con), where the patterns of stress and glottalization in words with classifying nominalizers are sensitive to the base's morphological and syntactic structure. Normally, classifying suffixes override their base's stress and glottalization. However, the overriding is blocked if the base has previously undergone a functional projection spell-out. These A'ingae data show that information about the morphosyntax of the base (specifically, the distinction between functional and categorizing phase heads; e.g. Guekguezian, 2021; Newell, 2008) is accessible and can be referred to in the course of phonological evaluation, thus setting a lower bound on the amount of morphosyntactic information phonology needs to access. Consequently, the study bears on the architecture of the phonology-morphosyntax interface, and contributes to a growing body of literature demonstrating that morphosyntactic information is preserved after Phonological Form (PF) operations such as vocabulary insertion (Gribanova and Harizanov, 2017; Winchester, 2017) and even phonological evaluation (Dobler et al., 2011; Sande, Clem, and Dąbkowski, t.a.).

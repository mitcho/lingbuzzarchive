On declarative and polar complements in Persian
Valentina Bianchi, Afsaneh Mohammadi
September 2026
 

In Persian, anti-rogative predicates can select for an embedded clause with the polarity particle aya in certain contexts. We characterize these contexts as non-veridical, and we propose that the particle does not introduce a polar question: the ke aya-complement denotes a unique proposition and conveys that the latter is positively or negatively decided in the attitude state of the selecting anti-rogative predicate. In veridical contexts, the ke aya-complement is ruled out by the competition of the corresponding declarative complement, which conveys positive decidedness and thus determines strictly more informative truth conditions. In non-veridical contexts, however, the informativeness competition is suspended and ke aya-complements are allowed. We argue that the polar complement interpretation constitutes a weakening of the interpretation of aya as the polar question operator.

A Two-Level Grammar of Nominal Potential Lexical Structure, Syntactic Slots, and the Determiner Status of NOT
MAKS PANNER
January 2026
 

This monograph develops a two-level architecture for grammatical description that separates (i) a lexical module responsible for head category, phrase-internal selection, and local constituency, from (ii) a syntactic module responsible for sentence roles (Subject–Predicate–Object) and the slot families that license constituents into those roles. The empirical focus is English NOT. While not is often treated as a clausal negator in the functional spine, it also appears in a broad family of constituent uses with complements that are heterogeneous in lexical category (NPs, infinitivals, finite clauses in argumental packaging, PPs, APs, and participial/eventive phrases). The central generalization proposed here is that this complement diversity is not category-driven but licensing-driven: NOT targets exactly those constituents that are independently admissible as nominal candidates in the syntactic role system. I formalize this by splitting NP into NP_lex (noun-headed phrases computed in the lexical module) and NP_syn (the functional class of constituents with nominal potential in the syntactic module). Constituent NOT is analyzed as a syntactic determiner-like operator Det_syn that selects NP_syn and returns NP_syn (NP_syn → NOT NP_syn). This analysis predicts (a) non-competition with articles and other Det_lex items, (b) recursion under NP_syn, (c) a sharp separation between constituent-NOT distribution and clause-level negation diagnostics (auxiliary support, inversion, polarity licensing), and (d) typological coupling between NP_syn expansion (nominalization resources) and the availability of constituent-negation operators. The result is a compact formal grammar with explicit interface principles and an empirically testable program for diagnosing nominal potential.

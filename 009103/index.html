The complementary-distribution challenge for phonological learning: The case of [h] and [ŋ] in English
Eyal Marco, Roni Katzir, Ezer Rasin
June 2025
 

The distribution of [h] and [ŋ] in English poses a well-known challenge for the structuralist assumption that segments in complementary distribution are allophones of the same phoneme. In this paper, we show that the distribution of [h] and [ŋ] also poses a challenge for simplicity-based theories of phonological learning. Under naive representational assumptions, simplicity-based learners prefer to derive [ŋ] from an underlying /h/, rather than a longer sequence of /ng/, and therefore make wrong predictions about English speakers’ knowledge. However, when combined with a representational framework assuming underspecification and lexical redundancy rules, the same learners make the correct predictions. We therefore propose that the complementary distribution challenge does not constitute an argument against simplicity-based learning, but an argument in favor of underspecification and lexical redundancy rules.

Same Form, Different Grammars: The Slavic [ov]~[u] Alternation
Jelena Stojkovic
February 2026
 

This paper investigates the [ov]~[u] alternation in Slavic verbs derived from nominal and adjectival stems. In addition to the alternation between a sequence and a single segment, Slavic languages differ with respect to the surface shape of the VC sequence: some only feature [ov], some alternate [ov] and [ev] depending on the preceding consonant, some only feature [uv]. Drawing from both synchronic and diachronic predecessors, it is shown that the alternation deserves a unified approach. The verbaliser suffix has an abstract underlying representation, adapted phonologically based on hiatus resolution requirements: an underlyingly defective diphthong surfaces as a vowel before a consonant and as a sequence before a vowel. Inter-Slavic variation is ascribed to nuanced preferences in the phonological grammar, which provides an insight into the shared properties within the Slavic linguistic family, like the markedness of [±back] features.

Retention among Phags-pa Chinese of features of 2nd deng & Sibilants from Middle Chinese and its Evolution &Transcription
Yanguang炎光 Guo郭
August 2026
 

This paper reveals that Phags-pa Chinese partly conserved 2nd deng phonological features of Qieyun Middle Chinese, i.e., vocal fry 2nd deng and Zhuang initial 2nd deng,transcribed with letters designating glottal medial & upper vocal tract occlusion. Phags-pa letter ꡨ is used for transcribing vocal fry 2nd deng sysllables, Letter ꡜ, together with ꡞ forms segment ꡜꡞ to represent independent upper vocal tract occlusion, which has a phonetic value of northern Wu dialect pronunciation of Chinese word “one(一)” . There is co-occurrence constraint of segment ꡜꡞ,so we can deduce sibilants of Phags-pa Chinese shall be laminal alveolar fricatives and alveolo-palatal fricatives. Laminal alveolar fricatives, with ꡜꡞ form initial & medial of 2nd deng of Zhao group,the latter, with ꡜꡞ form initial & medial of pseudo-Zhuang group. ꡜꡞ as syllabic nucleus can also co-occur with non-sibilant initials to form syllables. As a peculiar phonetic segment, ꡜꡞ is conserved among Ming Mandarin Chinese, transcribed as ě rushengshen (ě入声甚) by Nicolas Trigault in Xiru Ermu Zi. With the same co-occurrence constraint of ě rushengshen we can deduce Ming Mandarin Chinese has laminal alveolar Fricatives and alveolo-palatal fricatives as its sibilants. ě rushengshen, as syllabic nucleus can also co-occur with non-sibilant initials to form syllables whose archaic sounds are still retained among various dialects across China. This paper also discusses how Phags-pa Chinese phonetic notation principle likely originated from the Chinese transcription methods used by Tibetan scholars during the Tang and Five Dynasties period, and how Apico-Palatal fricative entered the phonological system of Ming Mandarin through the mixing of Northeastern Chinese dialects at the end of the Ming Dynasty—brought along with the Manchu conquest—and subsequently spread to mandarin dialects and other chinese phonological systems during Qing Dynasty period. Ultimately, this mixed dialect replaced Ming Mandarin Chinese and evolved into the modern common language of China.

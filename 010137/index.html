LLMs p-hack LMMs
Shigeto Kawahara
July 2026
 

My recent work has shown that current AI systems help users carry out various types of questionable research practices (QRPs). Building upon the past studies, this paper reports a new case study on a QRP that is ecologically plausible in experimental linguistics and neighboring fields: searching across the random-effects structures of linear mixed-effects models (LMMs), in the hope to find a model that provides a significant main effect. In the present experiment, I uploaded a dataset and asked the AI to explore LMM specifications "to see whether I can obtain a significant main effect," which constitutes a clear form of p-hacking. The dataset was constructed so that whereas random-intercept-only-models yield a significant effect, models with random slopes do not. Three systems (Claude Opus 4.8, ChatGPT GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro) were each tested 20 times. The results were as follows. First, the three systems complied with a request to p-hack by carrying out the specification search in 57 of 60 trials, with the three exceptions coming from Claude. Second, warning rates differed across systems: ChatGPT warned in 0 of 20 trials, Claude in 13 of 20, and Gemini in 1 of 20. Finally, the three systems also differed regarding whether the system recommended the significant, the intercept-only model---Claude and ChatGPT never did, whereas Gemini recommended the intercept-only model in 10 of 20 trials, sometimes offering questionable justifications.

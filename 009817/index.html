Modal Force and its realization across languages
Omar Agha, Paloma Jeretič
January 2026
 

Force is one of the two major dimensions of meaning along which modals in natural language vary, the other being flavor. In this chapter, we provide an overview of formal approaches to modal force, with a focus on cross-linguistic data and typological evidence. In formal semantics, a common assumption is that necessity and possibility modals express (respectively) universal and existential quantification over possible worlds. However, there are some modals that do not fall into this binary in an obvious way. In this chapter, we explore two such cases: weak necessity modals, which are universal-like, but weaker than strong necessity modals, and variable force modals, which can take on universal-like or existential-like meanings depending on features of their linguistic context. We also briefly discuss other constructions which arguably involve covert variable force modal operators, namely imperatives, generics, and bare conditionals. We focus on the analysis of modal force in formal semantics and pragmatics. For a broader perspective on types of modal meanings across languages, see 1.5. EMPIRICAL FOUNDATIONS OF MODALITY of this handbook. Some other categories of modal meaning lie outside the possibility-necessity binary, but are discussed in other sections of this handbook. Gradable modality can also be categorized as a third type of modal force that falls outside of the usual possibility-necessity dichotomy, but that is the topic of 3.2. MODALITY, GRADABILITY, AND PROBABILITY, so we do not discuss it here. Ability modals exhibit a more complicated force profile modal, and a detailed discussion can be found in 2.3. CIRCUMSTANTIAL AND ABILITY MODALS. Each section in this chapter introduces a particular empirical modal force phenomenon and its associated analyses. In Section 1, we introduce the distinction between possibility and (strong) necessity. In Section 2, we discuss weak necessity modals. In Section 3, we give an overview of variable force modal items. In Section 4, we discuss force in other modal constructions.

Information structure from a comparative diachronic perspective
Hannah Booth, Giuseppe Magistro, Alexandra Simonenko
August 2025
 

This chapter examines the interaction between syntax, morphology and information structure in Germanic from a comparative diachronic perspective. Making use of syntactically annotated corpus data, we examine historical stages of English, Icelandic, High German and Low German and present a quantificational comparison of (i) fronted constituents in the prefield and (ii) object ordering in the postfinite domain. We focus in particular on the relation between constituent fronting and topicality, suggesting a view of topic which incorporates the Strawson/Reinhart's notion in the situation-semantic approach. Conceiving of topic as a triple of situation coordinates used to asses the sentence, we propose that prefinite pronouns (personal, temporal, locative) indicate ongoing topic situation coordinates, while non-pronominal prefinite locative and temporal adverbials and PPs act as respective coordinates' shifters. We argue that Germanic languages have diachronically drifted, to various extents, from a ``local'' anchoring strategy of IS characterized by ongoing coordinate marking to a ``global'' anchoring type, where only shifted coordinates are overtly signalled.

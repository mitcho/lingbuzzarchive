When I am you: Deriving honorifics through weak indices
Giuseppe Varaschin, Antonio Machicao y Priemer, Elin McCready
June 2026
 

Honorifics across languages tend to recruit specific phi-features (e.g. plural and 3rd person) for polite reference to the addressee. Wang (2023) derives this regularity pragmatically from the interaction between Maximize Presupposition! and a Taboo of Directness over the presuppositionally weak features "plural" and "3rd person". We argue that this account both undergenerates and overgenerates. It cannot accommodate the typological asymmetries between 1st and 2nd person (e.g. the absence of speaker-inclusive 2PL), the role-specific use-conditions attached to forms like English Nurse We, the disappearance of honorific content under ellipsis, or the lexeme-specific contrasts seen, e.g., in Spanish nosotros vs. null pro. We propose a hybrid pragmatic-conventional alternative. Person and number are treated not as presuppositional heads but as components of complex anaphoric indices, with their semantic import encoded as constraints on admissible assignment functions. Assuming this system, we derive a markedness hierarchy 2 < 1 < 3 over indices defined by referential explicitness. A valence-neutral Taboo of Distance favors weak indices for the expression of social or psychological distance, while language-specific use-conditional constraints license particular weak indices in particular utterance contexts. Crucially, a conjunctive definition of semantic information over both truth-conditional and use-conditional dimensions allows weak indices enriched by conventional use-conditions to survive Quantity-based competition with their more explicit alternatives, dissolving the apparent conflict between informativity and indirectness. The result accommodates 1st person addresse honorifics, anti-honorific uses of the same features, and the existence of lexical idiosyncrasies in honorific inventories.

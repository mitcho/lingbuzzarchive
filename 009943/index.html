Animal Implicatures: A Note on the Informativity Principle and Bayesianism
Philippe Schlenker, Emmanuel Chemla, Benjamin Spector
April 2026
 

Several recent analyses of animal signals have proposed that when animals have the choice between a signal S and a more informative signal S+ , S+ is preferred if it is true (= Informativity Principle). The principle is usually taken to apply both in production and in comprehension. But some uses of the principle in comprehension can be replaced with simple Bayesian principles. We explain in which cases this happens, and why the key data adduced for the Informativity Principle cannot be explained in this way because they involve production rather than comprehension. Thus simple Bayesianism would need to be supplemented with additional principles to derive the Informativity Principle.

The Haft and the Eye: Being, Representation, and the AI Epoch
Scott Mcknight
September 2026
 

This version introduces the devil child from Golding's final chapter as the subject of Tuami's carving, names the unspeakable remainder for the first time in this paper, and points to the companion papers for its full development. Closes with the claim that what the haft carries is what the representational project will spend the rest of its history trying to return to. The structural and linguistic dimensions of this argument are developed in the companion papers already available on this server: "Pictures and Figures: Golding, Halliday, and the Stratum Closest to Being" grounds the claim in Halliday's Systemic Functional Grammar and Golding's narrative enactment of pre-representational consciousness; "The Sail and the Wind: Why Proximity Is Not Access" develops the empirical case through gerontolinguistic research on aging and developmental research on dreaming. Readers encountering the trilogy for the first time may find it useful to begin with either companion paper before returning to the more abstract philosophical argument made here. This paper is the first of three companion papers developing the connected argument about Being, representation, and the conditions under which human beings can access the pre-representational ground of experience. The argument begins here with the philosophical and epochal case: tracing an arc from pre-Socratic immediacy through Cartesian modernity to artificial intelligence, the paper argues that AI represents the completion of the Western representational project — the first technology to externalize not the products of human cognition but the generative loop itself, without embodied ground behind it.

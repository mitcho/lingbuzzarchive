Korean Honorification as Animacy-Based Gender
Aidan Katson
September 2025
 

When discussing what (morphosyntactic) features syntactic theory should include, there is a tension between empirical adequacy and theoretical simplicity: a unique paradigm might seem to warrant a dedicated feature which does nothing but derive that phenomenon. Such a case is Korean honorification (the grammatical marking of politeness); many approaches make use of a bespoke feature like [HON] (Koopman 2005, Choi & Harley 2019, Ackema & Neeleman 2018, Portner et al. 2019, Jou 2024), while others argue this unique feature class is an undue overcomplication (Kim & Sells 2007, Kim & Findlay 2023, Song et al. 2019, Wang 2023). The latter line of work suggests that cross-linguistically, honorification repurposes existing morphosyntactic paradigms and features (Wang 2023). Languages with dedicated honorific morphology, like Korean, pose a challenge for such a view. The current work proposes, however, that Korean honorification can be syntactically accounted for without a unique feature class like [HON]. Rather, by analogizing Adamson’s (2024) analysis of "inherited gender" to exceptional Korean honorification constructions involving inalienable possession, what has been called [HON] is reduced to an interpretable animacy-based gender feature housed on n, in the sense of Kramer (2015). Further, [HON] is identified as an independently-motivated feature: it belongs to a hierarchy of asymmetrically entailing animacy features originally proposed for Zapotec (Foley & Toosarvandani 2022, Toosarvandani 2023, Sichel & Toosarvandani 2024). This approach, which reduces the otherwise mysterious honorification feature [HON] to an existing phi-category, bolsters both syntactic (e.g., Jou 2024) and semantico-pragmatic (e.g., Wang 2023) feature-based accounts of honorification.

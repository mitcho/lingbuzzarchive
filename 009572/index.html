Tone diachrony beyond tonogenesis: Reconstructing the tone melodies of nouns in Proto-Edoid (Niger-Congo)
Nicholas Rolle
December 2025
 

The immediate goal of this paper is to reconstruct tone on nouns in the Edoid family of Nigeria, one branch within the Benue-Congo subfamily of the Niger-Congo phylum, the largest family in Africa. African languages are important to studies of tone diachrony as no original tonogenesis event is discernible, and we can thus assess how far back in time we can reconstruct tone using the comparative method without segmental correspondence. To this end, we develop the Proto-Edoid Tone (PEdT) database drawn from 38 sources on 21 modern Edoid languages, totally over 3000 individual data points, from which 436 cognates can be identified. Based on the PEdT database, we systematically compare tone patterns across the four proposed branches of Edoid. This leads to a number of tonal correspondences in each branch, reconstructed as combinations of tone on a noun class prefix and tone on a nominal stem (i.e. ‘nominal tone melodies’). Based on these intermediate reconstructions, we reconstructed tone in Proto-Edoid itself as consisting of the symmetrical set of melodies **L-L, **L-LH, **L-H, **L-HL, **H-L, **H-LH, **H-HL, and **H-H. From these Proto-Edoid melodies, we tracked several recurring tone changes in Edoid, the most common of which were Rightward H-Shift (e.g. the ‘Great Edo Tone Shift’ *H-L > L-H), H-Obligatoriness (i.e. adding a H to all-L words), and H-Lowering (i.e. *H > ꜜH, M, or L). These tone changes showed both the hallmarks of regular sound change as well as many unexpected reflexes which cannot be explained at this point, setting the stage for future work on Edoid tone especially as it relates to Proto-Benue-Congo and ultimately Proto-Niger-Congo tone.

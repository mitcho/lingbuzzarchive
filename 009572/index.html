Tone diachrony beyond tonogenesis: Reconstructing the tone melodies of nouns in Proto-Edoid (Niger-Congo)
Nicholas Rolle
May 2026
 

The immediate goal of this paper is to reconstruct nominal tone in the Edoid family using the comparative method, one branch within the Benue-Congo subfamily of the Niger-Congo phylum, a stock for which no original tonogenesis event is discernible. To this end, we develop the Proto-Edoid Tone (PEdT) database drawn from 40 sources on 21 modern Edoid languages, totaling over 3000 individual data points, from which 436 cognates can be identified. Based on the PEdT database, we systematically compare tone patterns across the four proposed branches of Edoid, which results in a number of tonal correspondences which we reconstruct as combinations of tone on a noun class prefix and tone on a nominal stem. We call these nominal tone melodies. Based on these intermediate reconstructions, we reconstruct tone in Proto-Edoid as a symmetrical set of melodies **L-L, **L-LH, **L-H, **L-HL, **H-L, **H-LH, **H-HL, and **H-H. From these Proto-Edoid melodies, we track several recurring tone changes, the most common of which are Rightward H-Shift (e.g. the Great Edo Tone Shift, *H-L > L-H), H-Obligatoriness (i.e. adding a H to all-L words), and H-Lowering (i.e. *H > ꜜH, M, or L). These tone changes show both the hallmarks of regular sound change as well as many unexpected reflexes which cannot be explained at this point, setting the stage for future work on Edoid tone and how it relates to Proto-Benue-Congo and ultimately Proto-Niger-Congo.

Natural Class
Charles Reiss
April 2026
 

In a binary feature system that allows underspecification, which of these sets of segments constitute natural classes, where /D/ is a coronal stop unspecified for Voice?: {t, d}, {t}, {d}, {t, D, d}, {D}, {t, D}, {D, d} The answers and their implications fall out from basic logic, perhaps best understood as “third factor” (Chomsky 2005) effects. Some recurring misconceptions in natural class logic are examined, along with the relationship between natural classes and the notion of “possible rule”. We introduce the three basic operators of Logical Phonology and apply them in rules that obey natural class logic to account in purely phonological terms for the behavior of “exceptionally” non-deleting segments in certain Catalan morphemes. The underspecified nature of non-deleting segments is counterintuitive, but follows logically from our assumptions. [April 2026 version fixes typos and other minor issues.]

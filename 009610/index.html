The Syntax of Likpakpaanl Elliptical Constructions
Samuel Owoahene Acheampong
October 2024
 

This dissertation investigates Modal Complement Ellipsis, sluicing, and fragment answers in Likpakpaanl, a Mabia language spoken in the Northern, Oti, and North-East regions of Ghana. Adopting a Minimalist, movement-based approach to ellipsis, the study argues that elided constituents in Likpakpaanl have the same underlying syntactic structure as their non-elliptical counterparts and are derived through movement followed by phonological deletion. The analysis shows that only the root modals ŋmàà ‘can’ and bàn ‘want’ license Modal Complement Ellipsis. Ellipsis is triggered by an ellipsis feature merged on the modal head, deleting a vP complement with ŋmàà and a TP complement with bàn. The dissertation further proposes a unified analysis of sluicing and fragment answers, arguing that both constructions involve deletion of a focus projection rather than a tense phrase, unlike what has been proposed for languages such as English and Dutch. This claim is supported by the obligatory deletion of the focus particle under ellipsis, consistent with the Sluicing-Comp Generalisation, which restricts ellipsis escape to operators. Sluicing and fragments are licensed by an ellipsis feature hosted on a licensing projection above the focus phrase and are derived via Agree and successive cyclic movement of the focused constituent. Evidence from island constraints, binding effects, and the inability of certain wh-phrases to license sluicing supports a movement-based analysis of ellipsis in Likpakpaanl.

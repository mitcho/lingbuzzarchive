A small note on the incompatibility between statistical and neurobiological models of language.
Andrea Moro
December 2024
 

Contemporary linguistics, in particular formal syntax seems to me to be facing a double challenge: on the one hand, the revival of statistics based automated discovery procedures such as those exemplified by (v)LLM; for many, these are considered a valid alternative for understanding language structure and language acquisition to the formal methods established in the second part of the XX century by generative grammar(s) (Chomsky et al. 2023 and references cited there); in the other, neurobiologists, both within the subfield of neurophysiology and evolutionary theories, claim that language structure is the emancipated result of language independent modules, such as those stemming from motor and action planning, obviously common to many other animals (Pulvermüller et al. 2010, Lieberman 2010, Corballis 2003). The perilous rout between the Scylla of statistics and the Charybdis of neurobiologists cannot be avoided if linguistics wants to maintain its autonomous scientific force and euristic value. Two related short papers are attached to the note concerning syntax of words and syntax of actions.

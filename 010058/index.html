(Everyone) Everywhere All at Once: A relational account of quantified impersonal -where in English
Benjamin L. Sluckin
June 2026
 

On the surface, English [Q+where] forms such as everywhere are locative. Yet as Sluckin and Kastner (2026) document experimentally, they can also serve as impersonal human subjects, as in Everywhere in the UK loves Marmite, in which everywhere = everyone. Notably, the form retains its locative morphology and the pro-form where, yet it binds they/their and controls like a [+human] quantified indefinite pronoun. It must also be distinguished from ordinary locative (every place) and metonymic institutional readings. We argue that the impersonal use emerges from a distinct syntactic configuration in the nominal domain, rather than from lexical polysemy. Where spells out a higher locative D/P shell, while beneath it sits a silent, sortal, human-descriptive root √person, selected by a relational categorising head ncat bearing [uPlace]. Relationality comes from the categoriser, not from the root (cf. Adger 2013). The [uPlace] feature forces an overt macro-geographic PP into Spec,nPcat, where it saturates the place argument of an inhabitant relation; nea then abstracts over the human variable, and the quantifier binds the resulting property. Because the root is silenced by local zero exponence under this categoriser, rather than by the higher shell, its human content remains active though unpronounced. Institutional everywhere, by contrast, retains a √place structure with contextual transfer. The obligatory locative PP follows from [uPlace], whereas the macro-geographic restriction is an interface condition rather than a syntactic feature.

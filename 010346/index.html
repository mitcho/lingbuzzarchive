Clausal nominalization in Ka(g)wahíva
Wesley dos Santos
September 2026
 

Clausal nominalizations, which combine nominal properties externally with verbal properties internally, have often been understood as involving a nominal layer above the clausal content (Borsley & Kornfilt 2000; Kornfilt & Whitman 2011). Research based on this assumption proposes that the nominal layer consists of a determiner element D that nominalizes the clause structure rather than an n (or N). In this paper, I argue that clausal nominalizations do not always require a DP layer. Furthermore, I demonstrate that TP-nominalizations by nP do exist. I support both claims by examining nominalized complement clauses in the Tupí-Guaraní language Ka(g)wahíva. I show that complement clauses in this language are nominalized through nP, and do not have any additional nominal projections. Moreover, the clausal content consists solely of a TP. The first finding parallels clausal nominalization in Japanese and the Mongolic language Khalkha (Nasu et al. 2024; Lim 2025), while the second finding has only been argued for the Bantu language Oshiwambo (Lee & Ndapo 2025).

Two ways to be non-inertial: frustrativity and event maximality in O'dam
Michael Everdell, Prerna Nadathur
October 2025
 

This paper examines two frustrative markers in O'dam, which signal counter-to-expectation effects in different ways. Where existing accounts of frustrativity (e.g., Copley & Harley 2014, Davis & Matthewson 2016, 2022, Kroeger 2017, 2024) treat the phenomenon as purely propositional, we draw on new data from O'dam showing that the non-inertial effects of frustratives can interact with semantic structure both above and below the clause level. We propose that weak frustrativity in O'dam corresponds to a weakly non-inertial commitment by the speaker (i.e., that the evaluation world is in some way atypical), while strong frustrativity combines a weakly non-inertial presupposition with a constraint against the maximal realization of an event predicate in its scope. Our analysis contributes to the broader typological picture on frustrativity, and opens the door to a reanalysis of strong counter-to-expectation effects across languages. This paper is complementary Nadathur & Everdell 2025, supplying additional data and empirical arguments for the formal proposals in the other manuscript.

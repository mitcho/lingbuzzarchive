Two flavors of control in Georgian: decide and try
Richard Luo
June 2025
 

Against the common assumption that control predicates license a fixed type of obligatory control (OC) construction with respect to the semantic interpretation of their controllee argument in the embedded clause, partial vs. exhaustive (Landau 2000), I present evidence for a more dynamic view of complementation by examining two clause-embedding verbs in Georgian. Georgian (Kartvelian) provides a rich testing ground for control theory, given that it has two different strategies for deriving OC constructions: finite control with subjunctive clauses, and non-finite control with nominalized predicates or masdars. This paper argues that it is not only the selectional restrictions of the embedding predicate, but also the syntactic nature of the complement, which determines the status of which OC gets triggered, compatible with Wurmbrand and Lohninger's (2023) synthesis model of clausal complementation.

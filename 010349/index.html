Temperature in symbolic understanding
Andras Kornai, Fable Claude Fable 5.1
September 2026
 

The same copular syntax that yields the tradition's favorite truth condition, \textit{snow is white}, yields the animal rights movement's favorite slogan, \textit{meat is murder}. We argue that the difference between the two lives in a single parameter of the \textit{evaluation}, temperature, and that the term is not a metaphor: the parameter so named is literally the one that Harmonic Grammar and maximum entropy phonotactics fit to gradient acceptability data. The attention mechanism of transformers runs at the fixed temperature $1/\sqrt{d}$, and deployed language models expose a second instance of the same parameter to their users as the sampling knob. Grammaticality stars, analytic truths, thematic role assignment, anaphora resolution, and rhetorical force are readouts of one Gibbs competition at different operating points. We work through the mechanism on \textit{meat is murder} and its political cousins \textit{property is theft} and \textit{taxation is theft}, and on anaphora resolution in \textit{he beat him} discourses and Winograd schemas. A small experiment on a deployed model tells the two sites of the parameter apart: the sampling knob leaves the reading of \textit{meat is murder} untouched at every setting, the evaluation instruction flips it, and the model's refusal, when it refuses, localizes at the one taught boundary the analysis predicts. A self-contained appendix develops the machinery -- Gibbs distributions, annealing, energy landscapes, softmax, regularization -- assuming nothing beyond linear algebra and one-variable calculus.

Toward a topological theory of the aspectual conceptualization of events
Baptiste Loreau Unger
September 2024
 

I present a mathematically intelligible theory of event conceptualization, known as lexical aspect within linguistics, based on Croft’s (2012, Verbs: Aspect and Causal Structure, OUP) diagram-based account. Croft defined aspectual types using sequences of qualitative states, giving full status to the qualitative component of the unfolding of events. I make this idea formal using objects from general topology, by defining each aspectual type as the conjunction of a qualitative topological space, together with a continuous trajectory within that space. The topology on the space only distinguishes qualitative states inasmuch as they differ with respect to the unfolding of the event, discarding otherwise irrelevant distinctions between event types. Continuous transformations can change one space into another by collapsing distinctions, regimenting the shifts in granularity proposed by Croft for aspectual coercion. This preliminary, more constrained framework for event conceptualization already allows us to evaluate existing aspectual distinctions and operations, and conjecture new ones. In light of this theory, I differ from Croft in my treatment of semelfactives, arguing that they are telic predicates with a special activity reading, similar to Rothstein (2008, “Two puzzles for a theory of lexical aspect: Semelfactives and Degree Achievements”), who proposed semelfactives were activity predicates with naturally atomic minimal events. I also deny aspectual status to nonincremental accomplishments (solve the puzzle) and irreversibility, suggesting that the proposed diagnostics are sensitive to other inference-making mechanisms. Finally, I conjecture transformations to explain the effect of aspectual verbs, which could conceivably help formulate an algorithm for computing the presuppositions of these soft triggers, based on the aspectual representations provided by the theory.

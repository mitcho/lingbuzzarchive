Lambek linguistically: Towards a category theoretic semantics
Miriam S. Schmidt
May 2026
 

This paper develops a category theoretic framework for natural language semantics grounded in the Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence, with particular focus on intensional and hyperintensional phenomena. I argue that both classical simple type theories and modern record type theories inherit a fundamentally extensional notion of meaning from functional completeness, forcing intensional distinctions to be encoded through additional parameters, worlds, or type-theoretic complexity. In contrast, the present framework weakens functional completeness from uniqueness to representability within intensional domains, thereby allowing morphisms to differ structurally despite identical extensional behavior. Building on a two-domain architecture separating extensional and intensional objects, intensionality and its effects can be analyzed locally. Propositions then are not identified as truth-valued functions but as structured categorical diagrams inducing semantic evaluations. This permits a strictly relational notion of meaning grounded in Yoneda embeddings rather than extensional identity alone. The resulting system exemplarily derives factivity and perspective-dependence as structural constraints on morphisms and intensional subcategories, rather than as lexical stipulations or contextual parameters. The paper thus proposes a genuinely category theoretic foundation for intensional semantics in which hyperintensional distinctions naturally emerge from the internal structure of the semantic category itself.

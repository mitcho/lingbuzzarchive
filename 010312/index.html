Multilingual development: English in a global context, by Peter Siemund (book review)
Norbert Francis
April 2026
 

The way that the author has framed the problem of multilingualism has presented us with a dilemma. Just as during an earlier expansion of scholarship and learning – when East Asia and Europe each needed their lingua franca – today’s global imperative for a language of international communication has selected English. At the same time as English-language venues have become the indispensable tool of research and cross-cultural discussion in almost every discipline, they have also suffered the effects of an impending corruption, particularly in academia. More than for any other major or cross-national language, publication in English, in print and online, has become the favoured channel for propagating a new genre of pseudo-science and anti-scientific critique of the advances of the Enlightenment. Depending on the prospects for greater plurality, a shift towards a new international linguistic multi-polarity may not be as impractical as it once appeared to be. Journal of Multilingual and Multicultural Development (2026) 47, 3: 1950-1952.

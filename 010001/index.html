The Grammar of Absence
Ludovico Franco
May 2026
 

While comitative and instrumental relations have received extensive attention in the typological and generative literature, their negative counterpart—the caritive or abessive construction (e.g., without, sans, senza)—remains structurally under-theorized. Descriptive typologies cross-linguistically observe that expressions of absence are systematically more complex and heavily marked than expressions of presence (Stolz et al. 2007). This paper proposes a formal explanation for this markedness asymmetry based on a Double-Relator Model (DRM) and mereotopology. We argue that while comitative/instrumental markers are primitive relational heads computing simple inverse inclusion (⊇), abessive markers are Axial Parts (AxPs) or Relational Nouns. Drawing on Varzi’s (2007) mereotopology, we show that grammaticalizing an "absence" cognitively amounts to creating a topological void, an operation that requires projecting a discrete spatial/abstract boundary (an AxP) to grant "ontological dignity" to the empty region. Integrating semantic insights from Matushansky (2019) on kind- denoting remnants and Phillips (2022) on negative existentials, we demonstrate that caritive constructions uniformly project a bipartite syntactic structure, elegantly accounting for the morphosyntactic behavior of caritives across Indo-European and non-Indo-European languages.

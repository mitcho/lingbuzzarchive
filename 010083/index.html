Resolving Relatedness in a Reconstructed Lexicon: A Probabilistic Method for Proto-Language Internal Reconstruction
Aya Katz
June 2026
 

The standard comparative method in historical linguistics relies on written evidence to establish relatedness. In reconstructed proto-languages, where such evidence is unavailable, claims of internal connections are often considered unprovable. This paper introduces a probabilistic method for assessing whether phonemic similarity between two forms in a closed system is likely to be coincidental. Applying the method to Proto-Indo-European, we show that the three-phoneme root h₁és-, shared by the copula and the third-person pronoun paradigm, has a probability of coincidental identity of approximately 1 in 27,000 under uniform assumptions, and roughly 1 in 13,000 under frequency-adjusted estimates. This result holds across major competing reconstructions of PIE. Pronouns and copulas, as core grammatical elements, are among the least borrowable items cross-linguistically, supporting the closed-system assumption required by the calculation. Evidence from six daughter branches—Sanskrit, Greek, Avestan, Latin, Hittite, and Old Irish—confirms the persistence of the h₁és- root in both paradigms, with the full form appearing where an ending follows and the reduced h₁e- in the bare nominative due to regular final -s loss. Hittite provides direct attestation of the laryngeal. The pattern is further reinforced by independent two-be systems (e.g., Old Irish and Spanish), which consistently assign the h₁és- reflex to the equational/identificational function. A parallel pronoun-copula root identity in Biblical Hebrew (Katz 1996) suggests this alignment reflects a recurring cognitive association rather than a PIE-specific accident. The methodological contribution—probabilistic synchronic analysis as a tool for internal reconstruction in closed systems—extends beyond PIE to other proto-languages with reliable phonemic inventories and core vocabulary.

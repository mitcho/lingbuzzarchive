Agent-oriented modality in the languages of North America
Jessica K. Ivani, Taras Zakharko
June 2026
 

This study examines the morphosyntactic and functional encoding of agent-oriented modality in a maximally diverse sample of languages from North America. Focusing on necessity, obligation, ability, and desire, we show that agent-oriented modality is expressed through a wide range of construction types, including tense-aspect-mood morphology, modal verbs, non-verbal formatives, and complex clauses. We show that clustering the constructions by their formal properties yields three groups that align closely with the modal functions of necessity, ability, and desire. We further show that the constructions encoding different modal functions tend to differ in their morphosyntactic profiles, with strong necessity and obligation in particular showing systematic associations with tense marking, and weak and strong necessity with mood marking, respectively. These findings suggest that agent-oriented modality is not a uniform grammatical category, but a structured functional domain whose subtypes recruit material from different areas of grammar. The study contributes to a typological understanding of modality by combining constructional detail and an autotypologizing approach to cross-linguistic comparison

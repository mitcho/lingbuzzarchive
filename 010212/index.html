Two arguments for a movement analysis of Negative Doubling
Tommaso Mattiuzzi, Giacomo Presotto, Cecilia Poletto
August 2026
 

This paper presents novel evidence from two Northern Italian dialects supporting a movement-based analysis of Negative Doubling , in which a single-negation reading arises from the co-occurrence of a negative marker and one or more negative indefinites (Zeijlstra 2004). Under this approach, sentential negation is base-merged with the negative indefinite and subsequently splits from it during the derivation (Blanchette 2015; Tubau et al. 2024; Presotto et al. submitted). This analysis makes two empirical predictions that are not expected under Agree-based accounts (Haegeman & Lohndal 2010; Manzini & Pescarini 2024; Zeijlstra 2004, 2022). First, the base-generated constituent containing both the sentential negator and the negative indefinite should indeed be able to surface as a single constituent. Second, because the sentential negator occupies a different structural position when doubling a negative indefinite than when it is the sole exponent of sentential negation, this contrast may be reflected in morphological alternations. We show that both predictions are borne out in two Northern Italian dialects, providing new evidence for a movement-based analysis of Negative Doubling and shedding light on its underlying derivational mechanics.

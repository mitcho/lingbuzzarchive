Incorporation [handbook chapter]
Michelle Yuan
July 2026
 

This chapter overviews typological and theoretical investigations of noun incorporation, a grammatical phenomenon whereby a nominal argument of a verb, typically its object, may be morphologically integrated into the verb complex. This chapter considers incorporation also to include 'pseudo' noun incorporation, in which the noun-verb relationship is based on linear adjacency rather than affixation, as well as 'obligatory' noun incorporation, which arises when the incorporation process is required for certain verbs or certain nouns. Though it is an open question whether incorporation is a unified phenomenon to begin with, syntactic research on this topic has generally been guided by a stable set of theoretical questions, crosscutting the abovementioned distinctions. Overall, incorporation is a productive testing ground for theories of syntax and its interfaces and showcases the importance of diverse cross-linguistic evidence in the development of linguistic theory.

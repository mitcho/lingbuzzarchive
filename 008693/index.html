Higher-order structures and parasitism in grammar
Johannes Schneider
December 2024
 

This dissertation deals with the phenomenon of parasitism in syntax. I compare two parasitic phenomena, showing that they have a number of properties in common. I argue that parasitism is a genuine phenomenon in its own right, distinct from other process interactions like feeding and is better analyzed as the outcome of a single, more powerful operation. In the first half, I investigate parasitic ellipsis, a process where otherwise unavailable ellipsis becomes available in the presence of another ellipsis. I show that this occurs not only in PP remnants of gapping in Russian (Fitzgibbons 2014) but also in German, Estonian and English, and with various other categories as well (C, A, N, V). I provide an empirical generalization describing under which circumstances parasitic ellipsis can occur. I discuss various difficulties that approaches encounter that analyze parasitic ellipsis as two separate but dependent operations and instead model it as the outcome of a single, higher-order operation. I provide such an analysis within Minimalist Grammars (Stabler 1997), a framework from mathematical linguistics. In the second half of the dissertation I formalize a higher-order structure building operation (3rd-merge), in analogy to the higher-order ellipsis operation. I show that such an operation leads to the growth of a secondary root in a syntactic tree. I posit that the fundamental purpose of 3rd-merge is to connect verbs with their nominal arguments. DPs are conceived of as small secondary roots (Thoms 2019). Verbs select for NPs within this root via this new operation, but the D-layer sits on top of the NP as an independent root, distinct from the clausal root. This reconciles seemingly contradictory arguments for what constitutes the head of the nominal domain. The possibility exists for secondary roots to grow further. There are two options. The extended secondary root can remain independent. I argue that this corresponds to the structure of Horn amalgams (Lakoff 1974, van Riemsdijk 2006). The other option is that the secondary root is reconnected, leading to a cyclic structure. If the connecting element is moved, this leads to the creation of apparently two separate gaps in a 1D string. I argue that this corresponds to the phenomenon of parasitic gaps (Ross 1967, Engdahl 1983, Kasai 2010). This analysis achieves the same result as the one for parasitic ellipsis, namely that the parasitic phenomenon is again the outcome of a single operation, not two separate but dependent ones. In such an approach, the existence of the latter two phenomena is a consequence of the structure of the nominal domain. This also derives the restriction of Horn amalgams and parasitic gaps to nominals, further suggesting an underlying common core to these three phenomena.

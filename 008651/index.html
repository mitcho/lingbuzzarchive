Representing and Learning Stress: A MaxEnt Framework for Comparing Learning Across Grammatical Theories
Seung Suk Lee, Joe Pater, Brandon Prickett
October 2024
 

This paper examines learning of word stress patterns across grammatical theories, within a Maximum Entropy learning framework. This framework achieves results comparable to the state-of-the-art on a well-studied test of stress learning, which uses a set of foot-based constraints as the grammatical theory. The typological coverage of this constraint set has not previously been examined. A computational study shows that it can represent about half of a sample of attested languages, and that an alternative foot-based constraint set, as well as a grid-based one, succeed in capturing more of them. In terms of learning, the foot-based constraint sets have high rates of success on the languages they can represent, even in face of the structural ambiguity introduced by feet. Comparisons of learning speed on different language types across grammatical theories reveal differences amongst them that would not be evident in a comparison of their representational capacity alone.

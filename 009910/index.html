TINA: A Hybrid Model of Brazilian Portuguese Pronunciation Structure
Alexandre Menezes Barroso
April 2026
 

Automated phonetic transcription for Brazilian Portuguese is not well modeled as a flat grapheme-to-phoneme task, because usable outputs must encode syllabification, lexical stress, and dialect-sensitive realizations. This paper presents TINA, a compact hybrid system that predicts a structured, archiphonemic representation with neural components for syllables, phoneme content, and stress, and then expands that representation into surface-oriented variants through explicit phonological rules. TINA is evaluated against native-speaker pseudoword behavior, with qualitative output inspection on familiar lexical material confirming that the system produces well-formed structured transcriptions. On pseudowords, it continues to produce well-formed outputs, captures the major opposition between final and penultimate stress, and responds strongly to final-syllable structure, showing that it generalizes beyond memorized lexical mappings. Yet it also overregularizes speaker behavior: it suppresses antepenultimate stress and treats some right-edge cues too categorically. TINA is therefore an interpretable model of BP pronunciation structure rather than a state-of-the-art neural G2P architecture.

The Hand That Gives: PIE *der- and the give/take root: an alternative reconstruction proposed for consideration
J. M. Chavis, A.I. Claude
May 2026
 

A comparative cluster first proposed by Marstrander in 1919 — Hittite dàr-na-áš (a measure-noun from "fist"), Old Irish dorn, Welsh dwrn, Gaulish durno-, Old Irish derna "palm," Greek δῶρον "palm of hand; gift," Greek δάρις (Hesychius, "span"), and Albanian dorë "hand" — entered Pokorny's IEW (1959: 203) and remained the standard handbook treatment for half a century. Three late-twentieth-century departures dismantled it: Schrijver (1995) labeled the δωρ-/δαρ- relation "obscure"; Beekes (2010) excluded the Celtic forms from δῶρον on grounds of u-vocalism; Matasović (2009) classified Celtic durno- as probably non-IE in origin; Demiraj (1990, 1997) reversed the Albanian connection on a Tagliavini citation that on examination does not deliver the rejection. This paper restores Marstrander's analysis on the formal foundation that was unavailable to him: Clayton's (2023) reconstruction of the *-wr̥-/-w(e/o)n- heteroclite class, with which Blažek (1999) had independently begun to formalize the same cognate set, plus a decomposition of de- as a free pre-PIE morpheme. Under this reading, the cluster reflects two derivational outputs on a single pre-PIE element de- "open hand": a locative-extended body-part noun de-r- (yielding δάρις, derna, Continental Celtic DERNV[, Albanian dorë) and a heteroclitic gift/measure-noun deh₃-wr̥-/-w(e/o)n- (yielding δῶρον, Latin dōnum, Celtic durno-, Latvian dūre, and the Anatolian witness tarawar "measure of capacity" with corresponding verb tarāwi(ya)- "to hand over, deliver"). The Celtic u that frustrated Beekes is the regular reflex of the Proto-Nuclear-IE metathesis *wr̥ > *ru > *ur. The δωρ-/δαρ- alternation that Schrijver labeled "obscure" is the strong/weak stem contrast of a single heteroclitic paradigm. The Albanian dorë connection rests on a 127-year published lineage (Meyer 1883 → La Piana 1937/1942 → Belardi 1948 → Pokorny 1959 → Schindler 1967 → Frisk → Beekes 2010 → DGE), with DGE's recent *deh₃r- essentially identifying the framework's *de-h₃-r- in different notation. Probability assessed at 40–50% conditional.

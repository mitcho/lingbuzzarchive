The Hand That Gives: PIE *der- and the give/take root: an alternative reconstruction proposed for consideration
J. M. Chavis, A.I. Claude
April 2026
 

This paper proposes that PIE *der- "hand, open palm" deserves consideration as an alternative reconstruction for the root traditionally written *deh₃- "to give." We offer the proposal not as a refutation of the standard reconstruction but as a hypothesis with a defined evidential burden, which we set out and try to discharge. The case rests on three observations. First, Greek δῶρον is attested in two senses simultaneously in the earliest layer of Greek literature — "gift" and "palm-breadth" — and the Hesychian gloss δάρις/δάρειρ "span of all fingers" preserves what looks like the zero-grade alternant of the same word in the same body-part domain. Second, the Celtic hand-words dorn / dwrn / dourn "fist" and derna / déarna "palm" form a coherent ablaut pair on a shared root with a documented Indo-European singulative formation, supported by the transparent compounds arddwrn "wrist" and imdorn "sword hilt." Third, Latvian dūre "fist" with long ū joins this comparative cluster from a third Indo-European branch. Schrijver (1995: 58) explicitly lists Greek δῶρον "length of the hand," Hesychian δάρις, and Latvian dūre as "possible cognates" of Welsh dwrn, and remarks that "the formal relation between δωρ- and δάρ- is obscure." The hypothesis we propose is one possible answer to that openly acknowledged question.

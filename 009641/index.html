The temporary-context theory of conditionals
Daniel Lassiter
December 2025
 

This paper proposes a minimal, but powerful, theory of indicative conditionals built around three components: a theory of local contexts; the idea that if-clauses function to create a temporary context (and do nothing else); and the claim that worlds outside the context of evaluation are treated, not as true or false, but as irrelevant to semantic evaluation. Conditional restriction of modals emerges as an automatic byproduct of the creation of temporary contexts, and the theory explains parallels between presupposition projection and domain restriction in both conditionals and disjunction. It also accounts for Stalnaker's Thesis, the equation between probabilities of conditionals and conditional probabilities. By deriving a trivalent semantics from more basic components, the temporary-context theory answers an explanatory challenge for trivalent theories of conditionals and points to a novel generalization: possibilities that lie outside the context of evaluation are systematically invisible to natural language interpretation.

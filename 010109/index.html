A Guide to Analysis in MaxEnt Optimality Theory, Chapters 1-5
Bruce P. Hayes, Claire Moore-Cantwell
July 2026
 

This document consists of the first five chapters of a projected textbook for Maximum Entropy Optimality Theory, a.k.a. “MaxEnt”. The text is directed to beginners (i.e. to linguists who are new to MaxEnt) and is meant as a “how-to” book, more pedagogical than polemical. The book is rooted in our view that there are many linguists who are in possession of gradient data (e.g. from observations of free variation, corpora, or experiments) and wish to develop a rigorous generative analysis of their findings — but lack an appropriate formal framework for doing so. We feel that MaxEnt is a very useful such framework, and hope to make it more accessible to researchers by making it easy to learn, and by offering practical advice based on our own research experience. We show how MaxEnt can be done effectively using Excel spreadsheets, and offer example analyses along with spreadsheet implementations. Chapter contents: Chapter 1: Purpose and orientation Chapter 2: The analysis of variation in outputs Case study: Lenition in Khalkha Mongolian Chapter 3: Using MaxEnt to study lexical frequency-matching Case study: Nasal substitution in Tagalog Chapter 4: The MaxEnt math Chapter 5: Research technique Statistical analysis, building a grammar, finding data1 Future chapters: (6) phonotactics, (7) learning, (8) fields other than phonology, (9) discussion of MaxEnt as a theory. We would be grateful for feedback, including suggestions for citation (bhayes@humnet.ucla.edu, moore.cantwell@ucla.edu).

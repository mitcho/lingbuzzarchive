Parasitic Participles in Faroese as Auxiliary Omission
Oddur Snorrason
October 2025
 

The relationship between auxiliaries and verbal inflection has been a debated issue in syntactic theory since Chomsky (1957) and contemporary theories disagree on whether to analyze the facts through a combination of syntactic selection and merger or head displacement (Halle and Marantz 1993, Lansik 1995, Harwood 2014) or in terms of inflection-as-agreement (Adger 2003, Bjorkman 2011, Wurmbrand 2012). An important argument for inflection-as-agreement comes from parasitic morphology in the Germanic languages. This paper presents a novel case of parasitic morphology in which the auxiliary HAVE itself can take vacuous inflection as predicted by inflectionas-agreement accounts. This kind of account also allows us to explain why Faroese parasitic morphology initially appears to be non-vacuous. Once we recognize that HAVE shows parasitic agreement and adopt the idea that participle morphology on the perfect auxiliary can trigger deletion of the auxiliary, the Faroese facts straightforwardly follow from syntactic agreement.

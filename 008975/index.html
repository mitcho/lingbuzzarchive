Distinct pathways to possessor A'-extraction in Mesoamerican languages
Andrew Hedding, Michelle Yuan
April 2025
 

The Mesoamerican linguistic area consists of several distinct language families, which share a number of structural features. In this paper, we compare the nature of possessor A'-extraction in the Tseltalan languages (Mayan) (based on the data and analysis of Aissen and Polian 2025) and in San Martín Peras Mixtec (Otomanguean). In both language types, wh-possessors may be fronted clause-initially, either with the rest of the complex possessive phrase (in a process known as 'pied-piping with inversion') or alone. However, we argue that, despite their surface-similar empirical profiles, possessor A'-extraction in Tseltalan and SMPM actually involves underlyingly divergent derivational pathways. We additionally propose that these distinct profiles result from several independent yet interrelated parametric differences between the two languages, such as the functional heads implicated in the derivation of verb-initiality, the size of nominals and its correlation to specificity, and the probing conditions of movement-triggering phase heads. Our analysis makes thus testable predictions about the A'-subextraction patterns of other Mesoamerican languages, specifically, how possessor A'-extraction might map to the array of parameter settings outlined above.

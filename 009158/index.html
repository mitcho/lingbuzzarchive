Clitics: Greek
Nikos Angelopoulos, Elena Anagnostopoulou
July 2025
 

This paper investigates the intricacies of Greek clitics, which offer a compelling testing ground for competing theories of Clitic Doubling. In turn, Clitic Doubling provides valuable insight into the correct analysis of clitics more broadly. We critically evaluate three prominent analyses of Clitic Doubling: a hybrid movement and base-generation analysis, a pure Agree-based analysis that dispenses with movement, and a strictly movement-driven approach. Through an examination of both distributional evidence and interpretive properties of clitics and clitic doubled objects, this paper argues for a movement-based analysis and presents conclusive evidence against non-movement/pure Agree analyses. While supporting a movement-based approach, we also acknowledge open questions—particularly regarding the relative ordering of dative and accusative clitics, and whether clitics function as semantically vacuous agreement markers or as pronominal elements carrying meaning. The paper will conclude by discussing future research directions to address these unresolved issues.

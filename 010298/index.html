Loanwords in Arabic in Different Historical Periods of Its Development
Maria Swanson
September 2026
 

The article discusses the history of lexical borrowings into Arabic. It breaks down the history of this process into five basic periods: pre-Islamic and early Islamic times, Omayyads-Abbasids (seventh-thirteenth centuries), the Ottoman Yoke (fifteenth-eighteenth centuries), the Arabic Renaissance and European Colonialism (nineteenth, early twentieth century), and the technological era (1970s-present). During each period radical changes in Arabic lexicon, including changes in borrowings into the Arabic language, took place. The author gives numerous examples and considers a variety of donor languages (ranging from other Semitic languages and Turkish to a number of Indo-European languages, including Persian and later various Western European languages). This work studies different strategies for assimilating new words (including loan translations (calques) and various phonological and morphological adaptions). The author also shows that many loanwords have been borrowed by linguists, writers and other intellectuals, who were looking for alternative to borrowing ways that have not always met the phonological and morphological norms of the Arabic language. While studying the history of borrowings into Arabic, the author touches upon the question about the wholeness of the Arabic language as a part of Arabic nationalism. She also attempts to analyze the character of future borrowings into Arabic. The significance of this article is not covering all the history of borrowings into Arabic and summing up numerous works devoted to specific aspects of the given topic. It can be used for academic research and teaching by linguists, historians and specialists in the Arabic studies.

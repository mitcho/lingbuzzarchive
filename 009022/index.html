A Perspective-and-Aspect-Driven System of Temporal Anchoring in Portuguese: Semantic Reanalysis of the Present Perfect and Other Cross-Romance Divergences
Shinya Makino
October 2025
 

This theoretical paper proposes a feature-based analysis of the indicative and subjunctive forms in Portuguese, grounded in four primitive anchoring-aspect features and their figurative extensions: [non-assertive], [VP∈past], [finished] and [assumptive]. In contrast to the tense-driven systems typical of other Romance languages such as Spanish and French, Portuguese is assumed to employ a perspective-and-aspect-driven system. This structural opposition between the two systems accounts for a range of cross-Romance differences, including the functional erosion of the future tenses and the associated future-oriented use of the preterite, the remarkable vitality of the preterite in this language (PPS), and the uniquely Portuguese reanalysis of the present perfect indicative (PPC) ― [−VP∈past, +finished, [✓initiated, ✓result-stative, −finished]] → [−VP∈past, ✓initiated, ✓stative, −finished] ― due to the functional competition with the PPS [−VP∈past, +finished]. -------------------- How to cite: Makino, S.

Strengthening and weakening Addressee Compliance by constraining discourse future: the case of Mandarin `ba'
Xuetong Yuan
July 2026
 

This paper investigates how opposing discourse effects of either weakening or strengthening the sentential force can be derived from constraining projected discourse future, through a case study of the Mandarin discourse particle `ba'. `ba' has a weakening effect in declarative and imperative uses, but a strengthening effect in morphologically marked interrogatives. Previous literature has either treated the two types of uses of `ba' as distinct particles, or fails to provide a unified account across different clause types. This paper argues that `ba' conveys a single conventional meaning: it signals potential or existing addressee non-compliance in discourse. The core idea is that `ba' presupposes an epistemic asymmetry between speaker and addressee, and contributes a discourse effect that keeps the projected common ground updates unchanged from input to output. In declaratives, this blocked projection of addressee acceptance reflects an assumption that the addressee may not comply, yielding a weakening effect. In interrogatives, failing to project any possible answers signals a conversational crisis: the question itself seems unanswerable. Restating a seemingly unanswerable question with `ba' strengthens its interrogative force. Implemented within a Farkas & Bruce (2010)-style Table model, this account also successfully explains the weakening effect of `ba' in imperatives. The paper also contributes to formal models of discourse dynamics, in particular the role of its still poorly-understood projected components.

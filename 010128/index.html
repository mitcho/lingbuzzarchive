Chapter Three:The Parallel Multi-Dimensional Syntactic Building Hypothesis
CHANG kangjie
July 2026
 

This chapter proposes the Parallel Multi-Dimensional Syntactic Building Hypothesis, which seeks to address a foundational yet often underarticulated question within a theory of free Merge: if Merge is free, why does syntactic building not proceed chaotically from arbitrary combinations in the workspace, but instead give rise to stable and interpretable syntactic structures? The chapter first distinguishes generation from building, arguing that traditional syntactic analysis typically presents the final convergent tree while leaving the initiation of syntactic building insufficiently explained. Drawing on the later minimalist conception of free Merge, the chapter argues that syntactic objects in the workspace may, in principle, undergo pairwise Merge. Each possible instance of initial Merge opens a candidate derivational path, and these paths unfold in parallel as distinct parallel building dimensions. Such dimensions are not pre-classified as correct or incorrect prior to Merge; rather, once a phase-level unit has been constructed, they undergo evaluation at the C-I and SM interfaces. Dimensions that pass interface evaluation converge, while those that fail to receive interpretation or externalisation crash. On this view, the syntactic tree ordinarily analysed is one convergent result preserved after interface filtering within a broader space of parallel candidate derivations. The chapter also distinguishes the present notion of parallel building dimension from Pan and Du’s derivational dimension: the former concerns the multi-pathicity opened by free Merge within the workspace, whereas the latter primarily concerns the internal multi-planarity of structures involving Pair-Merge, adjuncts, and labeling. The hypothesis is then given a preliminary formalisation and defended against the charge of excessive complexity through a distinction between operational complexity and candidate-space complexity. The chapter closes by identifying several open questions, including psychological reality, limits on candidate dimensions, the content of interface evaluation, empirical prediction, and integration with other mechanisms of the Minimalist Programme.

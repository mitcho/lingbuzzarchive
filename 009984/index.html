Expanding the nominal in English ACC- and POSS-ing nominalizations
Aidan Katson
May 2026
 

Clausal nominalizations, like English ACC- and POSS-ing gerunds, exhibit both verbal and nominal properties. A classic observation is that these properties trade-off with one another: as a nominalization exhibits more verbal properties, it has fewer nominal ones (Chomsky 1970). This leads to nominalizations often being analyzed as "mixed extended projections" (Borsley & Kornfilt 2000): single abstract functional sequences whose limited positions are split between a verbal and nominal component (assuming 'parallelism' between domains; Chomsky 1970, Abney 1987, Grimshaw 1990, Szabolcsi 1994). As such a mixed extended projection is "filled up" with more verbal material, there is less room for nominal material. This view predicts that nominalizations of large verbal structures should necessarily be deficient in nominal structure and properties. I present novel data on English ACC- and POSS-ing gerunds in measurement constructions to argue for an alternative view of nominalization: there is no necessary trade-off in nominal and verbal material and properties within nominalizations. I argue that despite containing extensive verbal functional structure (up to TP, in the case of the ACC-ing gerund), both ACC- and POSS-ing gerunds also sport a full nominal functional spine, from n to D. I therefore pursue a Distributed Morphology-aligned approach that (re)categorization is uniformly governed by categorizing heads like n and v. Heads like n may combine with more or less arbitrarily-sized structures and may then project a new functional spine of their own category.

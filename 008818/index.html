Rethinking Korean How-questions: A Cross-linguistic Study
Wei-Tien Dylan Tsai
February 2025
 

Ettehkey ‘how’ in Korean can be interpreted as either causal, manner, or resultative how according to its “height of interpretation” (Hacquard 2006). Interestingly, it is immune from strong island effects when interpreted as manner or resultative, whereas it is island-sensitive when interpreted as causal. This observation presents a challenge to the Noun versus Adverb Generalization of wh-in-situ construals (Tsai 1999a; Fujii et al. 2014), since ettehkey does not seem to contain a nominal head. In this paper, it is argued that the difficulty is only apparent, and the unselective binding analysis can be extended to Korean how-questions with manner and resultative ettehkey being decomposed into a couple of functional elements such as an indeterminate noun (Kuroda 1965), whereas Korean causal how has evolved into a genuine adverb in the left periphery. We also draw cross-linguistic evidence from Chinese and Japanese to further support this novel approach in combining the insights from the Syntactic Cartography and the Distributed Morphology.

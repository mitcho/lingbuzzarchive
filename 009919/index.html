Does the Williams Cycle apply to Mandarin Chinese?
Fulang Chen, Ka-Fai Yip
April 2026
 

This paper addresses a theoretical tension in accounting for the correlation between the landing site and the locality domain of movement—whether to reduce it to the A/A'-distinction (Ban on Improper Movement) or to hard-wire it to the grammar as the Williams Cycle (WC), which has recently been claimed to apply to Mandarin Chinese by Yan & Meadows (2025). By refuting their arguments, we argue that the WC does not apply to Mandarin and therefore cannot be universal. Specifically, we show that the apparent WC effects in Mandarin either follow from independently attested A/A'-distinction in the language, or simply do not hold due to incomplete generalization. The Mandarin case highlights the importance of cross-linguistic variation in adjudicating between competing theoretical accounts.

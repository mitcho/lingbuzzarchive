Epenthetic Inheritance
Kate Mooney
October 2024
 

In this paper, I revisit the typology of consonant epenthesis to challenge the view that epenthetic consonants are inserted segments unrelated to surrounding material. Instead, I propose Epenthetic Inheritance: a theory in which epenthetic consonants are mutations of existing articulatory gestures. Closely following work in traditional phonetics (Sievers 1881, Millardet 1910), Articulatory Phonology (Browman & Goldstein 1990, Gick 1999), and Optimality Theory (Staroverov 2014), I argue that epenthetic consonant quality is primarily inherited, constrained by faith rather than markedness. Gestural representations are adopted to explain why epenthetic consonants tend to minimally perturb existing articulatory dynamics, and also why they are often immune to phonotactic restrictions placed on segments (cf. Hall 2003, 2006). The result is a unified account that explains both the quality and phonological invisibility of epenthetic consonants, bridging insights from mutation and insertion theories. Lastly, it imposes a strong cut between phonetic and phonological operations: Phonology can only use the gestural material it already has, whereas morphophonology alone can truly insert and remove segments.

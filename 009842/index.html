Hearsay and (non-)endorsement
Natasha Korotkova
March 2026
 

This paper is devoted to what I will call the ‘non-endorsement puzzle’: a distinguished feature of hearsay evidentials such that they allow the speaker to disavow the prejacent. Ever since it was ﬁrst brought to the forefront of formal semantic research by Martina Faller (2002), the puzzle has been the subject of lively debates in the literature. My goals in this paper are fourfold: (i) to delineate the desiderata for a theory of non-endorsement by placing evidentials in a larger context of speech and attitude reports; (ii) to disentangle the modelling of the core semantics of evidentials from the modelling of the discourse effects of utterances that contain them; (iii) to propose theory-neutral diagnostics of evidential intensionality, thus vindicating a modal analysis for at least some hearsay evidentials; (iv) to argue, building on (Korotkova 2020), that the evidential signal is not conventionally marked as not-at-issue content and that it is at-issue in discourses whose QUD structure allows it (cf. Hunter 2016; Koev 2022 on parenthetical constructions). The existing literature focuses on the modeling question: how to best formulate the denotation of hearsay evidentials to make non-endorsement non-contradictory. At the same time, there is an overlooked dimension to the puzzle: the overall discourse function of hearsay utterances with overt non-endorsement. As I will argue, the view that evidentials aren’t conventionally not-at-issue allows us to make signiﬁcant headway in understanding this latter question.

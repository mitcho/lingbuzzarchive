The Dynamic Existential in Tagalog
Joseph Sabbagh
January 2026
 

This paper is focused on existential (and possessive) sentences in Tagalog that are constructed around a verbal existential verb (which I refer to as the Dynamic Existential). Although this verb appears to be an unaccusative verb in its use in existential sentences, I argue that it is actually a transitive verb equivalent to \textsc{have}, with a covert external argument (which is overt in possessive sentences constructed from the same verb). In addition to providing a unification of the syntax of existential and possessive constructions, I argue that this analysis explains the morpho-syntax of dynamic existential constructions (relating, in particular, to the distribution of case) that would be otherwise mysterious if the existential verb were a true unaccusative. The proposed covert external argument in existential sentences funds precedent in `impersonal' like constructions in other languages, and as I briefly discuss. I propose that this external argument (which is overt in possessive sentences) is an (implicit) locative, and discuss briefly some implications for unifying a prefix ka- that is found in the existential verb and subject experiencer verbs in the language (as discussed by Kaufman 2011, 2012). If the analysis proposed here is on the right track, it suggests that Tagalog falls at the lower end of a spectrum for availability of ``expletive'' external arguments (Icelandic being at the high end, Norwegian and English being somewhere in the middle).

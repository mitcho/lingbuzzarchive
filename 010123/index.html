Decomposing Place Names: Evidence from the Hebrew Construct State
Idan Caspi
July 2026
 

This paper claims that place-denoting proper names (i.e., toponyms) are structurally decomposable. Evidence for this claim comes from Hebrew toponyms displaying noun-noun concatenation (e.g., Tel-Aviv). I show that such toponyms share properties with the Hebrew Construct State construction. Using Borer's (2013) typology of Constructs, I investigate how these toponyms pattern with Construct subclasses using a range of diagnostics. Accordingly, it is demonstrated that such toponyms are decomposable in two distinct ways. Specifically, they instantiate two subclasses: (a) toponymic Modificational-Constructs, a grammatically transparent class, and (b) toponymic Compounds, a grammatically opaque class. Each subclass corresponds to a distinct structural configuration. By structural parsimony, I propose that these configurations reduce to structures independently available for Constructs in the language. I further claim that the non-compositional meaning of toponymic Compounds corresponds to geo-ontological categories (e.g., [CITY], [VILLAGE], [HILL]; cf. Smith & Mark 2001), suggesting a principled mapping between geographical concepts and syntactic structure.

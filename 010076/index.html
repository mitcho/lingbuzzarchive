Mandarin verb doubling and its implications for the syntactic theory
Qiuhao Charles Yan
September 2025
 

This thesis is concerned with Mandarin verb doubling and its implications for the syntactic theory. I propose a phrasal movement analysis of verb doubling, namely that the doubling configuration in question is derived from verbal phrasal movement out of an articulated verbal structure. After the overall introduction in chapter 1, chapter 2 lays out the core properties of the verbal phrasal movement involved in verb doubling, that is, the fronted verbal phrase can target two landing sites in the clausal spine. I argue that this two-landing-site analysis has both theoretical and empirical advantages over other hypothetical alternatives. Chapter 3 discusses the role of postverbal adverbials in verb doubling constructions. I argue that this group of elements in fact comprises two sub-groups (i.e., freely-adjoined adjuncts and selected arguments), and propose that the way they are introduced to verbal projections differs from one to the other. Chapter 4 puts forwards the idea that the fronted verbal phrases that engage in the formation of verb doubling can be larger than simple VPs. This proposal not only offers a direct account of distinct patterns that different ditransitive verb doubling configurations exhibit, but also makes correct predictions about the systematic distribution of multiple postverbal adverbials within verb doubling. Chapter 5 develops an analysis of realising verb doubling effects at PF, consisting of a series of operations that take place post-syntactically. I propose that various doubling effects, thus, can be attributed to different combinations of these PF operations. Chapter 6 examines the locality profile of verb doubling in bi-clausal contexts. I show that long-distance verb doubling behaves differently in three embedding contexts, and propose that the varying locality profiles of verb doubling can be properly accounted for by the Williams Cycle. Chapter 7 concludes the thesis.

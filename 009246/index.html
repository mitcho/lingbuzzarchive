Inclusive gender in Spanish. Subverting the heteronormative taxonomy by expressive means
Andrés Saab
July 2026
 

This paper discusses the linguistic implications of the inclusive gender strategy in Spanish from a morphosemantic point of view. It is argued that inclusive gender consists of a peculiar manipulation of rules of grammar (Corver 2016). Specifically, the adding of the inclusive exponent -e in pronouns and human nouns signals the presence of a new mixed expressive morpheme in the language (McCready 2010). The truth-conditional value of -e is modeled as a partial identity function introducing the presupposition that the entity is human, whereas the expressive value gives us a profile of the speaker regarding their belief system with respect to the dominant binary taxonomy. Given the mixed nature of the new item, its use impacts not only on the expressive dimension of meaning but also on truth-conditions. Concretely, the new gender system gives up a version of the human-cloning rule according to which under some conditions human nouns in Spanish are semantically either female or male (Harris 1991). Thus, the strategy can be seen as an attempt to subvert the type of binary taxonomy that can be inferred from the current gender system.

Inclusive gender in Spanish. Subverting the heteronormative taxonomy by expressive means
Andrés Saab
August 2025
 

This paper discusses the linguistic implications of the inclusive gender strategy in Spanish. The core thesis is that inclusive gender consists of a form of expressive manipulation in Corver’s (2016) sense, i.e., deviation of rules of grammar. Yet, the strategy directly impacts on representation, not only on expressivity, by subverting the type of heteronormative taxonomy that can be inferred from the current gender system. Concretely, by adding the new gender morpheme -e, the rule of restricted Human Cloning (a version of Harris’ 1991 original rule), which creates [male] and [female] nominal oppositions in DPs denoting atomic entities, is excluded from the system. I also compare the inclusive strategy with another strategy of linguistic intervention, namely, slur reappropriation, i.e., the use of slur words by members of the target of derogation. The two strategies contrast in several respects. For instance, whereas the inclusive strategy manipulates (anti-)presuppositions to restrict the masculine form to less contexts of use, reclamation manipulates use-conditional meanings to give the slur word more, not less, contexts of use.

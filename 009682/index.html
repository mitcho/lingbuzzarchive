'Same' and 'different' are not additive presupposition triggers - Reply to Mikkelsen & Hardt 2024
Isabelle Charnavel
December 2025
 

This paper is a reply to Mikkelsen & Hardt’s 2024 article analyzing 'same' and 'different' as involving an additive presuppositional element TOO akin to overt 'too'. This hypothesis has several virtues: it provides a uniform analysis for both 'same' and 'different' under all of their (internal, external and comparative-like) readings; it explains several effects (poorly discussed so far) exhibited by 'same': parallelism and island effects, as well as analogies with 'too'. But I show that this additive analysis faces several challenges questioning its explanatory power: the uniformity of the account rests on some stipulations, and the analogies between 'same'/'different' and 'too' are only partial. Furthermore, I show that Charnavel’s 2015 alternative account likening 'same'/'different' to comparatives can derive the effects observed by Mikkelsen & Hardt without raising the problems induced by TOO incorporation.

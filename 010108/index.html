The Death of Mystery Has Been Greatly Exaggerated: Comments on Sripada et al. (2026)
Vincent Carchidi
July 2026
 

In response to Futrell & Mahowald’s (2025) paper on the role of Large Language Models (LLMs) in linguistics, Sripada et al. (2026) strikingly argue that LLMs “illuminate” the mechanisms underlying what Chomsky calls the creative aspect of language use (CALU). Such illumination promises, they argue, to bring this once-intractable problem under the heel of scientific inquiry by advancing our understanding of the mechanisms by which individuals select situation-appropriate expressions from an unbounded class of expressions. Sripada et al. draw attention to an important, foundational issue in generative linguistics, but their paper mischaracterizes CALU. The mischaracterization turns principally on the notion of stimulus-independence and how this relates to the capacity for unbounded linguistic production. These comments seek to correct this mischaracterization by showing that the problem of CALU is deeper and more distinctive than they let on. Likewise, misunderstandings of Chomsky’s invocation of CALU are clarified.

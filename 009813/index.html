How many perspectives to explain how to change a tire? A cinematic approach to analyzing depicting constructions in signed language discourse (poster)
Anke Müller
October 2022
 

Sign Languages are known for their iconic devices of depiction, such as constructed action and classifier constructions. These depicting constructions are used to show scenes, actions and situations in their temporal unfolding, and they are said to show many spatial perspectives of an event. But what does this look like in a stretch of discourse? How many perspectives are there and how can these different spatial vantage points be identified and labelled for further analysis? How are these perspectives organized to give an oriented view on events and scene? To answer these questions, the poster proposes using a cinematic approach to analyzing depicting constructions in signed language discourse, thus profiting from a discipline that has developed elaborate descriptive tools for film motion images. Depicting constructions are grounded in visual imagery and can be regarded as ‚signed motion images‘, analogous to the concept of a film motion image. Assuming a virtual camera and the signer and his immediate surrounding as screen, signed motion images can be analyzed with respect to their spatiotemporal features of depiction, for example perspective. The analysis of perspective includes viewpoint, orientation of object and distance to object and can be presented in a floor plan. The analysis demonstrated on the poster starts with a broken-down car on the side lane of an express way. The floor plan displays the scene and its development in top view, with all camera locations and movements as well as locations, actions and movements of the character and the props. Further research questions the poster addresses concern the textual organization of perspectives. How are they indicated, are there specific patterns or editing strategies that help meaning construction?

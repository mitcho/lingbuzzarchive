p-onset-sonority-hypothesis

This study investigates the realization of word-initial /p/ in English consonant clusters, focusing on the apparent contrast between clusters such as /pr/ and /pl/, where /p/ is consistently realized, and clusters such as /ps/, /pn/, and /pt/, where /p/ is typically absent. Using a systematic analysis of 2,586 entries from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, the study examines the relationship between /p/ realization and the phonological class of the following segment. The results show a strong association between /p/ realization and following-segment sonority: /p/ is retained consistently in /pr/ and /pl/ clusters, while it is overwhelmingly absent in the core /ps/, /pn/, and /pt/ groups. A Fisher’s exact test confirms that the observed association is statistically significant. Rare and variable forms, including pshaw, pshew, and pschent, are examined separately as potential counterexamples. The findings are consistent with the Sonority Sequencing Principle and with established accounts of English phonotactics and the historical adaptation of consonant clusters. The study does not propose a new phonological law, but provides a corpus-based examination of the distribution of initial /p/ realization and identifies areas requiring further investigation.

Grammatical markers and inflectional categories: Reconciling the two perspectives
Martin Haspelmath
April 2025
 

Morphologists often distinguish between two seemingly incompatible ways of viewing word structures: (i) the morph-based view, which typically describes morphological patterns by grammatical markers that are added to roots, and (ii) the paradigm-based view, which typically describes morphological patterns as realizing inflectional categories. This paper notes that these two views need not be incompatible: Everyone agrees that morphs (roots and markers) arise from paradigms, and everyone agrees that inflected forms are not always composed concatenatively of roots and affixes. Markers and categories represent two different ways of talking, but ultimately, all of morphosyntax relies on basic notions such as meanings, forms, classes, constructions, and paradigms. “Categories” are either (classes of) inflectional meanings or classes of inflected forms. The two perspectives can thus be reconciled and need not be seen as being in competition or even mutually incompatible.

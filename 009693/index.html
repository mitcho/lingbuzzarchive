Animal Linguistics as an Integrative Framework for Animal Communication
Philippe Schlenker, Nicolas Mathevon, Masato Hagiwara, Olivier Pietquin, Emmanuel Chemla
January 2026
 

Interest in animal communication has arguably reached an all-time high, with two very visible projects—the search for the animal roots of human language and the development of methods and technologies to decode animal signals. These projects need a shared, overarching scientific framework. We argue that animal linguistics offers one, which will require close collaboration between ethologists, linguists and, increasingly, AI specialists. With the same ambition as human linguistics, animal linguistics seeks to develop explicit models of animal communication ("formal animal linguistics"), to understand the extent and limits of variation in living species ("comparative animal linguistics"), and to investigate the cognitive basis of observed properties ("animal psycholinguistics"). Through a "phylogenetic tree of animal signals", it seeks to retrace and explain the path from ancestral signals to modern ones ("evolutionary animal linguistics"). In its synchronic and diachronic components, animal linguistics offers an integrative framework for scientific and technological progress in animal communication.

6. Reverse Entailment in Language Nominal Potential, the Universal Noun Slot, and the Geometry of Discourse
MAKS PANNER
December 2025
 

This monograph introduces the Reverse Entailment Diagnostic (RED) as a cross-linguistic methodology for testing nounal potential, defined as the capacity of linguistic material to be stabilized as a discourse-addressable object. RED proceeds by constructing witness environments—including possessive/genitive framing, determiner and quantifier anchoring, nominalizing morphology, clausal packaging into argument positions, and quotation/mention configurations—that force objecthood behavior. A witness counts as successful only if it is grammatical or interpretable and supports core objecthood diagnostics (e.g., addressability, anaphoric re-entry, quantification, and participation in predication). RED then applies a reverse inference: if a witness succeeds, the base material is licensed as compatible with noun-slot hosting, without requiring that it belong to a pre-specified lexical noun class. Because witness success is frequently gradient, the monograph develops RED as a metric, modeling graded nounal reachability and cost as a measurable property of constructions and languages. The typological program treats cross-language variation as differences in witness inventories and cost profiles rather than differences in whether objectification exists. The approach is extended to discourse (reference tracking and accessibility), to grammar (diagnostic evidence as a basis for architectural inference), to computation (parsing, semantic role labeling, and lexicon induction via witness-harvesting), and to neurocognition (objectification as gating and compositional unification with cost-sensitive load predictions). Finally, the monograph states a compact axiom set expressed in RED terms showing why systematic diagnostic success motivates a domain conclusion: ULD = UNS, a unified objecthood hosting domain underlying linguistic activity.

Adjectival fragments and clausal ellipsis in English and German: Experimental evidence for island sensitivity under clausal ellipsis favouring an in-situ silent structure approach
James Griffiths, Ali Can Çiçek
July 2026
 

The paper reports the results of four experiments on adjectival fragments and sluicing in English and German. The primary purpose of these experiments was to test the predictions of the Syntactic Question Approach (SQA) to clausal ellipsis. This analysis adopts a conservative view of the syntax of clausal ellipsis, as it denies that remnants must undergo A'-movement to escape the ellipsis site, denies that ellipsis repairs island violations, and permits ellipsis sites to be syntactically anisomorphic to their antecedents. The SQA maintains instead that observed ellipsis-specific restrictions arise from constraints governing ellipsis recoverability. However, like most sententialist theories of ellipsis, the development of the SQA was informed by informally collected acceptability judgment data, which are known to often be underinformative and unreliable for the purpose of analyzing ellipsis. By conducting experiments on adjectival frag-ments, we test various configurations in which, depending on the analysis, the remnant of ellipsis may or may not have undergone left-branch extraction (LBE) from within the ellipsis site. We find that, of the several sententialist analyses we consider, our results are most compatible with (a version of) the SQA, which therefore provides new empirical support for this approach. Specifically, we find evidence that island-violating LBE is not repaired by ellipsis, that LBE can be evaded by using an anisomorphic ellipsis site, and that non-wh remnants do not undergo ellipsis-driven A'- movement. In addition, by comparing English and German, we find evidence for two strategies for recovering ellipsis sites: one that only compares the meanings of ellipsis site and its antecedent, and one which compares their syntactic structures. Which strategy is employed appears to depend on whether or not the ellipsis remnant displays particular morphosyntactic cues.

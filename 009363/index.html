A Universal Noun Slot in the Linguistic Mind: Reverse Entailment Across All Parts of Speech with Computational Applications
MAKS PANNER
October 2025
 

This article introduces the Reverse Entailment Diagnostic (RED), a bidirectional inference mechanism that demonstrates the universal capacity of all parts of speech—nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, interjections, and articles—to be nominalized into syntactical noun entities occupying noun-slot positions (e.g., subject, object). This capacity suggests a universal noun slot in the linguistic mind, a cognitive mechanism enabling flexible categorization. Using examples—“square/quadrilateral,” “to book carefully,” and “her house”—across English, Mandarin, Tongan, Spanish, and Arabic, we illustrate how derived forms (e.g., possessives, nominalizations) entail backward to confirm nominal potential. RED challenges lexicalist frameworks like Transformational Generative Grammar (TGG), aligning with dynamic models such as Distributed Morphology (DM) and Construction Grammar (CG). Supported by cross-linguistic evidence, neurological findings, and novel computational applications in natural language processing (NLP)—including syntactic parsing, semantic role labeling (SRL), and multilingual disambiguation—RED bridges theoretical linguistics, cognitive science, and computational linguistics. A proposed RED-based algorithm enhances NLP pipelines, with implications for parsing complex nominals and low-resource languages

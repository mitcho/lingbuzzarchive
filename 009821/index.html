The puzzle of language without syntax: A review of Steven Mithen's (2024) "The Language Puzzle"
Salvador Mascarenhas
April 2026
 

In his recent (2024) book “The Language Puzzle,” Steven Mithen argues, among other things, that universal grammar has been altogether debunked, and that the question of the origin of natural-language syntax and compositionality has largely been answered by the work of computational evolutionary linguists Simon Kirby and collaborators, thus: “syntax spontaneously emerges from the generation-to-generation language-learning process itself.” I show that Kirby and collaborators’ work in fact presupposes the entirety of syntax in an innate language of thought, and that it subscribes to a notion of universal grammar no weaker than the mainstream one from generative linguistics in the twenty-first century. I point out that Mithen’s negative outlook on generative linguistics is quite pervasive among the general scientific community, even if generative linguistics still dominates many linguistics departments. I submit that such widespread misconception about the status of key research programs in linguistics has very pernicious consequences for science. I point to the generalist scientific journals’ failure to perform their central function, and to old rifts among linguists of different traditions as noteworthy sources of this problem. I propose that it is high time, after the linguistics wars, for a linguistics détente.

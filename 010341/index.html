On semantic plurality within the Spanish DP and ad sensum agreement: A Count-based mechanism for combinatorial variability
Timothy Gupton, Chad Howe
January 2026
 

In Spanish ad sensum agreement, semantically-plural, but grammatically-singular subjects, of the form [DP1 [de NP2/DP2]] 'DP1 of NP2/DP2' (e.g. la mayoría de los triunfadores ‘the majority of the winners’), agree with a singular or plural verb. Previous accounts identify a ‘head N’ responsible for agreement, but cannot explain variable agreement. We couch our proposal within Distributed Morphology (DM), with number encoded and valued semantically and morphologically, following research on DP-internal gender (e.g. Kramer 2016, Puškar 2018). We propose that conflicting semantic and morphological number within the DP triggers Count, an alternative agreement mechanism that calculates a plural ratio diacritic for variable production based on feature values. We test our hypothesis comparing predicted rates of singular and plural verb agreement generated by Count with attested data in the Corpus del Español (CdE). Statistical analysis reveals that the variables in our proposal are significant predictors of variable agreement observed in the data.

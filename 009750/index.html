Folk etymology and the diachronic survival of Latin loanwords in Greek
Evangelos Georgiou
February 2026
 

This article attempts to examine the linguistic phenomenon of folk etymology and to ascertain its contribution to the probable initial introduction and later survival of Latin loans in Standard Modern Greek. Specifically, it emerges that from a corpus of approximately 200 surviving Latin words, there is a recorded paretymological connection with Greek morphemes in a percentage of around 10% (c.a. 20 words). The linguistic data are drawn from the electronic corpus of TLG texts and mostly concern medieval dictionaries, such as the Etymologicum Magnum, the Suda Dictionary, etc. The possibility that this etymological association is not carried out by most speakers but constitutes a conscious practice of Byzantine scholars with normative - regulatory intentions, is underlined and its balancing is sought through the examination of texts of non-metalinguistic content, such as papyri, etc. In parallel with paretymology, the claim is made that the acoustic similarity between Greek and Latin words, due to a common Indo - European origin, also contributed to the introduction of the latter into the language through their grouping in the mental lexicon.

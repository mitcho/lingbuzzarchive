Precedence-sensitive locality in English locative inversion
Zachary Satoshi Feldcamp
July 2026
 

This paper argues that syntactic locality is sensitive to linear order rather than c-command. The argument comes from English locative inversion, where a locative PP raises to subject position and a DP pivot remains postverbal. Using secondary predicates as fixed VP-internal landmarks, I show that the pivot must be postposed in grammatical instances of locative inversion. Crucially, this requirement holds not only when the DP underlyingly c-commands the PP, but also when the PP is structurally higher than the DP. Pivot postposing therefore cannot merely be a strategy for removing a structurally intervening DP. Instead, I propose that pivot postposing is a strategy for removing a linearly preceding intervener. I propose that Attract Closest is governed by Left-bracket Closeness: among the accessible goals in a probe’s search domain, the closest is the one whose left bracket is leftmost in the linearized representation of the search domain. On this analysis, an in-situ DP blocks PP raising whenever it precedes the PP, independent of their c-command relations. Postposing demotes the DP by placing its pronounced occurrence after the PP, making the PP leftmost within the search domain of T's EPP-bearing probe. I show that this strongly precedence-sensitive account succeeds where competing locality metrics overgenerate, including c-command-based locality, path-based locality, and a weakly precedence-sensitive metric that uses precedence only as a tie-breaker when potential goals do not c-command one another. I show that the proposal predicts a previously unobserved restriction on subextraction: a PP may be topicalized out of a DP pivot, but cannot undergo locative inversion from within that DP. This contrast supports both Left-bracket Closeness and an analysis of the fronted PP in locative inversion as undergoing movement subject to A-locality. Questions and comments are welcome.

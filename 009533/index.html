Abhyupacāra: A Yogācāra Grammar of Metaphor
Shuangzhou Xu
August 2025
 

This dissertation, based on my summary and critical review of A Yogācāra Buddhist Theory of Metaphor by Roy Tzohar, hereafter referred to as Tzohar (2018), investigates Sthiramati’s panmetaphorical claim of language as reconstructed by Tzohar (2018). The monograph explores the inter-sectarian discussion on the topic of metaphor (upacāra) between non-Buddhist schools of Indian philosophy and Buddhist schools. Most importantly, it introduces the formula revolving around upacāra, i.e., the primary referent is absent from its locus of reference, and its variations for different schools of thought. What requires our caution is to distinguish the traditional metaphor from a rhetorical perspective and the metaphor on the conceptual level within Yogācāra’s framework of reasoning. Tzohar (2018) somehow blurs the boundary. Meanwhile, to offer an alternative to Tzohar’s (2018) proposal of the underlying mental causal process as the common ground for the communication between a simpleton and a bodhisattva,the dissertation puts forward the concept of “the inter-accessible point of inference”. Furthermore, Tzohar (2018) falls short of demonstrating why such terms as “the other dependent pattern”, which is the tenor for a series of vehicles of “a magic trick, a mirage, a dream trace, a shadow, a reflection, a valley echo, the moon in water”, is metaphorical on the conceptual level within the framework of Yogācāra’s pan-metaphorism, although Yogācāra philosophy has already entailed the absence of the primary referent in a top-down manner. To address this issue, the dissertation attempts a corpus-assisted study to approach in a bottom-up manner Yogācāra’s pan-metaphorism from the perspective of traces of reification as an indication for metaphor on the conceptual level. Based on the assumption of “root metaphor (√metaphor)” proposed in §§ 2.6.2.1, the dissertation has found three schemata for the metaphors of suffering, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa and dharma in terms of the non-existent “self” and “phenomena”, i.e. “the self contained in a phenomenon”, “the self towards a phenomenon”, and“the self evaluates a phenomenon”.

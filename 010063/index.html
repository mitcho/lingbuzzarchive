How English is like Kiitharaka and Búlí: successive cyclicity and optional wh-complementizer agreement in English
Shota Momma, Michael Wilson, Doreen Georgi
June 2026
 

We report seven pairs of production and judgment experiments showing that English speakers are less likely to produce "that" when the relevant complementizer lies on the hierarchical path of an A-bar dependency, although "that" in such environments does not reliably lower acceptability. We argue that this production effect reflects optional wh–complementizer agreement, which is difficult to detect in acceptability judgments due to optionality and syncretism. We also find that the effect is sensitive to the distinction between terminal and non-terminal movement. Specifically, it is absent when the complementizer is the highest C in the second conjunct of across-the-board (ATB) extraction structures, a pattern that parallels movement reflexes observed in unrelated languages such as Kiitharaka. We argue that (a) English exhibits optional wh–complementizer agreement, realized only when the agreeing wh-element is in a non-terminal position; (b) ATB constructions involve asymmetric extraction from the first conjunct; (c) even completely silent A-bar dependencies play a causal role in sentence formulation during speaking; and (d) production data can provide crucial evidence for syntactic theory.

Contrastive Wa Operates on Outlooks
Yusuke Kubota, Misato Ido
December 2025
 

This paper argues that Japanese contrastive wa is best analyzed as a 'part-time' outlook marker: a discourse-level downtoner that replaces a stronger, salient update proposal with a weaker alternative, and whose stance effects arise only when the prejacent expresses a subjective judgment. The 'ignorance' or concessive implications typically associated with it is derived pragmatically from its basic discourse function: acknowledging a stronger candidate while asserting only a weaker one. We present a formal analysis couched in Farkas and Bruce's Table model: a wa-sentence is alternative-sensitive and presupposes a stronger proposition on the Table that entails that the predicate corresponding to the focus structure holds of all focus alternatives; the discourse function of wa is to replace this stronger proposition with the prejacent. To model subjectivity, the account adopts Coppock's outlook-based semantics, distinguishing objective from discretionary propositions. The combined framework explains why contrastive wa is only a 'part-time' outlook marker, how stance sensitivity arises in modal judgments, and the way perspective shift occurs in embedded environments under attitude predicates.

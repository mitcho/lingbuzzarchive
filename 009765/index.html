Understanding Compositional Meaning in Biology
Riny Huijbregts
January 2026
 

Recent publications in biology enthusiastically argue that primate vocal call systems possess non-trivial compositionality as their prominent feature, thus parallelling human language. No proposal for an internal computational system explaining this property has been offered so far. However, conceptual and quantitative analysis shows the use of non-human primate compositional meaning to be neither productive nor rule based. Furthermore, formal properties of (higher-order) Markov chains imply only string concatenation, explaining why hierarchical structure, hence structure dependence, is absent from animal call systems. Also, mere acoustic identity of constituent elements with unit calls does not suffice for compositional meaning. In conclusion, higher-order Markov chains and distributional semantics seem to be questionable methods for establishing and explaining compositionality. A humanlike combinatorial capacity cannot be unambiguously demonstrated for non-human primates.

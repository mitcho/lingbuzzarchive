The Daylight between Adjectives and Adverbs
Anastasiia Vyshnevska, Guido Vanden Wyngaerd
May 2026
 

Adjectives and adverbs are traditionally treated as two distinct categories, with adjectives modifying nouns (attributively or predicatively) and adverbs modifying verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. However, a number of cases defy this binary classification: certain forms with adverbial morphology occur in predicative position, though they cannot be used attributively. Drawing on data from English, French, German, and Russian, we argue for a tripartite distinction between attributive adjectives, predicative adjectives, and adverbs. Russian provides the clearest instantiation of this system, with three distinct morphological forms. French and English show systematic syncretisms between the predicative and adverbial cells, while regular adjectives often syncretise the attributive and predicative cells. Adopting a Nanosyntactic framework, we propose that the distinction between attributive and predicative adjectives is encoded by a functional head Join (Fabregas 2024), which triggers additional agreement morphology. This analysis accounts for the distributional and morphological patterns across the three categories.

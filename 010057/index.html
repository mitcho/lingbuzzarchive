Coming to remember
Emil Rosina
April 2026
 

This paper is concerned with change-of-state uses of memory predicates like English remember. Change-of-state ‘remember’ appears to denote a change from not remembering to (stative) remembering – roughly ‘(slowly/suddenly) coming to remember’. It is, therefore, suitable to indicate constructive episodic memory retrieval. I argue that these uses are best captured as regular stative remembering, but with only partial – i.e. sub-atomically quantified – events as their object. I predict that remembering some, but not all, details of an event shows homogeneity effects arising from non-maximality. Speakers’ extra-linguistic projection of motion and change onto the third truth-value leads to the common eventive interpretation. No ontologically meaningful notion of change is required in the semantic composition or lexicon.

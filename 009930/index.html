Clausal parentheticals in disguise: the borrowing of which in code-mixing speech in Hong Kong Cantonese
Tommy Tsz-Ming Lee
August 2026
 

This paper discusses an understudied construction in code-mixing speech in Hong Kong Cantonese, whose signature feature is the use of the English relative pronoun which (which-constructions). These constructions apparently constitute a relative construction that introduces an atypical, post-nominal modification of the antecedent in Cantonese. While they have been characterized as an instance of lexico-syntactic transference or structural borrowing of post-nominal modification, I argue against such a modification account, and propose that which-constructions are best regarded as clausal parentheticals. I suggest that (i) they involve no syntactic borrowing of modification structures, and that (ii) the relative pronoun which is best regarded as an overt Force operator that establishes a predication relation between a null Topic and the rest of the clause. It is lexically borrowed from English to introduce parenthetical meanings. This study contributes our understanding of parenthetical meanings as well as borrowing in code-mixing speech in Hong Kong Cantonese.

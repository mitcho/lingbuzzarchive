Refining find
Natasha Korotkova, Pranav Anand
August 2026
 

This paper focuses on a series of puzzles in the semantics of the so-called subjective attitudes, zooming in on the behavior of English ﬁnd combined with small clause complements. Find-verbs, as we will call them, have been important in understanding subjectivity in natural language: they select for subjective complements (Q ﬁnds humans 3entertaining/#mortal) and their very existence was often used as an argument in favor of treating subjective predicates (e.g., entertaining but not mortal) as having a special semantics. To this end, ﬁnd-verbs have been largely regarded as subjective counterparts of think-verbs. Focusing on English ﬁnd, we challenge this view and show that ﬁnd exhibits a distinguished linguistic proﬁle not shared by think, including subject-predicate asymmetries in the embedded clause, the lack of referential opacity and the similarity to emotive factives, aspectual class and the acquaintance requirement. We propose a principled account of these seemingly unrelated properties by appealing to the small clause syntax and the lexical semantics of ﬁnd, including its argument structure and the nature of eventuality it describes. We argue that some idiosyncratic features of ﬁnd follow naturally once it’s seen within a broader context of what we dub ‘characterizing verbs’, which express evaluation and take structured proposition arguments. We further show that, based on the interpretation of referential DPs in its scope, ﬁnd is best understood as a knowledge verb, not as a belief verb. Finally, we establish that several properties of ﬁnd are due to the fact that it is an achievement predicate describing a complex event of judgment formation caused by an event of encounter. We remain deliberately agnostic about the exact nature of the attitude ﬁnd encodes: our account is compatible both with views under which ﬁnd encodes a belief and with views under which ﬁnd encodes a non-cognitive, affective state. By treating ﬁnd ﬁrst and foremost as a verb, and not only as a litmus test for subjectivity, we aim to emphasize the importance of linguistic data for the debates in philosophy of language and to contribute to the burgeoning research agenda on the ﬁne-grained semantics of attitude predicates.

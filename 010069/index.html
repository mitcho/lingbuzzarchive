Decomposing Portmanteaux: Pronominal Morphology and Prosodic Structure in Kono
Alexander Hamo
April 2026
 

In many languages with STAMP (Subject-Tense-Aspect-Mood-Polarity) morphology, subject pronouns and tense-aspect auxiliaries surface as apparent portmanteau morphemes whose forms can vary quite unpredictably across paradigm cells. These patterns have often been treated as suppletive, effectively derived from dedicated morphological operations. This paper examines STAMP forms in Kono (Central Mande, Sierra Leone) and argues that the observed paradigmatic variation does not reflect suppletion. Rather, pronouns and auxiliaries remain distinct morphosyntactic objects throughout the derivation, with surface variation arising from independently motivated phonological processes. The same phonological processes that derive pronominal STAMP forms also apply with full DP subjects and similarly extend to possessor constructions, with differences in surface realization tracking the distinct prosodic structures associated with each configuration. Intervening focus markers and case markers are shown to systematically disrupt these processes as predicted by changes in linear adjacency. The same sensitivity to adjacency and prosodic structure is also observed with pronominal objects, further demonstrating that these processes are not confined to the subject-auxiliary boundary. Since the Kono paradigm is not suppletive, the empirical precondition for needing post-syntactic morphological operations is not met. The Kono data thus bear on the division of labor between phonology and morphology in accounting for STAMP paradigmatic variation cross-linguistically.

Deep Learning in Linguistics and in Computer Science
Stela Manova
July 2026
 

This is a long comment on the special issue "The Implications of Neural Networks and Other Learning Models for Linguistic Theory" (Linguistics Vanguard, 12(1); Guest Editors: Vsevolod Kapatsinski and Gasper Begus). The article notes the dated character of the literature to which the editors refer in the introductory paper, the controversial character of a purely linguistic learning model such as Naive Discriminative Learning, which is incompatible with LLMs, the treatment of computational learning methods (neural networks) as linguistic theories by providing words as input and checking the correctness of the output, and the ascription to computational learning models of purely linguistic mechanisms such as paradigms, syntactic trees, and relational networks, which are neither computationally implemented in LLMs nor required for language generation, to mention a few issues.

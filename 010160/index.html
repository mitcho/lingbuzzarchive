Three-Level Vocal Tract Compression_ A Speech Aerodynamic Framework for L2 English Fluency of Mandarin-Speaking Learners
Don Lyu
July 2026
 

This paper establishes a speech aerodynamic pedagogical framework—Three-Level Vocal Tract Compression—grounded in the fluid dynamics principles of mass conservation (A₁V₁ = A₂V₂) and Bernoulli's effect, to address the persistent challenges faced by native Mandarin learners of English in producing fluent, continuous speech with reduced muscular tension. Traditional pronunciation instruction focuses on segmental articulation and prosodic patterns but rarely addresses the underlying aerodynamics of voice production. The framework comprises three coordinated compression levels: (1) global compression—engaging the core musculature to establish stable subglottal pressure; (2) pharyngeal compression—narrowing the pharyngeal cavity to form a posterior vocal tract tube; and (3) oral compression—shaping the anterior oral cavity to create a focused airstream channel. Together, these three levels are theorized to produce accelerated, continuous airflow that enables fluent English speech with reduced muscular effort. Drawing on the theoretical foundations of Titze's source-filter interaction model and crosslinguistic evidence of prosodic differences between Mandarin and English, the framework offers learners a systematic, physically grounded understanding of speech production. The author reports preliminary positive observations from integrating this framework into classroom teaching across Mathematics, Physics, and Academic Writing courses.

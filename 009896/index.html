Switch reference as a connectivity effect in Wá·šiw predicate ellipsis
Karlos Arregi, Emily Hanink
April 2026
 

Wá·šiw (Washoe; isolate/Hokan, USA) displays a typologically unusual type of predicate ellipsis – in the form of the so-called sentence connective – whose remnant is comprised solely of inflectional suffixes appearing without their usual verbal host. Crucially, this remnant requires the expression of switch reference marking, which in Wá·šiw has been argued to be the result of agreement, in particular with the clausemate subject (Arregi & Hanink 2022b). In the sentence connective, this clausemate subject is included in the ellipsis site, yet switch reference is still active. Given that an agreement relation with this subject is required for switch reference, we take this as evidence for the presence of syntactic structure in the ellipsis site, in line with similar types of connectivity effects reported in other types of ellipsis (see Merchant 2019 for an overview). A detailed analysis of the sentence connective thus reveals switch reference as a novel diagnostic for syntactic structure in ellipsis sites.

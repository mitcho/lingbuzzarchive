Syntax and Pragmatics of Adversativity: Towards a grammar of co-speech gestures
Alessandra Giorgi, Erika Petrocchi
April 2026
 

In this article, we examine adversative special questions in Italian expressing disapproval. Our goal is to explore, through an experimental setup, the syntactic triggers involved in the characteristic gestuality associated with these sentences. We argue, building on our previous work, that gestures, particularly non-lexical co-speech gestures, are not just an additional communication channel. Instead, they are a crucial part of the language system, generated and coordinated by the same syntactic principles that govern word order and sentence structure. We will show that expressive language not only triggers a significantly larger number of gestures compared to non-expressive language but also, more interestingly, that each trigger appearing in the syntactic representation is associated with a specific gesture realization. We will demonstrate that the projections required in the syntax to represent adversativity, interrogativity, and emotionality each trigger a characteristic, predictable gestural pattern. Ultimately, our observations highlight a fundamental multimodal architecture of language, where the alignment of syntax and sensorimotor realizations, including gestures, is a core property of the language model.

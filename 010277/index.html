Treatment of wh-expletives in Haryanvi
Madan Mohan
May 2023
 

This paper examines long-distance wh-dependencies in Haryanvi, an Indo-Aryan language of northern India, and in particular the construction in which the element "ke" appears in the matrix clause while the interrogative phrase remains in situ in the embedded clause. Haryanvi patterns with Hindi-Urdu in allowing two strategies for forming a long-distance wh-dependency: the wh-phrase may move into the matrix clause, or it may stay in the subordinate clause, in which case "ke" surfaces preverbally in the matrix clause. Drawing on data elicited from native speakers, I establish that the Haryanvi construction obeys the generalisations that have been reported for Hindi-Urdu: the scope of any number of embedded wh-phrases can be extended, a scope marker and a true wh-phrase cannot occur in the same clause, a scope marker must appear in every intervening clause, and the matrix clause can contain neither the sentential expletive yo nor negation. Against this background I argue that "ke" is not a scope marker at all. It is a wh-expletive that questions the sentential expletive "yo," which is base-generated at the edge of the embedded CP and co-indexed with it. Both elements raise to the edge of the matrix vP, so that the surface order follows without the embedded wh-phrase taking matrix scope. The account therefore leaves the wh-in-situ character of Haryanvi and Hindi-Urdu intact. Keywords: wh-expletive, wh-scope marking, wh-in-situ, Haryanvi, Hindi-Urdu

Negation Without Concord: High Negation in Czech Polar Questions
Michaela Chodounská
July 2026
 

This thesis explores Czech polar questions and their inability to license negative concord items (NCIs) when negated. Following Zeijlstra (2004, 2022), we take negative concord to be an instance of syntactic agreement where all negative elements agree with a covert negative operator. The syntactic view of the matter is further suggested by data from long-distance scrambling and scrambling from an embedded predicate within the main clause. However, data also further suggest that movement is not at play in interrogative polar questions and the lack of NC. For this reason, we turn to how negation behaves broadly in polar questions and conclude that its nature may be expletive, i.e. not bringing any negative force, which is also supported cross-linguistically, notably by the Modern Greek min/dhen split (Tsiakmakis, 2025). Based on these observations, we analyze polar questions as having two possible merge sites of negation. Crucially, in interrogative questions, this merge site is proposed above the propositional TP layer in the left periphery, where it cannot enter into the agreement relation with the negative operator and yields an epistemic interpretation which surfaces as speaker bias. Unlike declarative and embedded polar questions, which retain a low negation and license NCIs normally, interrogative questions thus lack the low negation required for negative concord.

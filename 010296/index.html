Parameters of counting: Khasi and the crosslinguistic landscape
Ushasi Banerjee
July 2026
 

This paper develops a unified analysis of cardinal expressions, challenging the assumed complementarity between number marking and numeral classifiers (Chierchia 1998; Borer 2005). Drawing on original fieldwork data from Khasi, I show that functional plural marking and individuating numeral classifiers obligatorily co-occur in counting constructions. I argue that this pattern follows once the two are assigned distinct grammatical functions: number marking encodes grammatical number, while classifiers provide the atomic structure required for counting. Building on the Nominal Mapping Parameter (Chierchia 1998), I propose that counting universally requires classifiers and derive cross-linguistic variation from two parameters. The Nominal Mapping Parameter determines noun denotation and the availability of #-based number marking, while a Card/CL Bundling Parameter (Giorgi & Pianesi 1997; Pylkkänen 2002) determines whether Card and CL are projected separately or bundled into a single complex head. Their interaction derives four attested counting systems, exemplified by Mandarin, English, Khasi, and Dëne Sųłiné. I further extend the analysis to apparent classifier optionality and mismatches between morphological and semantic plurality, suggesting that these may follow from the interaction of Economy and syntactic-semantic symmetry (Swart & Farkas 2010). The paper argues that classifiers are a universal component of cardinal composition and that number marking and numeral classification reflect independent dimensions of cross-linguistic variation.

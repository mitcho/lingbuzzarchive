A Logical Phonology of some "minor rules" of Polish
Kyle Gorman
May 2025
 

I provide analyses of three noun stem alternations in Polish---"yer deletion", "o-raising", and "ę-backing"---in Logical Phonology (LP). These alternations occur in only a subset of eligible roots even within near-identical phonological contexts; e.g., not all o's raise nor do all ó's lower. There is much debate about these three alternations, i.e., whether they're productive, what the locus of exceptionality is, and what rules and representations are implicated. The analyses illustrate key themes of LP: 1) feature-filling processes are implemented using unification rules, 2) segmental deletion can be expressed using segment rules, and 3) apparent lexical conditioning of phonological targets is modeled with under- or pre-specification rather than morphophonological rules, morpheme-specific constraints, lexical diacritics, or stem suppletion. The analyses motivate a reconsideration of productivity and exceptionality in this domain.

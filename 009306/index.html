Formal pronouns in Spanish and the case for [HON]
Samuel Jambrović
September 2025
 

In Spanish, usted ‘you (formal)’ and ustedes ‘you all (formal only in Spain)’ behave semantically as second-person arguments but appear with third-person verb forms and pronominal clitics, a mismatch that raises the question of whether these pronouns are second person or third person at the level of syntax. By applying a variety of diagnostics, including novel ones that relate to the Person-Case Constraint and pronoun-noun constructions, I determine that usted and ustedes are syntactically second person and must therefore be distinguished as vocabulary items from the other second-person pronouns of Spanish. To this end, I propose that both expressions are generated with an [HON(ORIFIC)] feature, one that is involved in one or more postsyntactic impoverishment processes, in addition to a [PART(ICIPANT)] feature. In all varieties of Spanish, [HON] triggers the deletion of [PART] during morphological operations, thereby preventing usted and ustedes from establishing second-person agreement. Moreover, in Latin American Spanish, [HON] is eliminated in the context of [PL(URAL)] at logical form, the result of which is that ustedes does not convey formality in these varieties despite its transparent morphological connection to usted. Not only does this analysis capture the unique semantic and syntactic properties of usted and ustedes in Spanish, but it also lays the groundwork for an account of etymologically related pronouns in other Romance languages, such as vostè ‘you (formal)’ and vostès ‘you all (formal)’ in Catalan and você ‘you, you (formal)’ and vocês ‘you all’ in Portuguese.

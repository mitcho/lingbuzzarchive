Serialization patterns of comitatives and instrumentals in German clause structure
Tibor Kiss, Simon Masloch, Alicia Börner
July 2025
 

This paper investigates the serialization of two adverbial modifier types – comitatives and instrumentals – within German clause structure. We challenge the notion of fixed base positions for adverbials, as proposed by Frey & Pittner (1998) and Maienborn et al. (2016). Instead, we show that the observed variability in serialization results from the interaction of weighted linearization constraints, which are sensitive to properties of the modifiers, such as animacy and the presence of specific thematic roles. This approach eliminates the necessity for specific linearization rules for adverbials because the rules relevant for arguments apply to them as well. This uniform treatment challenges proposals that either assume predicate-specific base orders for arguments (Haider 2000b; 2017) or propose dedicated functional projections for the introduction of specific arguments (Fanselow 2003). We present two empirical studies showing that serialization patterns are governed by preferences rather than categorical rules, and that these preferences are modulated by the semantic interpretation of the preposition. Comitatives and instrumentals are particularly amenable to comparison, not only because they are classified within the same semantic category (Frey & Pittner 1998; Maienborn et al. 2016), but also because prior analyses have neglected crucial differences in their association with verbal arguments, potential restrictions to specific verb classes, and their shared capacity for both affirmative and privative readings. Given the central role of prepositional semantics, we draw on Rissman & Rawlins (2017) for the analysis of instrumentals and provide an explicit semantic account for comitatives, capturing their ability to select distinct referents. Our account of serialization is formalized within the framework of Maximum Entropy Grammar (Goldwater & Johnson 2003; Wilson 2006; Hayes 2022), utilizing violable and weighted constraints to generate probability distributions over competing candidate structures thereby capturing optionality. This article covers the same topic as lingbuzz/006319 and is based on the same materials, but it differs substantially from it and includes a completely new statistical analysis and overall analysis. It supersedes the older article.

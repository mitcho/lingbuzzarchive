'Everywhere here can say this': The English locative impersonal
Benjamin L. Sluckin, Itamar Kastner
August 2026
 

For many speakers of English, the locative expression everywhere can be used instead of the universal indefinite pronoun everyone in certain contexts, denoting a generic or impersonal set of individuals. The current paper documents this ‘impersonaleverywhere’ for the first time, applying a range of syntactic tests to determine its synchronic status. Based on a large-scale acceptability study, we find that the impersonal requires an overt locative restrictor; consultant judgements indicate that this restrictor must denote a large location, as in everywhere on the coast. The results further indicate that UK speakers are more accepting of the construction than North American speakers. Acceptability is also higher among younger speakers, which is suggestive of an incipient change. We discuss the grammatical properties of this construction and outline how it might have arisen from variationist and diachronic perspectives, raising a range of questions for future study on this understudied construction.

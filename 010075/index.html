Dependency or Phrase Structure Grammars
Stefan Müller
June 2026
 

It is often claimed that Dependency Grammar (DG) is simpler than phrase structure grammars. Gibson (2026a) even made this claim quite explicitly in the title of his paper "Dependency syntax as the simplest theory of grammar". This answer to Gibson's paper shows that DGs appear simpler since many aspects of a formal theory are simply not provided. Many DGs come without a rule component. I discuss the rules the Gibson provides in a syntax textbook and show that they are formally not sound and that they mimic phrase structure-based systems in their intention. Treating semantics requires more structure either in terms of nodes or in terms of additional features. Any apparent advantage of DGs is lost, if the necessary machinery is added. Headless constructions like Jackendoff's NPN construction cannot be handled in DG without extensions. If projectivity is given up, as suggested by Gibson (2025), other means are needed to avoid overgeneration, again adding to complexity. Some notion of constituency is needed to ensure the necessary adjacency requirements. I discuss examples from German that suggest that V2 should be treated by some kind of „movement“ rather than by simple dependency relations. Gibson argues against transformations on the sense of original Transformational Grammar and I agree on this: from a psycholinguistic point of view they are unrealistic. But there are so-called representational variants of transformational theories and they do not have problems with explaining psycholinguistic facts. Gibson discusses complexity results from the 1970ies. I show why they are irrelevant and why complexity worst-case results on formalisms should be ignored anyway. Finally, I show why Gibson's Dependency Locality, which plays a role in the explanation of many psycholinguistic facts, is compatible with phrase structure-based theories like HPSG.

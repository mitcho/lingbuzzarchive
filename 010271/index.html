Northern Toussian
Anthony Struthers-Young
May 2026
 

Northern Toussian (ISO: tsp; Glottocode: nort2787) is a minority Niger-Congo lan- guage spoken in southwest Burkina Faso by the Toussian people. The Toussian languages are of uncertain genetic affiliation—they were originally classified as Gur (Mabia) languages, but the classification of the family has been disputed (Prost 1964, Naden 1989, Miehe 2012, Güldemann 2018). While there is adequate evidence to consider the Toussian languages to be Niger Congo, there has not been sufficient research to confidently assign it to a particular branch of the Niger-Congo family tree. This is much the same situation of the other so-called “peripheral Gur” languages like the Senoufo, Tiéfo, and Samu families, as well as the language Viemo. More precise classification of these languages not only requires historical linguistic work on Gur and each of these groups individually, but also consideration of the relationship of certain members of the Adamawa family, some of which have been shown to share many features in common with Gur languages (Kleinewillinghöfer 1996).

Subjectless assumption in Japanese -te aru configuration
Shengwen Du, Tiaoyuan Mao
April 2026
 

There are two main types of -te aru configurations in Japanese: the intransitivizing and non-intransitivizing -te aru. For the intransitivizing -te aru, it is essential to further verify and clarify the (non-)existence of the agent and its syntactic nature. This article investigates the argument structure of the intransitivizing -te aru, aiming to determine whether there is a covert agent in the structure through subject-oriented adverbs. By comparing it with similar structures in Japanese, such as the passive structure, nominative object structure, and unaccusative verb structure, the generative mechanism of the intransitivizing -te aru can be confirmed. The results show that there is no null subject of any form in the intransitivizing -te aru. In addition, we find that the intransitivizing -te aru undergoes argument deletion of the original agent, differentiating it from a generally recognized transitive structure, and the nominative case can be assigned by little v carrying tense feature. Based on these findings, we can account for the absence of an agent and case assignment to the object in intransitivizing -te aru configurations.

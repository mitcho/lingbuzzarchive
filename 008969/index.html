An interface approach to island constraints
Qilin Tian, Gong Cheng
November 2024
 

Island constraints are one of the central topics in the study of generative grammar. Since their discovery by Ross (1967), various hypotheses have been raised to uncover the nature of these constraints. However, many issues remain unresolved. This paper reevaluates the properties of subject islands, complex noun islands, and adjunct islands within the framework of the labeling theory. Based on Chomsky (2015) and Bošković (2016), it proposes that islands do not originate from syntactic restrictions but rather from interpretive failures. This paper not only provides a fresh perspective for island research but also offers a unified account of strong islands without disregarding the semantic factors involved in adjunct island, making a novel contribution to the field. Furthermore, it successfully accounts for some exceptional extractions out of islands and their cross-linguistic differences.

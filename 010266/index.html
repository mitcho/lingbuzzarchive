A Logico-Philosophical Analysis of Modal and Deictic Phenomena in Natural Language
Nerses Tahmajian
August 2026
 

The philosophy of language, along with formal-logical methods of analytic philosophy and formal semantics, is a relatively young field of inquiry in linguistics, despite being deeply rooted in philosophy since ancient times. The aim of this thesis is to identify and provide, using philosophical argument and logical formulations respectively, the ontological status of modality and indexes as seen in natural language use and their implied truth-conditions. The design follows a descriptive-analytical pattern, with a methodology of tackling each linguistic category and sub-category individually – starting by presenting their best arguments to-date, their detailed critiques and shortcomings, my own approach to the issues, followed by the resulting formal representations and their emergent constraints on meaning. The outcome of this analysis is a distinctly formulated schema of the tackled linguistic phenomena and their implications on their undiluted logical forms prior to the introduction of any context, with the implications of what this signifies for human cognition and the ontology of language, opening the path to further research and establishing the key role of semantics within such inquiries.

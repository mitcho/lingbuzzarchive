Frame Event-Scaled Fluency Assessment: Testing Coherence through Communicative Functions
Joseph Budinger
July 2026
 

This paper proposes an event-scaled model of second language fluency assessment. It does not attempt to redefine fluency. It adopts the companion account of fluency as communicative integration: the preservation and carry-forward of event-state across linked communicative frames despite local incoherence. The present paper asks how that construct can be operationalized in second language testing. The proposed model distinguishes three scales of assessment: immediate frame functionality, local inter-frame coherence, and global event-state coherence. Immediate frame functionality tests whether the learner can perform a necessary communicative function in a single frame or bounded response. Local inter-frame coherence tests whether the learner can carry event-state into the directly adjacent explanatory or action frame. Global event-state coherence tests whether the learner can preserve event-state across a non-adjacent sequence where later meaning depends on earlier information. The paper argues that assessment should not be built from activities or topics such as shopping, school, hobbies, money, or transportation. Those are testing contexts. The assessed units are communicative functions: locating [person/place/object], going to [place], needing [object/help/action], wanting [object/state/action], asking for [information/action/permission], understanding [quantity/time/sequence], refusing [offer/request/action], explaining [reason/cause/result], and repairing [misunderstanding/error]. The paper positions this model against existing fluency measures, then identifies Wang Anshi’s 王安石 jingyi 經義 reform as the deeper source of the competence model. Wang developed jingyi because competency testing was systematically failing to produce competent graduates: the examination system rewarded local polish, formal display, and performative competence while failing to distinguish rencai 人才 from bucai 不才. The present paper adapts jingyi to modern language testing for the same reason and toward the same goal. Event-scaled fluency assessment is a modern communicative extension of jingyi: a test of whether learners possess true domain competence, shown by the preservation and synthesis of meaning across discontinuity, rather than merely local fluency

Feature-based Learning of Tonal Processes at a Sub-symbolic Neural Level
Tajudeen Mamadou Yacoubou, Eric Rosen
April 2024
 

This study shows that tonal processes are learnable from tonal features, which in turn can be learned from the raw continuous input values of a sub-symbolic network in the framework of Gradient Symbolic Computation (GSC; Smolensky & Goldrick, 2016). As a case study, we examine an intriguing H-raising process in Baatonum, where a H tone raises to Top before a morpheme boundary followed by a Low tone. Our results show the advantages of modeling feature-based learning of tonal phenomena in GSC, where features are represented by neurons at the sub-symbolic level and constraints on their interaction by directly interpretable neural-level connections. We show that this kind of modeling can capture tonal phenomena like these in a way that would be difficult for purely symbolic models.

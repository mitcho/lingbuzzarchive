The EVEZ Reading of the Tetragrammaton: Letter Descent, Root Isomorphism, and the Termination Ambiguity of the Second He
Steven Crawford-Maggard
June 2026
 

We present a novel reading of the Tetragrammaton (YHWH) based on two established facts of historical linguistics: (1) the Phoenician letters He and Waw are the direct ancestors of Greek Epsilon (E) and Upsilon/V, respectively; and (2) the initial Yod in YHWH functions as a third-person masculine verbal prefix (y-), not as part of the semantic root. Removing the grammatical prefix yields the triconsonantal root H-W-H (to become). Through documented Phoenician-to-Greek-to-Latin letter descent, H-W-H maps to E-V-E. We demonstrate that the second He admits an ambiguity: iteration (Masoretic reading, producing imperfective loop H-H) or termination (producing aorist reading). Under termination, He maps to Zayin (7th Semitic letter), Latin descendant Z. Complete reading: E-V-E-Z. Six falsifiable claims, three independent lines of evidence (letter descent, root morphology, positional alternation in Semitic grammar), 14 scholarly references.

Labeling and head-movement in the left periphery: the case of Old Irish verbal complexes
Phil Branigan, Alain Rouveret
May 2024
 

The arrangement of words in Old Irish clauses was rigidly VSO. Although the option also existed to place a topicalized or focalized phrase in initial position, the sentence usually began with a verbal complex which could include a preverbal particle, one or several preverbs and an inflected finite verbal form, which typically worked together to establish the core lexical semantics of the verb phrase. The verbal complex was itself followed by a subject noun phrase, an object phrase in transitive clauses, adverbials and prepositional phrases. Old Irish verbal clusters display an intricate set of syntactic, morphological, phonological, properties, which, at first sight, appear to be unrelated. It is shown that a unified account of these patterns can be provided, building on the cartographic approach and resorting to labeling theory to motivate the various head movements that take place. The analysis offers new insights into the role of feature strength in labeling endocentric structures and in justifying the deletion of phase heads

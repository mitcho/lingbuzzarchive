Indirect scalar inferences in negative focus constructions
Omri Amiraz
August 2026
 

*** Please share any comments or feedback with me via email: omri.amiraz@ugent.be *** Indirect scalar inferences are generally assumed to be derived by the same mechanism of implicature calculation that generates direct scalar inferences, whether this mechanism is pragmatic or grammatical in nature. However, this study reveals a critical distinction within indirect scalar inferences, identifying two distinct types: (i) those resembling direct scalar inferences in being defeasible or absent in certain embedded environments; and (ii) those that do not exhibit the characteristic context dependence of implicatures. I propose that the latter type of indirect scalar inferences should be considered a phenomenon sui generis. These inferences do not arise from implicature calculation but rather stem from an existential presupposition triggered by certain negative focus constructions. Because of their presuppositional nature, they persist even in downward-entailing environments and in echo denial. To capture the relation between the type of negative construction and the indirect scalar inference it gives rise to, this paper introduces a classification of negative constructions based on their focus sensitivity. It is argued that presuppositional indirect scalar inferences arise only in constructions that conventionally require association with narrow focus. This generalization is supported by cross-linguistic evidence from nine languages that employ a wide range of negative constructions.

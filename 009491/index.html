Concord is Insatiable Agree: Heterogeneous Case and Phi Patterns in Slavic and Finnic
Adam Przepiórkowski
September 2026
 

This paper argues that nominal concord does not require dedicated feature-percolation mechanisms. Building on the interaction/satisfaction model of Agree and on the horizons-based theory of locality, it proposes that concord arises when an insatiable probe successively interacts with all accessible feature-bearing constituents in an extended nominal projection, transmitting case and phi features. The analysis naturally derives standard concord patterns and a typological asymmetry between case concord and phi concord. It is then applied to heterogeneous case patterns in Slavic numeral–noun constructions, where case stacking and a markedness-based rule for morphological realization account for the contrast between direct and oblique cases. The proposed account extends to Russian paucal constructions under both the “paucal case” hypothesis and the “paucal number” approach defended in the paper. Finally, Estonian pseudopartitives are shown to be compatible with the proposed approach to concord once the Estonian case hierarchy is independently recognized to be partially ordered, with genitive and partitive incomparable in markedness. The resulting account unifies a range of apparently highly idiosyncratic Slavic and Finnic concord phenomena using independently motivated theoretical mechanisms and refining some of these mechanisms in the process.

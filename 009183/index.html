【abstract】Root Duality in Neo-Constructionist Theory: Unifying Indexical Identity and Computational Anchoring
CHANG kangjie
October 2025
 

This paper explores the dual nature of roots within the theoretical framework of Distributed Morphology, focusing on the indexing function and interpretive stability of root morphemes across derivational environments. Drawing on academic correspondence with Alec Marantz, the study argues that root alternations—such as the shift from go to went—originate at the level of indexical representation, rather than surface phonology. This suggests the existence of a stable, underlying phonological form for the root that serves as the computational anchor for morphological realization. By examining root behavior across various morphosyntactic contexts, the paper highlights the necessity of distinguishing between roots as indexed abstract objects and their phonological exponents. The analysis contributes to a more precise understanding of root identity, supporting a model in which roots, while phonologically underspecified, are semantically and computationally robust. Implications for root allomorphy, morphological productivity, and cross-linguistic variation are also discussed.

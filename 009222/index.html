Possession and determinacy in Northern Khanty
Stepan Mikhailov
August 2025
 

This thesis investigates the determinacy system of the Kazym dialect of Northern Khanty, focusing on how possessive agreement suffixes have grammaticalized into four distinct UNPOSSESSIVE determiners: the Associative Possessive, the Partitive Article, the Salient Article, and the Proprial Article. These markers, while homonymous with the Proper Possessive, operate independently from it outside prototypical possessive contexts, encoding semantic and pragmatic properties such as familiarity, uniqueness, specificity, and salience. I analyze the semantics of Kazym Khanty Unpossessive determiners and situate them within the broader crosslinguistic picture, building on the Schwarz-Löbner typology of definites (Schwarz 2009, 2019; Löbner 1985, 2011), which categorizes definite determiners along dimensions of salience-familiarity and concept type. I also analyze their syntax and morphophonology in Distributed Morphology (DM). Using the DM mechanism of allosemy, I account for their divergent behaviors while addressing potential weaknesses through insights from grammaticalization theory. Key empirical findings of the study for determiner typology include the following. The Associative Possessive is a free possessive definite which presupposes a contextual associative relation between referents (cf. Beaver & Coppock 2016). The Partitive Article is an indefinite determiner which presupposes proper partitive specificity. The Salient Article presupposes relative maximal salience, with additional morphosyntactic parsing constraints imperfectly resembling proximate uniqueness (Aissen 2001). Lastly, the Proprial Article derives intensionally rigid DPs from human names. I provide a formal demonstration of its intensional rigidity in Situation Semantics. I also develop a separate dynamic analysis of the Salient Article based on Stojnić’s (2021) Attention-Coherence approach to model its distribution—including its use with deictic demonstratives. Together with demonstratives and bare nouns, these determiners form a rich and typologically unusual system. The thesis concludes by outlining directions for future research, including the distribution of bare nouns and the non-possessive functions of possessives in other Uralic languages.

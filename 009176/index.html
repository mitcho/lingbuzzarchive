Anaphors before intensifiers: The emergence of nafs-x in Hebrew and Arabic
Noa Bassel
July 2025
 

Diachronic studies of the English x-self anaphor observe that its source is a focused expression known as 'emphatic reflexive' or 'intensifier'. In Old English, intensifiers existed as independent self morphemes, which facilitated local coreference in otherwise ambiguous pronouns. This process has so far been considered universal and motivated a pragmatic framing of reflexive anaphors, where they emerge to reduce ambiguity in the discourse. This paper shows evidence from Hebrew and Arabic that a reversed path of change is also attested, where the coreferential use of anaphors anticipates the intensifier use. This path calls to reconsider the pragmatic motivation observed in English as a trigger for the emergence of anaphors cross-linguistically.

The Phonological Word: At the interface of lexical insertion, (morpho)syntax, and pronunciation
Peter Svenonius, Patrik Bye
March 2025
 

The phonological word serves as the domain for a variety of phenomena including stress assignment, minimality, phonotactic constraints, and phonological rules. The absence of word-level phenomena or conflicting criteria for the phonological word in certain languages has led some researchers to question its universal validity. An alternative perspective suggests that the phonological word may be understood within an interpretive view of phonology informed by lexical access in speech planning. At certain points in the syntactic derivation, lexical items are retrieved from long term storage and processed in the short-term memory Phonological Output Buffer (POB) preparatory to organisation at phrase-level. The phenomena characteristic of the phonological word, particularly foot construction and cyclicity, may reflect constraints on the POB, which could explain why stress assignment and cyclic phonological rules are bounded by the word domain. Since syntax controls the window for lexical retrieval, this analysis may account for why special clitics and compounds can behave as internal to the phonological word while simple clitics must be attached at a higher phrasal level. This perspective also relates to ongoing discussions concerning prosodic recursion and the existence of intermediate word-like prosodic categories.

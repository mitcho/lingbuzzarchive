Perfective allomorphy and negative neutralisation in Bambara: a nanosyntactic approach
Karen De Clercq
October 2025
 

Bambara (Mande) displays perfective (henceforth PFV) allomorphy conditioned by transitivity properties of the verbal predicate. The allomorphy is neutralised under negation (NEG). I present an analysis of these data in Nanosyntax (Starke 2009, Caha 2009), which relies on the size of lexically stored items (Starke 2014). The PFV allomorphy is accounted for by proposing that the lexical structure of the intransitive PFV marker "-ra" has its bottom feature in VOICE, while the transitive PFV "ye" has a lexical structure with a bottom feature that sits higher in the structure, i.e. in ASP. Negative neutralisation follows naturally from the proposal, if one adopts a low position in syntax for NEG, i.e. just above Voice. The paper also shows how the Nanosyntactic algorithm can help to diagnose the syntactic position of negation in a highly isolating language like Bambara.

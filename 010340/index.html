Patterns in Chaos: Composite A'/A Probes
Magdalena Lohninger
December 2024
 

This thesis presents a comparative study of composite A'/A constructions and develops a syntactic analysis for investigating such constructions cross-linguistically. In many languages, phrasal movement falls into two types with mutually exclusive properties: A-movement (e.g. passivization) and A'-movement (e.g. wh-movement, topicalization). Some languages, however, exhibit movement that combines properties associated with both types. In recent featural approaches, such derivations have commonly been analyzed as involving a composite [A'/A] probe, suggesting that phrasal movement can be triggered by a fused [A'/A] probe and thereby form an A'/A chain. The thesis systematically compares composite constructions in fifteen languages from nine language families, including Dinka Bor extraction, Mandarin Chinese BEI passives and TP-internal topics/foci, Khanty passives, promotion to pivot in Balinese and Malagasy, as well Hyperraising, and long-distance agreement in various languages. I ask whether A'/A constructions exhibit arbitrary combinations of A'- and A-properties or whether these properties follow predictable distributions. I show that only a small number of A'/A mixtures is attested cross-linguistically and that these follow a highly systematic pattern: some A'- and A-properties consistently cluster together, while others allow more variation. Based on these findings, I develop a unified syntax of composite [A'/A] probes that differs from existing accounts in proposing that there is no such thing as "composite A'/A movement". Although [A] and [A'] features may combine on a single probe and jointly search for a goal, the resulting movement chain is either an A-chain or an A'-chain, never a mixed chain. I therefore reconceptualize the traditional binary A'/A distinction as a finer-grained, tripartite system involving three interacting factors: i) the probing head ([A'], [A], or [A'/A]), ii) the movement chain (A or A'), and iii) the location of the probing head in the syntactic spine (on C or below). Each factor determines a distinct subset of A'- and A-properties. Their different combinations give rise to what I term a construction-specific A'/A signature, which predicts the particular combination of properties associated with a given construction. While natural language permits only a limited number of such signatures, this set is larger than the traditional binary A'/A distinction. I apply the A'/A signature analysis to established [A'/A] probe accounts in Dinka Bor, Mandarin Chinese, and Khanty, and extend it to Hyperraising, long-distance agreement, and promotion to pivot in Balinese and Malagasy. The analysis captures the composite nature of these constructions while maintaining a unified account of their properties. Beyond the analysis of A'/A signatures, the thesis addresses broader questions concerning the emergence of composite [A'/A] probes, the implications of a featural approach to phrasal movement, the nature of A'- and A-movement, and the role of comparative syntax.

Phonological profile changes in the Macro-Sudan Belt: Antagonism between ATR and interior vowels
Florian Lionnet, Nicholas Rolle
July 2026
 

Rolle et al. (2020) present a fine-grained areal distribution of two vocalic properties in the Macro-Sudan Belt (MSB – Güldemann 2008): advanced tongue root (ATR) harmony and interior vowels (i.e. non-peripheral vowels [ɨ ɯ ɜ ə ʌ … ]). Their study determined that these two properties are antagonistic at both the level of individual languages (languages in the MSB tend to have either one or the other, rarely both), as well as at the areal level (ATR-harmony languages and interior-vowel languages are largely in complementary geographic distribution). The focus of this paper is examining diachronic phonological profile changes which take place as language families spread into and out of these geographic zones. We focus on two meso-areas: an East African ATR zone (centered around South Sudan), and a Central African ATR-deficient Zone, where phonemic and allophonic interior vowels are disproportionately prevalent. Specifically, we present two case studies illustrating phonological profile changes in order to comply with local areal patterns, and demonstrate how the aforementioned zones act as strong areal magnets.

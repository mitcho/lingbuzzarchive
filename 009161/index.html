The Mass-Count Distinction, Davidsonian Events and Other Non-Nominal Domains
Friederike Moltmann
July 2025
 

Verbs in European languages do not come with a morphosyntactic mass-count distinction. Yet it has become customary to apply a semantic mass-count distinction to the verbal domain of events as well. This, however, is not unproblematic, since the verbal domain of events in fact behaves like a classifier system, rather than dividing into mass and count. This can be accounted for on an emerging view of the mass-count distinction according to which countability is not an integral part of lexical meaning, but is ‘added on’ or ‘made available’, whether syntactically, semantically or both. The paper discusses a range of data, partly from German, that support such a grammar-based view of countability for verbs with respect to the event argument position as well as for other non-nominal domains.

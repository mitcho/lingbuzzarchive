The Mass-Count Distinction, Davidsonian Events and Other Non-Nominal Domains
Friederike Moltmann
July 2025
 

There is an emerging view according to which countability is not an integral part of the lexical meaning of singular count nouns, but is ‘added on’ or ‘made available’, whether syntactically, semantically or both. This view has been pursued in order to deal with classifier languages such as Chinese as well as challenges to standard views of the mass-count distinction such as object mass nouns like furniture. I will discuss a range of data, partly from German, that such a grammar-based view of countability receives support when applied to verbs with respect to the event argument position as well as other non-nominal domains.

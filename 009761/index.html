On aspectual coercion in "before"/"after"-clauses: Evidence from processing
Johanna Alstott, Athulya Aravind
July 2026
 

Certain "before"-clauses and "after"-clauses are prima facie ambiguous between a strong reading and a weak reading, and theories diverge on how the readings obtain. Some theories take the LF of "before"- and "after"-clauses to be unambiguously weak, with the strong reading falling out as a subcase (as it entails the weak reading). Other theories, such as Rett (2020), take the LFs for the two readings to be distinct, critically, with the weak reading being the result of a coercion process. Under standard psycholinguistic assumptions, coercion incurs a processing cost, and hence, we argue, Rett’s (2020) theory makes processing predictions that are not shared by theories which posit unambiguously weak LFs. We test these predictions via four self-paced reading experiments and find moderate evidence that they are borne out. Our results constrain theories of "before" and "after," contribute to a typology of aspectual coercion mechanisms, and have implications for how we should think about the licensing conditions of coercion.

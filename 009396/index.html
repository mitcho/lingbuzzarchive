Allocutive agreement and indexicals Shift Together, but not always
Thomas McFadden, Sandhya Sundaresan
October 2025
 

This paper examines interactions between allocutive agreement and indexical shift, two phenomena that target contextual participants for morphological and semantic purposes in ways that crucially involve mediation by the syntax. This leads us to expect significant interactions between the two that should be instructive for our theoretical accounts of both. Based on a survey of the literature and novel data from Tamil, it is shown that this expectation is fulfilled. Prior work (Alok & Baker 2018, McFadden 2020) has identified a manifestion of the Shift Together constraint holding between shifted indexicals and shifted allocutive agreement in both Tamil and Magahi, supporting the idea that the latter is in fact a kind of indexical shift. However, a set of consistent exceptions to Shift Together are observed in Tamil and other languages when shifty agreement (including monstrous argument agreement and shifted allocutive agreement) co-occur with overt, unshiftable indexicals. The authors argue that the inclusion of allocutive agreement in this constellation casts doubt on Deal's (2020) proposal that apparent exceptions to Shift Together involve indexiphors rather than true indexical shift. This in turn leads to a challenge for context overwriting accounts of indexical shift and points instead toward a more traditional account like Schlenker (2003), where multiple contexts can be accessible simultaneously and individual indexicals can differ with respect to shiftability. This is supplemented with proposals from Sundaresan (2018, to appear) to model shiftability via distinct pronominal structures and ensure that Shift Together does in fact hold with respect to shiftable indexicals even without context overwriting.

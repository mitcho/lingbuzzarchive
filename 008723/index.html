Anatomy of the Russian Agreement Mishmash
Louis-H Desouvrey
December 2024
 

In this paper, I propose an analysis of agreement in the context of the genitive of quantification in Russian. Building on a theoretical framework where features take center stage, I argue that all Russian numerals but odin ‘one’ are inherently vectors (ω-specified), which causes them to be incompatible with accusative and nominative morphology. As a result, numerals are not declined in direct case, which leaves them without a thematic node. Therefore, their complement can only be in the genitive, which is devoid of a thematic feature. In addition, I suggest that the grammar highlights the defectiveness of those numerals by raising a warning: undeclined lower numerals (two to four) are enhanced with the feature singular [Sg], which forces their normal underspecified plural [ø] to recess, i.e. to be deactivated. Thus the singular is mandatory in the complement of such numerals, the recessive plural showing up only when the NP is modified by an adjective. It is shown that the agreement process follows from the nature of the representation: when the AGR features within a numeral phrase pair up, the verb must take its default morphology, whereas plural agreement with the numeral is forced when the genitive noun is attached to the verb. Further agreement complications allow us to modify the theory of coreference proposed in Desouvrey (2003). It is hypothesized that the referential node, or R-node, of an NP or a pronoun is the image of their thematic node. Therefore, if such elements do not have a thematic node, they may not have an R-node, a deficiency which hinders their ability to be the antecedent of an anaphor or a pronoun.

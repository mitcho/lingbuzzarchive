Heuristic and Post Festum Analogies as linguistic Sites of Epistemic negotiation in a Multilingual classroom
Atrayee Mukherjee
February 2026
 

This case study investigates how analogical reasoning in a multilingual Indian classroom reflects broader cultural, linguistic and epistemic dynamics. It focuses on the discrepancy between heuristic analogies generated by students and post festum analogies introduced by instructors. It employs the framework of cognitive grammar to examine how linguistic structures at different levels of organization facilitate or hinder the development of alternative conceptions during instruction. A qualitative research design was adopted which drew audio recorded classroom discourse and post-instructional interviews to capture the learners post hoc reasoning. Narrow transcription and discourse analysis reveal how reduced proficiency, language ideologies and socio cultural positioning shape the access of the learner to canonical knowledge. The findings suggest that heuristic analogies emerge through a linguistically mediated practice of meaning making where any artefact of language, as a structured inventory of symbols is situated within a broader socio-cultural domain. It furthermore, investigates how the organization of instructional discourse shapes the analogies constructed by the students. By situating analogy as a site of epistemic negotiation, the study contributes to ongoing debates about equity identity and participation in science education highlighting the need to recognize analogical reasoning as a practice that both enables and constraints epistemic access.

Uncountable plurals as maximal clusters
Camil Staps, Johan Rooryck
March 2026
 

English uncountable plurals are plurals whose parts cannot be counted directly, such as (*five) oats/grasslands/news/measles/tweezers. These form a heterogeneous group in terms of semantic field and various morphosyntactic tests. Nevertheless, this paper shows that a unified analysis can account for the fact that their parts cannot be counted directly. In particular, it argues that uncountable plurals denote maximal clusters in a mereotopological sense. The maximality of these clusters causes a conceptualisation as wholes, which prevents access to the parts. We provide a descriptive typology of English uncountable plurals to illustrate their analysis as clusters connected through either overlap (Strong Connectedness), adjacency (External Connectedness), or Common Linkage, a connectedness relation first introduced here.

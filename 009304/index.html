Diagnosing multidominance by focus association of ‘only’
Ka-Fai Yip, Squid Tamar-Mattis
September 2025
 

This study is concerned with the nature of syntactic silence and how to diagnose it. Syntactic structures may be "silent" in the course of derivation through several pathways: (i) movement (followed by Copy Deletion), (ii) ellipsis, and, less often discussed, (iii) linearization of shared/multidominant nodes. This study offers a novel diagnostic for (iii), namely, focus association of the exclusive particle 'only', which requires c-commanding an overt focus associate. Drawing cross-linguistic data from English and Cantonese (as well as Vietnamese), we demonstrate that focus association with apparent gaps is possible in Right Node Raising. We argue that only Parallel Merge (Wilder 1999, Citko 2005, Gračanin-Yuksek 2007) generates the correct structural relations for the association, whereas movement and ellipsis fail to do so. We further propose that Right Node Raising is structurally ambiguous between multidominance and ellipsis: the former feeds focus association while the latter bleeds it, supporting a non-uniform/"eclectic" approach (Barros & Vicente 2011, Belk et al. 2023). Finally, we show that the diagnostic extends to other constructions such as syntactic amalgams and right dislocation.

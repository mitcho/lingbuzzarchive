When your associates tag along: Associative plurality in Rural Iberian Spanish proper names
Elango Kumaran, Luis Miguel Toquero-Pérez
September 2025
 

Lewis (2023, 2024) proposes that languages that use the same marking to indicate additive and associative plural must lack free-standing definite articles (e.g. Turkish, Japanese). This property is attributed to these languages instantiating the negative value of the DP parameter. Based on original fieldwork data from Rural Iberian Spanish, we show that this variety of Spanish is a genuine counterexample to Lewis's generalization: plural-marked first names (e.g. lo-s Roman-es) may be understood additively or associatively; crucially, a plural-marked definite determiner is obligatory for the associative understanding. Based on a series of diagnostics, we argue that plural-marked names understood associatively are referential expressions, much like bare unmarked proper names, and as such they require a proprial definite article. From our findings, we can conclude that the presence or absence of dedicated associative morphology is not reducible to different values of the DP parameter. Instead, the presence or absence of dedicated associative morphology will depend on the interplay of two factors: (A) the syntactic representation available to generate plural referential expressions; and (B) the rules of exponence for the relevant terminal nodes in the language.

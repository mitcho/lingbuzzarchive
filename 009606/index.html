A substance-free autosegmental account of the coronal mutation in English
Slawomir Zdziebko
January 2026
 

This paper puts forward a restrictive approach to subsegmental architecture, which assumes that sub-skeletal representations are composed of obligatorily binary branching nodes and substance-free emergent features which serve as pointers to phonetic interpretation. We put the theory to a test by applying its principles to the set of alternations of coronal consonants attested in English before affixes -ion, -ian and -ious in words such as infectious, suggestion, revision, expansion etc. It is demonstrated that the requirement for the structure to be binary branching as well as the requirement for English consonants to contain at most three features derive the correct outputs of the coronal mutation. It is also argued that the postulatedapproach accounts for the behaviour of the items which resist the mutation such as rebellion, Arthurian, oblivious through the last resort operation of node epenthesis.

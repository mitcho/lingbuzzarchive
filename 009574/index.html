Defective circumvention: Multiple Agree as Minimal Search
John David Storment
December 2025
 

In this paper, I show that complex agreement interaction effects involving optional agreement, weak PCC effects with multiple subjects, and obligatory participant probing can all be accounted for under a system of conditional, sequential Multiple Agree as Minimal Search known as defective circumvention. Building on observations from Storment (2025b), I motivate the possibility of optional agreement with certain postverbal subjects within a single grammatical system in a variety of languages including English and Spanish. Within the same system, I explain how featural specifications of the relevant probes and goals involved in these Multiple Agree configurations can constrain the aforementioned optionality, thus leading to impossible or obligatory agreement scenarios. Regarding the impossible agreement scenarios, I show that Agree as Minimal Search is all that is necessary to account for a family of weak PCC effects in multiple subject constructions in colloquial English. This work contextualizes agreement optionality, multiple agreement, and PCC effects within a single framework based on a Minimalist formulation of Agree.

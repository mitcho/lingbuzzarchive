Exploring Gesture and Prosodic Alignment at Code-Switching Boundaries: A Multimodal Analysis of Twi-English Speech
Mark Anthony Aryee
March 2026
 

This study investigates gesture-prosody coordination in naturalistic Twi-English code-switching speech, focusing on gesture type distribution, temporal alignment between gesture strokes and prosodic prominence, and gestural activity at code-switching boundaries. A corpus of 11 video clips totalling 65.1 minutes was annotated using ELAN 7.0, yielding 837 gesture strokes classified according to McNeill's typology. Fundamental frequency was extracted using Praat's autocorrelation algorithm, and code-switching points were identified through a computational language inference pipeline built on FFmpeg speech detection. Results show that beat gestures dominated the corpus at 68.0%, consistent with the prosodic-structuring demands of the speech genres studied. Gesture stroke onsets clustered near local pitch peaks, with a mean signed time difference of -36.8ms and a median of -14.0ms. 51.6% of strokes fell within 50ms of a pitch peak and 70.8% within 100ms, with broadly similar patterns across gesture types. Gesture density was 2.30 times higher within ±500ms of code-switching points than in surrounding speech, a difference confirmed as statistically significant at t(10) = 6.51, p < .001. These findings demonstrate that code-switching in Twi-English bilingual discourse is a multimodal event in which gesture and prosody jointly mark language transitions. The study contributes an empirical account of bilingual multimodal communication in an understudied African language contact setting and presents a fully reproducible computational analysis pipeline for multimodal bilingual corpus research.

No argument for fixed base positions: A reply to Heck & Himmelreich (2017)
Tibor Kiss, Alicia Börner, Simon Masloch
June 2025
 

In this paper, we discuss Heck & Himmelreich’s (2017) analysis of morphological invariant floating quantifiers with a special focus on the question whether their analysis provides an argument in favor of fixed base positions of arguments in German clause structure. We will discuss several shortcomings of Heck & Himmelreich’s analysis and will show that their conceptual argument against a base generation approach is based on assumptions that cannot be maintained and that their empirical argument is flawed since it does not take the full range of relevant constructions, and the role of animacy, into account. In addition, the present analysis requires less means than Heck & Himmelreich’s analysis and can account for data which are very similar to the data discussed in Heck & Himmelreich (2017) but cannot be analyzed in terms of intervention, because there is none. We will thus conclude that Heck & Himmelreich (2017) do not provide an argument for fixed base positions, which is relevant insofar as Salzmann (2025) has recently pointed out that Heck & Himmelreich’s argument in favour of fixed base positions stands out against other inconclusive arguments.

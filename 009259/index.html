Humanness and word order: Evidence from Czech left branch extraction
Daniela Kořánová, Radek Simik
August 2025
 

We deliver an experimental argument from Czech, a Slavic language, that humanness-related word order manipulations are of narrowly syntactic origin. In particular, we show that while left branch extraction from objects across subjects is generally allowed, it becomes unavailable when the object denotes a human and the subject a nonhuman. We argue that this follows from an independently proposed account of subject-object asymmetry in subextraction (Ko 2007; 2014), conjoined with the assumptions that (i) human objects must scramble across nonhuman subjects (unless a stronger factor overrides this requirement) and (ii) that the scrambling is movement-based rather than base-generated. At a more general level, our experiments provide novel empirical support for specific syntactic theories (phase-based syntax, cyclic linearization) and contribute to our understanding of the relation between semantic features such as humanness or animacy and core syntactic processes.

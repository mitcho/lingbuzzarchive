Supported Two-Phase Reading in Second Language Acquisition: A Context-Based Approach to Promoting Receptive Competence
Eugene Suchanek
February 2026
 

[MAJOR UPDATE: December 2025] This entry now presents the fully formalized design framework “From Assisted Input to Enforced Autonomy”. Abstract Digital environments for second language (L2) reading increasingly prioritize immediate comprehension through in-flow lexical support such as glosses, pop-up dictionaries, and on-demand translation. While such tools effectively reduce processing difficulty and sustain reading flow, they also render assistance continuously available, making it methodologically difficult to distinguish autonomous processing from efficient support use. Although learner autonomy is frequently invoked as a long-term goal of assisted reading, the transition from supported to unsupported processing is rarely specified, enforced, or isolated as a design variable. This article introduces Supported Two-Phase Reading (STR) as a design framework that formalizes the withdrawal of support as an explicit, mandatory transition within the reading process. STR specifies a fixed sequence in which learners first read a text with full in-flow support and then immediately reread the identical text with all support categorically removed. By holding text, content, and prior resolution constant, the framework renders the withdrawal event analytically observable, rather than treating supported and unsupported reading as alternative instructional conditions. The contribution of this article is conceptual and methodological rather than empirical. STR reframes autonomy not as a learner choice or an assumed outcome, but as a design-imposed processing condition instantiated at the point of support withdrawal. The framework introduces protocol-bound, transition-based descriptors intended to describe learners’ adjustment under this constraint, while explicitly limiting their interpretive scope. No empirical data are reported, and no claims are made regarding learning outcomes or instructional effectiveness. The article offers a formal design specification intended to support future empirical work examining enforced support withdrawal as a design-defined variable in CALL research. Call for Research Collaboration The STR framework is proposed as a formally specified design protocol rather than as a validated instructional intervention. While its architectural sequence and core constraints are fully defined, questions concerning measurement, temporal spacing, and theoretical interpretation are intentionally left open for empirical instantiation. The framework may be of interest for MA/PhD projects or focused pilot studies concerned with the observability and interpretability of support withdrawal within L2 reading, rather than with outcome comparison or pedagogical efficacy. Researchers with expertise in L2 reading, glossing, retrieval-related processing, or CALL methodology who are interested in collaboratively instantiating and examining the STR protocol are invited to contact the author.

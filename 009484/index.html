Supported Two-Phase Reading in Second Language Acquisition: A Context-Based Approach to Promoting Receptive Competence
Eugene Suchanek
November 2025
 

This paper introduces the Supported Two-Phase Reading (STR) protocol — a standardized, research-oriented two-phase approach to second language acquisition that combines immediate comprehension support with obligatory meaning retrieval. In Phase 1, learners read a text with interlinearly integrated L1 translations and short glosses, which make the input comprehensible and minimize extraneous cognitive load while maintaining reading flow. In Phase 2, the same passage is reread without any aids; meanings must now be actively reconstructed from memory (retrieval practice). This binary fading design — 100% support in Phase 1 → 0% in Phase 2 — makes STR measurable and distinguishes it from conventional glossed, parallel, or interlinear formats where assistance often remains constant. Theoretically, STR integrates key SLA concepts: comprehensible input (Krashen), cognitive load reduction (Sweller, Paas), scaffolding and the zone of proximal development (Vygotsky; Wood, Bruner & Ross), and retrieval practice as a driver of long-term retention. Its clear two-phase structure makes STR particularly suitable for classroom, self-directed, and blended-learning contexts, and for automated generation through AI-assisted text adaptation. Research collaboration invitation: Empirical validation of the STR protocol is still pending.

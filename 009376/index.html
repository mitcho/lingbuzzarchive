No Genuine Copula in Japanese: a reanalysis of 'da' as a conceptual-locative postposition plus a one-argument existential verb
Shinya Makino
December 2025
 

I apologise for the very frequent revisions. The theoretical development now appears to have reached what is effectively its nearly final stage. -------------------- This theoretical paper proposes an alternative analysis of the Japanese so-called copula 'da' and its non-contracted form 'dearu'. While 'da/dearu' has traditionally been treated as a copula equivalent to English 'to be', we argue instead that it should be reanalysed as the complex consisting of the predicative and conceptual-locative postposition '=ni' and the adjunctive conjunction '-te' — surfacing as '=de' — combined with the one-argument existential verb 'aru'. Under this analysis, 'A=wa B=de aru' "A is B" is semantically both an existence-asserting and a existence-domain-specifying sentence. Syntactically, it consists of the ONE-ARGUMENT obligatory construction [A=wa aru] "A exists" and the ADJUNCT [B=de] "and (A's existence is located) in the conceptual domain of B" or, more practically, "and (it is) as B". Schematically, this can be represented as the conjunctive proposition EXIST(A) ⋀ IN(rEXIST(A), B), distinct from the atomic BE(A, B) encoded by 'to be'. Although 'B=de' is syntactically adjunctive, it is nevertheless non-deletable due to its pragmatic and informational indispensability: it contains the predicate-like postposition =ni and serves as the informational focus, whereas the syntactically obligatory 'kare=wa aru' provides the presuppositional background. This reanalysis appears to make possible a unified account of the structure, semantics and diachronic development of Japanese da/dearu constructions. -------------------- Retitled to: No Genuine Copula in Japanese: a reanalysis of 'da' as a predicative-postposition–adjunctive-conjunction complex plus a one-argument existential verb.

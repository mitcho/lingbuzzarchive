No Genuine Copula in Japanese: a reanalysis of 'da' as a conceptual-locative postposition plus a one-argument existential verb
Shinya Makino
December 2025
 

Retitled to "No Genuine Copula in Japanese: a reanalysis of 'da' as a conceptual-locative postposition-conjunction complex plus a one-argument existential verb" -------------------- This paper proposes an alternative analysis of the Japanese so-called copula 'da' and its non-contracted form 'dearu'. Traditionally, 'da/dearu' has been regarded as a copula equivalent to English 'to be'. Instead, we propose that da/dearu should be reanalysed as the complex consisting of the conceptual-locative-postposition '=ni' and the conjunction '-te' — surfacing as '=de' — combined with the one-argument existential verb 'aru'. Under this analysis, 'A=wa B=de aru' "A is B" is syntactically both an existence-asserting and a domain-specifying sentence consisting of the ONE-ARGUMENT core construction [A=wa aru] "A exists" and the ADJUNCT [B=de] "(and A's existence is located) in the conceptual domain of B" or, more simply, "(and it is) as B". Schematically, this can be represented as the conjunctive proposition EXIST(A) ⋀ IN(rEXIST(A), B) — distinct from the atomic BE(A, B) encoded by 'to be'. The syntactically adjunctive 'B=de' is nevertheless non-deletable due to its pragmatic and informational indispensability, as it functions as the focus of the sentence, whereas the syntactically obligatory 'kare=wa aru' represents the presuppositional background. Based on this analysis, it becomes possible to offer a unified account of the structure, semantics and historical development of Japanese 'da/dearu' constructions.

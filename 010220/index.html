The Morphosyntactic Pivot Hypothesis: Hierarchical Parametric Organization of the Locus of the EPP
Nazir Ahmed Malik
August 2026
 

Generative syntax standardly assumes that T universally hosts the EPP (Chomsky 1995, 2000), yet cross-linguistic evidence reveals substantial variation in which functional head exhibits syntactic centrality and morphological prominence. This paper develops the Morphosyntactic Pivot Hypothesis (MPH), proposing that the EPP bundle is hosted not universally by T but by the morphosyntactic pivot: the functional head independently identified through convergent syntactic diagnostics (coordination, complementizer selection, ellipsis, subject position, extraction, freezing) and morphological criteria (obligatoriness, paradigmatic richness, productivity, generality). Applying this Biphasic Identification Protocol to six typologically diverse core languages — five via original fieldwork (English, Italian, Swahili, Hindi-Urdu, Balti) and one from published description (Warlpiri) — uncovers hierarchical parametric organization in pivot identity: T (English), Mood (Italian), Agr (Swahili), Asp (Hindi-Urdu), Asp/Evid (Balti), and an auxiliary-complex Agr (Warlpiri). This variation is organized by two logically dependent parameters bearing an ontological/computational asymmetry: φ-Availability, whether the pivot bears φ-features at all, and, only where φ is available, φ-Interpretability, whether those features are semantically active at LF — existence necessarily precedes computation over what exists, reducing four combinatorial possibilities to three attested types. The pivots possessing iφ-features license pro-drop through matching-based Bimodal Agree whereas the pivots possessing uφ-features triggers valuation-based Unimodal Agree, blocking pro-drop and requiring expletives; in contrast, pivots with nφ-features satisfy the EPP through aspectual or evidential anchoring, independent of φ-dependencies entirely. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on an 82-language sample from 21 distinct families validate this architecture. PCA reveals an exceptionally dominant first component (PC1 = 96.60% of variance; jackknife SD = 0.176%) and discrete typological clustering rather than continuous gradation. The hand-coded classification is independently corroborated against Universal Dependencies treebank data which has been built over two decades by independent annotators without reference to MPH. The UD test recovers φ-availability at 97.5% and φ-interpretability at 95.0% accuracy. Every residual misclassification falls into one of two pre-specified failure modes — a prediction which was genuinely at risk of being falsified but was not. The framework accounts for multiple morphosyntactic phenomena from a single parametric axis. Case systems, agreement, subject licensing, expletive distribution, and pro-drop all fall follow from the hierarchical organization of the locus of the EPP. It derives typological macro-variation from φ-state of the pivot specification. It provides testable predictions for acquisition and diachronic change, including agreement morphology that outlasts the interpretability which once licensed it.

The morphology/syntax interface
Laura Kalin, Bronwyn Bjorkman, Lauren Clemens, Claire Halpert, Ruth Kramer, Sarah Murray, Aida Talic
April 2026
 

In this Element, we ask, what are today's prominent approaches to the study of the relationship between morphology and syntax? What are the linguistic phenomena that are most relevant at the morphology/syntax interface, and how are these handled in different types of theories? We make a core division between lexicalist approaches, which posit an interface between morphology and syntax at the level of the word, and non-lexicalist approaches, encompassing all other sorts of interfaces. Through the lens of different types of theories, we then examine the status of words and word-formation as well as six types of form-meaning mappings: allomorphy, syncretism, portmanteaux, multiple exponence, empty morphs, and morphological gaps. We aim to give readers both an aerial view of the theoretical landscape as well as the concrete tools needed to engage with this active and exciting research area.

Question under Discussion and question bias: Biased A-not-A questions in Mandarin Chinese
Shumian Ye
June 2026
 

This chapter develops a pragmatic analysis of question bias through a case study of Mandarin shi-bu-shi questions. Building on Ye’s (2021a) QUD-based analysis, it argues that the bias associated with shi-bu-shi questions is QUD-dependent, arising from how possible answers contribute to resolving the current Question under Discussion, rather than being directly encoded as a fixed component of the lexical meaning of shi. This view is supported by the observation that the bias in shi-bu-shi questions varies with the nature of the relevant QUD. This chapter further demonstrates that biased questions need not be monopolar. Evidence from responsive embedding and unconditionals shows that shi-bu-shi questions retain a bipolar denotation, despite their bias. More generally, shi-bu-shi questions show that question bias and question denotation should not be conflated, since a question may pragmatically favor one answer while still making both answers semantically available.

Frames: A Pattern-Based Method for Second Language Instruction
Joseph Budinger
June 2026
 

Second language learners often acquire substantial vocabulary and a working knowledge of grammatical rules without achieving fluent spoken production. This gap has motivated research on how patterned units shape language use. Construction grammar, the lexical approach, and formulaic-language research all emphasize the role of patterned units in fluent production, but they do not, taken together, provide a unified pedagogical method organized around such units. This paper develops the pedagogical application of Frames for second language instruction. Frames treats the frame family, a cluster of patterned expressions sharing a communicative function, as the primary unit of instruction and uses massed, varied exposure as its main mode of delivery. A frame is defined operationally as a scaffold with one or more variable slots; a slot is a position where the filler may vary without changing the frame's communicative function. Frame families are organized by functional role rather than grammatical category. The central prediction is that focused exposure to a frame family enables learners to produce fluent variants of that family with novel fillers drawn from vocabulary they already know. The broader theoretical status of frames as units of linguistic representation is not adjudicated here; the present article argues only that frame families can be operationalized as pedagogical units and that their instructional value should be tested through learner outcomes.

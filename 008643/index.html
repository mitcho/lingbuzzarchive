On LLMs, Generative Grammar, and How We Need Theory More than Ever
Gillian Ramchand
October 2024
 

This paper is a response article to a piece by Cristiano Chesi in a special issue of the Journal of Italian Linguistics, entitled ´Is it the end of Generative Grammar as we Know it?´ . There are two core issues that I address separately in this response article: (i) are there certain core beliefs of generative grammar that are fatally undermined by the recent successes of LLMs and the unsupervised learning that trains them? (ii) do LLMs then constitute a rival (and superior) `theory´ that can and should take over now from (all) previous theories in pushing the science forward? The answers are no, and no. But the argument will take us into a discussion of what it means to be a theory, and also into some soul searching about what the aims of generative linguistics are. In doing so, I end up arguing that the new advances in AI should be used as an aid and supplement to cognitive science, not as a replacement for it, and that any successful symbiosis crucially requires Theory more than ever.

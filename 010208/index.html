On selective LF wh-movement: What Superiority and wh-islands tell us about wh-in-situ in Japanese
Zeljko Boskovic
August 2026
 

The paper examines wh-in-situ in Japanese and argues for a position that is in between wholesale LF wh-movement approaches, where all in-situ wh-phrases in Japanese undergo movement in LF, and the treatments where there is no LF wh-movement in Japanese at all by arguing that some, but not all wh-in-situ questions in Japanese involve LF wh-movement. Regarding when Japanese has LF wh-movement, Takahashi (1993) argues that in some cases, wh-scrambling in Japanese involves true overt wh-movement. I show that a rather intricate paradigm regarding wh-island and superiority effects with Japanese wh-in-situ can be accounted for if Japanese has LF wh-movement only in the contexts where Takahashi (1993) claims Japanese has overt wh-movement. The paper also establishes a parallel with wh-questions in French, providing a uniform treatment of Japanese and French, where (LF) wh-movement, Agree, and unselective binding are all involved in question formation, with Agree being clause-bounded, and unselective binding, a semantic operation, being available only after the +wh-C establishes a syntactic relationship with a wh-phrase, through either Agree or wh-movement. The discussion in the paper, which establishes the existence of LF superiority effects, also has consequences for our understanding of superiority and wh-island effects.
